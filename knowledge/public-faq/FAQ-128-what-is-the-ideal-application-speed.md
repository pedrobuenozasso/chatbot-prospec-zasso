# What is the ideal application speed?

## Short Answer

There is no single ideal application speed for every Zasso use case. The ideal speed is the one that gives the electrodes enough contact time to deliver the required energy while still meeting the customer's productivity, safety, and crop-protection needs. Based on current Zasso product material and external field studies, practical operating speeds commonly fall in the low single-digit km/h range, but the right setting depends on the machine, applicator, weed stage, weed density, soil condition, crop system, and whether the protocol uses one slow pass or repeated moderate-speed passes.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding is a contact-time and delivered-energy process. If the applicator moves too quickly for the target weeds and field condition, the electrodes may not remain in effective contact long enough to deliver the dose needed for durable control. The result can be visible burn-down or wilting shortly after treatment, but with enough surviving plant tissue for regrowth.

Slower speeds generally increase contact time and energy delivered per plant or per treated area. This can be important for larger weeds, dense stands, grasses, perennial weeds, plants with substantial crowns or rhizomes, or situations where a single pass must achieve the highest possible control. However, slower operation also reduces field capacity and may not always be the most economical or operationally practical approach.

For that reason, Zasso should not present one universal speed as the ideal. A more accurate answer is that the ideal speed is selected from the product's approved operating range and adjusted to the weed and site. Zasso's product portfolio material lists working speeds such as 1.5-3 km/h for several urban products, 1.1-3 km/h for one vehicle-mounted urban configuration, and 1.5-4 km/h for the XPower XPS vineyard, orchard, nut, and olive application. Public Zasso material for the XPower XPA also presents a 1.0-5.0 km/h working-speed range. These are product ranges, not guarantees that every weed will be controlled equally at every speed.

External studies reinforce the same principle. Oregon State University Extension reported that slower speeds of about 0.5-1 km/h gave the strongest single-pass results in organic blueberry trials with Zasso equipment, while repeated moderate-speed passes at about 2-4 km/h could match or exceed one slow pass in some regrowth-prone situations. A Weed Science vineyard study using a Zasso XPower machine reported strong biomass reduction at about 1.1-1.4 km/h under the tested vineyard conditions. These results support speed selection by protocol, not by a universal number.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat application speed as a quality setting, not just a productivity number. Driving faster can increase hectares or kilometers treated per day, but only if contact quality and delivered energy remain adequate for the target weeds. A slower pass may be justified when weeds are mature, dense, grassy, perennial, wet, difficult to contact, or growing in conditions that reduce energy delivery.

For easier targets, such as small, upright, accessible weeds under favorable soil and contact conditions, a moderate speed within the approved range may be more practical. For difficult or regrowth-prone targets, a slower first pass, better applicator positioning, pre-mowing where appropriate, or sequential passes may produce a better agronomic and economic outcome.

The practical recommendation is to set speed during technical commissioning, operator training, or field validation for the specific crop, weed community, product, and safety context. Follow-up scouting should confirm whether the chosen speed is producing the expected control and whether a second pass is needed.

## Safe Sales Wording

"There is no one ideal speed for every field. We select speed from the product's approved operating range based on weed stage, density, soil condition, applicator setup, and the customer's control target."

"In general, slower travel gives more contact time and can improve control in difficult conditions. In some applications, repeated moderate-speed passes can be the better balance between efficacy and productivity."

"For a specific customer, Zasso validates the recommended speed as part of the full treatment protocol, including contact quality, safety, crop clearance, and follow-up scouting."

## Caveats

Do not promise a universal ideal speed, one-pass control, guaranteed root kill, or the same result across all crops, weeds, soils, machines, seasons, and operators. Speed interacts with power, voltage, current, electrode contact, weed height, weed stage, weed density, plant water content, soil moisture, soil conductivity, residue, slope, crop proximity, fire risk, and pass strategy.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, control logic, customer-specific trial data, pricing, unpublished performance data, telemetry, or engineering parameters. If a customer requests a numerical recommendation, provide only product-approved guidance or arrange technical validation for the specific application.
