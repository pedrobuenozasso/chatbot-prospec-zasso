# Why use Zasso instead of mowing?

## Short Answer

Customers use Zasso instead of mowing when they need more than a temporary cut of the top growth. In suitable applications, Zasso can send controlled electrical energy through the plant and into its recovery tissues, which can provide more durable suppression than mowing alone, especially where rapid regrowth is a recurring problem. The safest commercial position is not that Zasso replaces mowing everywhere, but that it can be a stronger tool where the goal is deeper plant injury rather than simple height reduction.

## Detailed Answer

Mowing is a useful and widely accepted weed-management practice. It is simple, familiar, and often effective for reducing height, improving access, and keeping vegetation visually under control. Its main limitation is that it usually cuts or bruises above-ground biomass without necessarily damaging the roots, crown, rhizomes, or other tissues that allow the plant to regrow. In many perennial, grassy, or vigorous mixed stands, that means mowing becomes a repeated suppression practice rather than a durable control event.

Zasso is chosen instead of mowing when the customer wants a different result. Electroherb is designed to transfer controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit. When the treatment reaches the plant's conductive tissues and recovery structures effectively, the result can be closer to a systemic physical injury than to a surface cut. That can make Zasso attractive where repeated mowing passes are labor-intensive, where regrowth pressure is high, where customers want to reduce dependence on repeated mechanical top-kill, or where a non-chemical control method is preferred.

The difference is especially important in weeds that recover quickly after cutting. A mower can make the site look clean immediately, but if crowns, stolons, rhizomes, or protected basal tissues remain healthy, the weed burden can return fast. Electrical treatment is often considered when the customer is trying to weaken or kill the plant more deeply, not just trim it back.

That said, mowing still has a role. In tall, dense, tangled, or grassy vegetation, mowing before electrical treatment can improve access and contact. In those situations the best answer may be mowing plus Zasso rather than mowing versus Zasso. The right comparison is therefore practical, not ideological: use mowing when height management is enough or when field preparation is needed; use Zasso when the customer wants a non-chemical treatment aimed at stronger suppression and potentially less regrowth, subject to species, growth stage, moisture, density, safety, and operating setup.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the question is what job needs to be done. If the goal is only to cut vegetation down for appearance, access, or short-term maintenance, mowing may remain the simpler option. If the goal is to reduce regrowth pressure, add a non-chemical mode of action, or avoid returning again and again for top-growth removal, Zasso may offer better value in suitable conditions.

This is especially relevant in perennial crops, non-crop areas, resistant-weed programs, and situations where repeated mowing is creating operational cost without solving the underlying weed problem. Zasso can also be useful where the customer wants to diversify beyond a maintenance cycle built only around mowing and herbicides.

Customers should still expect local validation. The practical fit depends on weed species, whether the target is broadleaf or grass, plant size, density, regrowth biology, moisture conditions, contact quality, speed, number of passes, residue level, and safety requirements. In some fields Zasso can replace a mowing pass; in others mowing is the right preparation step before the electrical pass.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Use Zasso instead of mowing when the customer needs more than a temporary cut of the canopy and wants a non-chemical treatment aimed at stronger suppression of regrowth."

"Mowing is still useful for preparation and vegetation management, but Zasso can add a deeper physical effect when the electrical treatment reaches the plant's recovery tissues effectively."

"In suitable applications, Zasso may replace a mowing pass or reduce the number of repeated mowing cycles, but the fit depends on species, density, regrowth biology, contact quality, and operating conditions."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is always better than mowing, always kills roots, or always reduces the number of passes. Some weeds, especially grasses or plants with protected recovery structures, may still require repeat treatment, mowing before treatment, or a broader integrated program.

Do not imply that mowing has no role. In dense, tall, tangled, or grassy vegetation, mowing can be an important preparation step that improves access and contact before the electrical pass.

Do not make universal economic claims. Whether Zasso is more efficient than mowing depends on weed pressure, number of repeat cuts otherwise needed, speed, operator training, energy or fuel use, safety setup, and the value of achieving a more durable result.
