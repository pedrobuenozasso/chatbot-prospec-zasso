# Does it leave chemical residues?

## Short Answer

No. Zasso's electrical weeding does not leave chemical herbicide residues because it does not apply a chemical active ingredient to the crop, soil, or surrounding environment. It uses controlled electrical energy as a physical mode of action, so there is no spray deposit, herbicide metabolite, or persistent chemical residue from the treatment itself. Customers should still check local crop, certification, and operating requirements for the intended use.

## Detailed Answer

When customers ask about chemical residues, they are usually concerned about herbicide active ingredients, metabolites, spray deposits, drift deposits, or residues that could remain in soil, water, crop material, harvested products, or nearby sensitive areas. Zasso's technology is different because it is not a chemical herbicide. The weed-control action comes from electrical energy delivered through contacted plants and the plant-soil circuit during operation.

Because no molecular herbicide is applied, Zasso does not create a chemical residue pathway from the treatment itself. There is no tank mix, spray droplet, active ingredient, chemical metabolite, rainfast period, spray drift deposit, or herbicide leaching pathway associated with the electrical treatment. This is one of the practical advantages of Zasso for customers who want to reduce chemical load, avoid spray drift, support residue-sensitive production, or diversify away from herbicide-only weed-control programs.

The claim should still be stated precisely. "No chemical residues" does not mean that nothing remains visible in the field. Treated weeds can remain as wilted, dried, or decomposing plant material. Normal field operations can also involve dust, soil contact, plant debris, fuel, lubricants, equipment wear, and site-management obligations. Those are operational considerations, not chemical herbicide residues from Zasso's mode of action.

For food, export, organic, or certification-sensitive situations, customers should avoid treating this FAQ as a blanket compliance statement. Zasso can be described as a non-chemical, residue-free weed-control method in the herbicide-residue sense, but local regulations, crop rules, certification standards, customer specifications, and product manuals still need to be followed.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical benefit is that Zasso can reduce or avoid chemical residue concerns in suitable weed-control applications. This can matter in organic and residue-sensitive production, public or urban areas, water-sensitive sites, permanent crops, integrated weed-management programs, and markets where customers want to lower reliance on chemical herbicides.

It may also simplify questions that are specific to chemical spraying, such as spray drift, rainfastness, chemical withholding periods, and herbicide residues in the environment or food chain. However, customers should still validate the full operating plan for the crop, site, product, season, local regulation, and certification framework.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso has zero environmental impact in every condition or that no field trace of any kind remains. The correct claim is narrower: Zasso does not leave chemical herbicide residues from the treatment because it does not apply chemical herbicides.

Do not promise regulatory approval, organic certification, export acceptance, food-residue compliance, or customer-specific residue compliance unless the relevant jurisdiction, crop, certification body, and customer standard have been checked.

Do not use this FAQ to answer unrelated environmental endpoints such as biodiversity, soil biology, water contamination, groundwater, aquatic life, crop injury, or long-term monitoring. Those topics require their own evidence and caveats.
