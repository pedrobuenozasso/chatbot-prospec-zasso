# Does Zasso avoid chemical residues?

## Short Answer

Yes, in the herbicide sense. Zasso's electrical weed-control treatment does not apply a chemical active ingredient, so the treatment itself does not create herbicide residues on the crop, soil, water, or surrounding area. The safe claim is narrower than "nothing remains at all": treated plant material, soil, dust, and other normal field traces can still be present, but not chemical herbicide residues from the Zasso treatment itself.

## Detailed Answer

Customers usually ask this question because they want to know whether a weed-control pass leaves behind herbicide active ingredients, metabolites, spray deposits, or other chemical residues that can affect food, export markets, organic systems, nearby vegetation, workers, water, or public perception. Zasso is different from a sprayed herbicide because its mode of action is physical rather than chemical. The system delivers controlled electrical energy through contacted plants and the plant-soil circuit instead of depositing a molecular herbicide.

Because no herbicide chemistry is applied, the Zasso treatment itself does not introduce a chemical-residue pathway from the weed-control pass. There is no tank mix, no sprayed active ingredient, no herbicide metabolite from the treatment, and no herbicide residue left behind by the electrical mode of action itself. That is why Zasso can be positioned as a non-chemical, residue-free alternative in the herbicide-residue sense.

That message still needs discipline. "Avoids chemical residues" does not mean that the field becomes chemically clean in every broader sense, that prior residues disappear, or that every regulatory and certification question is automatically solved. Customers may still need to consider other inputs used in the system, previous chemical applications, site contamination history, crop-specific standards, export requirements, organic certification rules, and local operating obligations.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical value is straightforward: where Zasso replaces a herbicide pass, it can remove the residue concern tied to applying that herbicide. This is particularly relevant in residue-sensitive production, organic-compatible programs, public and municipal spaces, permanent crops, areas near water, and situations where growers want to reduce dependence on chemical weed control.

It can also simplify adjacent discussions that normally come with sprayed herbicides, such as spray drift, rainfastness, re-entry intervals linked to chemical exposure, and residue questions from buyers or auditors. However, those downstream compliance questions should still be checked against the crop, country, certification scheme, customer specification, and local rules that apply to the use case.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso does not apply a chemical herbicide, so the treatment itself does not leave herbicide active-ingredient residues behind."

"It is a physical electrical mode of action rather than a spray chemistry, which helps customers avoid the residue pathway associated with the herbicide pass being replaced."

"For crop, certification, or export requirements, we still recommend checking the applicable local rules and buyer standards."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso removes all contaminants, erases residues from earlier chemical applications, or guarantees compliance in every crop and market. The defensible claim is narrower: the Zasso treatment itself does not add chemical herbicide residues.

Do not overextend this FAQ into broader environmental claims such as zero impact, universal safety, or automatic approval for organic, food, export, or municipal programs. Those questions depend on the full operating context and sometimes have their own separate regulatory rules.

Do not imply that every operation becomes residue-free if other chemical inputs are still being used elsewhere in the customer's program.
