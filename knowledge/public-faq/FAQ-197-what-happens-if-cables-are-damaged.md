# What happens if cables are damaged?

## Short Answer

If a Zasso cable is damaged, the machine should be stopped and treated as unsafe until the cable and the related electrical system have been inspected according to the approved product manual and training. Operators should not touch, move, tape, bypass, or continue using damaged high-voltage cables, connectors, or insulation while the machine may be energized. The equipment should return to service only after trained or authorized personnel confirm that the damaged part has been repaired or replaced with approved parts and the required checks are complete.

## Detailed Answer

Damaged cables matter because Zasso equipment uses controlled high-voltage electrical energy. A cable fault can change where current flows. Instead of remaining in the intended treatment path through the applicator, target vegetation, soil, and return route, current may leak through damaged insulation, wet residue, mud, a connector, the machine frame, a metal object, crop infrastructure, a service tool, an animal, or a person.

The practical response should be conservative. If an operator sees cut, crushed, abraded, burnt, pinched, cracked, loose, exposed, wet-contaminated, overheated, or otherwise abnormal cable insulation or connectors, the operator should stop high-voltage operation, keep people and animals away from the machine and application zone, and follow the product-specific shutdown, discharge, lockout or restart-prevention, inspection, and escalation procedure. The machine should not be restarted simply because the visible damage seems small or because no spark is seen.

Depending on the product and configuration, Zasso systems may include layers such as insulation monitoring, leakage or ground-fault detection, high-voltage inhibit logic, emergency stop, alarms, fault latching, event logging, protective grounding or bonding, and safe shutdown behavior. Those layers help manage defined abnormal conditions, but they do not make damaged cables acceptable for continued operation. A monitoring system is a protective layer, not permission to keep working with compromised insulation.

Repair decisions should follow the approved manual and service process. Some visible issues may require replacement of a cable, connector, protective sleeve, strain relief, electrode assembly, or related component. Deeper electrical inspection may be needed after arcing, crushing, water ingress, repeated alarms, abnormal shutdowns, suspected energized metal, or any shock, fire, smoke, or burning smell. Operators and sales teams should avoid giving field repair instructions unless those instructions are explicitly approved for the specific product.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, damaged cables are a stop-work safety condition. The correct operating culture is: inspect before use, stop if damage is found, keep the area controlled, do not improvise repairs, and restart only after the approved checks are complete.

This protects people and animals first, but it also protects the equipment and treatment quality. A damaged cable can create shock, fire, arcing, fault, downtime, diagnostic, or poor-efficacy risks. Continuing to operate can turn a manageable service issue into a safety incident or a larger repair.

Fleet owners, contractors, municipalities, farms, and distributors should make cable inspection part of pre-use checks and maintenance routines. Teams should know what visible damage looks like, who is allowed to inspect or replace parts, what alarms require escalation, and when the machine must stay out of service.

## Safe Sales Wording

"If a cable is damaged, the operator should stop the machine, keep people and animals away, and follow the approved Zasso inspection and service procedure before restarting."

"Damaged insulation or connectors are safety-relevant because they can create unintended current paths. They should not be taped, bypassed, or worked around in the field unless the approved manual specifically allows a defined action."

"Zasso equipment uses layered protection and fault detection where applicable, but damaged cables still require trained inspection and approved repair or replacement before return to service."

## Caveats

Do not treat this FAQ as a product manual or a repair procedure. Exact shutdown steps, discharge checks, lockout requirements, PPE, inspection methods, replacement criteria, cable types, connector ratings, alarm codes, and restart rules may vary by product, configuration, country, and site.

Do not claim that every cable fault is automatically detected, that a damaged cable is safe if no alarm appears, that electrical tape is an approved repair, that an operator can splice or bypass high-voltage cables in the field, or that pressing emergency stop alone proves the cable is safe to touch.

Do not disclose confidential cable specifications, insulation ratings, grounding architecture, fault thresholds, response times, diagnostic logic, service tests, supplier details, incident history, or approved repair procedures unless Zasso has cleared them for external use.
