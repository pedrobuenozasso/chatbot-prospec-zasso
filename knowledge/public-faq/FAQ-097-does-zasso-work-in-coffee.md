# Does Zasso work in coffee?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work in coffee when the application is designed for the crop layout, weed spectrum, soil conditions, and operator safety requirements. Zasso has publicly launched E.Coffee as a dedicated electric-weeding solution for coffee growers, especially for Latin American coffee systems and other narrow-row perennial crops. The strongest claim is that Zasso can be a relevant non-chemical tool for directed weed control in suitable coffee plantations; it should not be presented as a universal, crop-contact-safe, one-pass, or all-conditions replacement for every herbicide or mechanical operation.

## Detailed Answer

Coffee is a strong fit for the type of problem Zasso is designed to address: repeated weed pressure in a high-value perennial crop, often with pressure to reduce herbicide use, protect soil cover, manage labor, and work in constrained row geometry. Coffee plantations commonly require integrated weed management, combining cultural, mechanical, manual, cover-crop, mulch, and chemical options depending on climate, terrain, crop age, weed species, and production system.

Zasso's Electroherb technology controls contacted vegetation through electrical energy. The system is physical and non-chemical: it targets weeds by passing current through the contacted plant tissue and into the plant-soil circuit. This makes it useful where growers want to reduce chemical herbicide use, avoid spray drift, support organic or regenerative positioning, or manage weeds that are difficult or costly to control with manual or mechanical methods.

For coffee, the practical fit depends on whether the applicator can contact the weeds while avoiding unacceptable contact with the coffee plants, trunks, irrigation components, stakes, wires, and other infrastructure. Coffee rows, slopes, ground cover strategy, weed height, plant moisture, soil moisture, residue, and operating speed all matter. In suitable layouts, Zasso can be discussed for directed inter-row or under-row weed management, organic coffee systems, residue-sensitive programs, and farms looking for a non-chemical complement to their current weed-control program.

Zasso's public E.Coffee launch material is important because it shows that coffee is not only a theoretical use case. Zasso announced E.Coffee in Brazil in June 2024 as a device developed for coffee growers, presented at Expocafe 2024 in Minas Gerais, and positioned for coffee and similar narrow-row crops. Current public product information states that E.Coffee is commercially available in Latin America. Those facts support a positive answer, while still requiring site-specific validation before making performance, cost, or crop-safety promises.

## What This Means for Customers

For coffee growers, Zasso should be positioned as a targeted tool inside an integrated weed-management program. It can help reduce reliance on chemical herbicides in suitable applications, support organic or low-residue strategies, and address weeds in areas where mechanical tools, mowing, or hand labor are expensive, inconsistent, or operationally difficult.

A good customer evaluation should start with the coffee system: row spacing, alley width, slope, crop age, canopy structure, weed species, weed height, soil and plant moisture, irrigation equipment, residue or mulch, fire risk, safety distance, tractor access, labor cost, and current herbicide or mechanical-control program. Performance should be measured through weed suppression, regrowth, crop safety, operating speed, number of passes, labor need, fuel or energy use, cost per hectare, and compatibility with the farm's soil-cover strategy.

Customers should expect the best results where weeds are contacted consistently, the crop and infrastructure are protected, the application speed is matched to weed size and density, and operators are trained. Zasso can be especially attractive in higher-value, sustainability-driven, organic, or herbicide-constrained coffee systems, but it should normally be evaluated through a structured local pilot before broad deployment.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can be used in coffee where the application is designed around the plantation layout, target weeds, crop protection, and safety protocol."

"Zasso's E.Coffee solution was developed specifically for coffee and similar narrow-row perennial crops, and it offers a non-chemical tool that can complement integrated weed management in suitable farms."

"For coffee, we recommend a local pilot that measures weed control, regrowth, crop safety, operating speed, and cost under the customer's real field conditions."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is selective to coffee plants. Electrical weeding is a physical, contact-based method; if coffee tissue or sensitive infrastructure is contacted, injury or damage may be possible unless the specific setup and protocol have been validated.

Do not claim universal coffee efficacy, permanent control, one-pass control, full herbicide replacement, guaranteed cost savings, or suitability in all slopes, row spacings, soils, irrigation systems, weed stages, weather conditions, or fire-risk conditions. Performance depends on applicator contact, row geometry, crop age, weed species, weed stage, plant and soil moisture, soil conductivity, biomass density, residue, speed, operator training, safety procedures, and local regulations.

Avoid repeating broad marketing claims such as "zero environmental impact" without qualification. Use careful wording such as "non-chemical," "designed to reduce herbicide reliance," "no chemical herbicide residue from the treatment," and "supports integrated weed management where validated."
