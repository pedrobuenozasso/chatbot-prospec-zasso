# Does Zasso work in organic crops?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work in organic crops when the weeds are emerged, reachable by the electrodes, and the application can be managed without unacceptable crop contact or safety risk. Because Zasso is a non-chemical physical weed-control technology, it can fit naturally into organic and transition-to-organic weed-management programs. The right answer still depends on the crop, weed species, soil and plant moisture, crop stage, machine configuration, local organic rules, and certifier acceptance.

## Detailed Answer

Organic crops often need weed-control options that do not rely on conventional synthetic herbicides. Zasso's electrical weeding technology addresses emerged weeds by applying electrical energy through contacted plant tissue, rather than by spraying a chemical herbicide. That makes it especially relevant for organic systems where growers already combine prevention, crop rotation, mulching, mowing, cultivation, hand weeding, flame, steam, grazing, and other approved physical or cultural methods.

The strongest organic-crop fit is usually in higher-value systems where weeds are accessible and where avoiding herbicide inputs, residues, hand labor, repeated cultivation, or soil disturbance has clear value. Examples may include organic berries, orchards, vineyards, vegetables, nuts, plantation crops, row-crop escapes, field borders, and transition-to-organic fields, provided the crop and weed geometry allow safe electrode contact with the target weeds.

Zasso should not be presented as automatically suitable for every organic crop. Electrical weeding is a contact-based physical treatment. It works best when target weeds are visible and reachable, the crop can be protected from direct contact, the soil and plant conditions support energy transfer, and the operator can follow the required safety and fire-risk procedures. It also has no residual soil activity, so it does not prevent later weed emergence by itself.

For certified organic production, there is a second question beyond field performance: compliance. In the United States, organic rules explicitly include physical and mechanical weed-control methods and list electrical means among allowed ways weed problems may be controlled. In the European Union, organic production is based on reducing external inputs and controlling which plant-protection substances may be used. Even so, growers should confirm the specific Zasso use with their certifier or organic advisor and document it in the farm's organic system plan where required.

## What This Means for Customers

For organic growers, Zasso can add a non-chemical tool to the weed-management toolbox. It may be useful where hand weeding is expensive, repeated cultivation risks crop roots or soil structure, flame or steam are slow or fuel-intensive, organic-approved contact products are limited, or weed escapes threaten crop quality and future seed pressure.

The practical starting point should be a crop-specific pilot. A customer should define the target crop, weed species, weed size, treatment zone, crop shielding, soil moisture, application speed, pass number, safety distance, fire-risk conditions, and success criteria before scaling. Good outcomes are more likely when Zasso is integrated with crop rotation, cover crops, mulches, mowing, cultivation, scouting, and follow-up passes rather than used as a stand-alone cure for all weed pressure.

## Evidence and Context

USDA AMS organic guidance describes mechanical and physical practices as part of the organic crop pest, weed, and disease management hierarchy: https://www.ams.usda.gov/grades-standards/crop-pest-weed-and-disease-management-practice-standard

European Commission organic-production guidance states that organic production aims to reduce external inputs and that plant-protection substances used in organic agriculture must be pre-approved: https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-production-and-products_en

Oregon State University Extension published 2026 guidance from certified-organic blueberry field studies using Zasso equipment. ## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is automatically approved for every organic standard, country, crop, buyer program, or farm. Organic compliance depends on the local rules, certifier interpretation, machine use, cleaning practices, records, and the farm's organic system plan.

Do not claim universal crop selectivity. If the crop is contacted in the treatment zone, crop injury may occur. Suitability depends on crop architecture, plant spacing, row geometry, weed height, electrode configuration, shielding, operator training, and safety procedures.

Do not imply residual weed prevention. Zasso treats emerged vegetation; new weeds can germinate after treatment, so follow-up scouting and integrated weed management remain important.

Do not overstate performance across all organic crops. The strongest public organic-crop evidence surfaced in this run is from organic blueberry and broader electrical-weeding literature, not from a complete proof package covering every organic crop category.
