# Does Zasso work on mature plants?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on mature plants when the plant is accessible to the electrodes and the treatment program is adjusted to the plant's size, structure, and regrowth potential. Mature plants are usually more difficult than young weeds because they often have thicker tissues, larger roots or crowns, more stored energy, and more ability to shield themselves or nearby plants from contact. For mature vegetation, Zasso should be positioned as a strong non-chemical tool that may require slower operation, good contact, suitable timing, sequential passes, or integration with mowing or other weed-management practices.

## Detailed Answer

Mature plants can be treated with electrical weeding, but they should not be treated as the easiest target category. Zasso's Electroherb technology works by transferring controlled electrical energy into contacted vegetation and through the plant-soil circuit. For a mature plant, the objective is not only to desiccate visible top growth; it is to deliver enough useful energy into the tissues that allow the plant to survive and regrow.

Plant maturity changes the treatment challenge in several ways. Mature plants may have thicker stems, more lignified tissue, waxier or hairier surfaces, larger canopies, deeper or larger roots, crowns, rhizomes, tubers, stolons, or other recovery structures. They may also occur in dense stands where taller plants shield lower stems, smaller weeds, or neighboring plants from electrode contact. These factors can reduce contact quality, divide energy among multiple plants, or increase the amount of energy and contact time needed for durable control.

That does not mean mature plants are unsuitable. Public and internal evidence supports the use of electrical weeding on later-stage weeds in suitable conditions, including research with Zasso XPower equipment in vineyards, blueberries, and Australian field trials. The practical message is that mature-plant control depends more strongly on species, morphology, plant water status, soil moisture, weed density, electrode contact, speed, power delivery, pass number, product configuration, operator training, and follow-up scouting.

For sales and agronomy discussions, the safest claim is that Zasso can treat mature plants, but mature weeds often need a more deliberate program than young emerged weeds. Customers should expect local validation for the target species and site, especially where plants are woody, perennial, rhizomatous, dense, lodged, dry, or already close to reproduction.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers facing mature weeds should plan Zasso application around control objectives and regrowth risk. If the goal is burn-down of visible top growth, the result may be visible quickly. If the goal is longer-lasting suppression, especially for mature perennial or grass weeds, the protocol should consider slower speed, better electrode contact, sequential applications, and follow-up checks over days or weeks.

Mature plants are often still worth targeting when herbicide options are restricted, resistance is present, chemical residues or drift are a concern, or soil disturbance should be minimized. However, mature vegetation usually leaves less margin for poor timing or poor operation. A successful trial should record weed species, growth stage, height, density, moisture conditions, recent mowing or residue, operating speed, number of passes, and regrowth observations.

The customer-friendly expectation is: Zasso can be part of a mature-weed control strategy, but the treatment should be matched to the field rather than sold as a universal one-pass fix.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can treat mature plants, but mature weeds usually need more careful application than young weeds because plant size, root reserves, density, and contact quality become more important."

"For mature vegetation, we focus on matching speed, contact, timing, and pass strategy to the weed species and field conditions rather than promising one universal setting."

"In suitable conditions, Zasso can be a valuable non-chemical tool for mature weed control, especially where reducing herbicide reliance, drift, residues, or soil disturbance matters."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso always controls mature plants in one pass, permanently kills every mature root system, or works equally well on every species and site. Mature plants can be difficult when they are woody, dense, lodged, dry, rhizomatous, tuberous, protected by crowns, shielded by canopy, or poorly contacted by the electrodes.

Avoid giving universal plant-height, speed, power, dose, or pass-number recommendations without product-specific and crop-specific guidance. Mature weeds near seed set may also require management of seed return, not only control of existing biomass. Do not disclose confidential settings, control logic, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, telemetry, certification details, or private meeting content.
