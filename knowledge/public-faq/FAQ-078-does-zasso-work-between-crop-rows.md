# Does Zasso work between crop rows?

## Short Answer

Yes. Zasso can work between crop rows when the target weeds are emerged, reachable, and contacted by the electrodes without contacting the crop. Inter-row use is one of the most natural fits for electrical weeding because it can provide non-chemical control of weeds in the open space between rows while reducing soil disturbance compared with cultivation. It should not be presented as automatic control of weeds hidden inside the crop row or as safe if the electrodes touch the crop.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology is a contact-based, non-chemical weed-control method. In an inter-row application, the objective is to place the electrical treatment zone in the space between crop rows so that the electrodes contact weeds rather than the crop. When the machine setup, guidance, row spacing, weed stage, and operating speed are suitable, this can reduce emerged weeds between rows without relying on herbicide over the full field area and without the same soil movement caused by inter-row cultivation.

This makes inter-row use particularly relevant for row crops where early weed competition is important, where herbicide reduction is a goal, where resistant weeds are present, or where the grower wants another physical tool in an integrated weed-management program. Public and Zasso-related materials support strong inter-row performance in selected conditions, including sugar beet and cotton examples. Evidence also shows that broadleaf weeds can be especially responsive, while grasses may require slower speeds, stronger contact, or more than one pass.

The main limitation is selectivity. Electrical weeding is not chemically selective: it affects contacted plants. The system must therefore avoid direct contact with crop plants, and it will not control weeds that remain hidden in the crop row if the electrodes do not reach them. For many customers, inter-row Zasso use is best positioned as a complementary tool alongside in-row weed control, guidance systems, crop shielding, herbicides where allowed, mechanical tools, or other local practices.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers can consider Zasso for between-row weed control when weeds are visible and reachable between crop rows, crop rows are sufficiently defined, and the equipment can be guided accurately through the field. The value proposition is strongest where customers want non-chemical control, reduced soil disturbance, resistance-management support, or fewer herbicide-treated areas in suitable programs.

Field setup matters. Row spacing, crop growth stage, weed height, weed density, soil moisture, residue, terrain, travel speed, electrode configuration, and guidance accuracy all influence results and crop safety. A local demonstration or protocol validation is especially important before customers rely on inter-row treatment in a commercial crop.

Customers should also plan for the weeds that Zasso does not touch. Weeds inside the crop row, underneath leaves, protected by crop canopy, or physically shielded from the electrodes may need follow-up control through other tactics. Zasso should be sold as part of integrated weed management, not as a one-pass guarantee for every weed in a row-crop field.

## Evidence and Context

These sources consistently support a conditional answer: inter-row electrical weed control can be effective, but results depend on contact quality, crop protection, timing, speed, weed species, and whether the equipment is designed and operated for the crop geometry.

SharePoint research reviewed Zasso marketing and technical material on inter-row post-emergence weed control and electrical-weeding application arrangements. The marketing material reports sugar beet inter-row field results where XPower electrical treatment achieved up to 95% weed reduction in the tested conditions and helped reduce herbicide use when in-row control was handled separately. The technical material supports the concept of segmented or inter-row applicators and reinforces that electrode arrangement and contact quality are central to performance.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can be used between crop rows when the weeds are emerged and reachable and the crop is protected from electrode contact. It is a non-chemical inter-row tool, not a guarantee of controlling weeds hidden inside the crop row."

"In suitable row-crop applications, Zasso can help reduce inter-row weed pressure while limiting soil disturbance compared with cultivation, subject to row spacing, guidance, weed stage, speed, and local validation."

"For best results, we treat inter-row electrical weeding as part of an integrated program: Zasso handles contacted weeds between rows, while other tactics may still be needed for in-row or shielded weeds."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is selective to crops. Crop safety depends on avoiding direct electrode contact with the crop and using the right equipment setup, guidance, operating procedures, and field conditions.

Do not claim full-field, one-pass, season-long, or in-row control from an inter-row application. Weeds inside the crop row, under crop canopy, protected by residue, too small for reliable contact, or emerging after the pass may require additional control.

Avoid overgeneralizing from sugar beet, cotton, lupine, or proof-of-concept row-crop studies to all crops and all row spacings. Performance should be validated locally, especially where row spacing is narrow, crops are small or easily damaged, grasses dominate, terrain is uneven, or guidance accuracy is limited.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, private meeting details, commercial terms, certification timelines, source files, or internal product-roadmap commitments unless approved for external use.
