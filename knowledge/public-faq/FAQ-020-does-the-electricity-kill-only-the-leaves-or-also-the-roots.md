# Does the electricity kill only the leaves or also the roots?

## Short Answer

Zasso's electrical weeding is not designed to only scorch leaves. The treatment makes the plant part of an electrical circuit, so current can move through above-ground tissues toward the crown, root system, soil, and return path. When enough useful energy reaches the plant's recovery tissues, including crown and root-zone tissues where relevant, the chance of lasting control improves. However, it should not be described as guaranteed complete root kill in every plant or condition.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding starts with contact above ground, usually through leaves, stems, or other exposed plant tissue touched by the applicator. From there, the plant conducts current through its internal water- and mineral-rich tissues. Because living plant tissue has electrical resistance, part of the energy is converted into heat and cellular injury, damaging membranes, vascular bundles, and the plant's ability to move water and nutrients.

The practical objective is more than visible leaf burn. If only leaves or upper stems are damaged, many weeds can recover, especially perennials, grasses, rhizomatous plants, or species with protected growing points. Durable control is more likely when the treatment disrupts the tissues that let the plant recover: stems, crown, vascular pathways, and root-zone structures. That is why Zasso materials often frame electrical weeding as a root-oriented or systemic physical effect rather than simple foliar desiccation.

The result still depends on the situation. Root effect is influenced by weed species, plant size, root architecture, plant water content, soil moisture, soil conductivity, electrode contact, operating speed, dose, pass number, and whether the current path goes through the target plant or is partly lost through easier pathways. Broadleaf weeds with good contact may respond differently from dense grasses or deep-rooted perennials. Some plants may need slower operation, better contact, mowing in selected canopy situations, or a second pass to reduce regrowth.

So the safest customer-facing answer is: Zasso can damage both shoots and below-ground recovery pathways when applied correctly, but the level of root control is condition-dependent and should be validated for the crop, weed spectrum, machine setup, and operating protocol.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not judge success only by whether the leaves brown quickly. Early wilting or dry-down is useful, but the more important question is whether regrowth is suppressed over the following days and weeks. Follow-up scouting is therefore part of good electrical weed-control practice.

For annual weeds or susceptible broadleaf species, a suitable pass may provide strong control when contact and operating conditions are right. For perennial weeds, grasses, rhizomes, large root systems, dense canopies, or stressed field conditions, lasting control may require adjusted speed, improved electrode contact, a planned repeat pass, or integration with mowing, cover, mechanical, or herbicide programs where appropriate.

This positioning helps avoid overpromising while still explaining the core benefit: electrical weeding is a non-chemical physical mode of action that can reach beyond the leaf surface and help manage regrowth when the application is correctly matched to the target.

"Zasso does not aim to just burn the leaves. The goal is to drive controlled electrical energy through the plant so the treatment can damage the tissues that support recovery, including crown and root-zone pathways when conditions are suitable."

"Root effect is one of the reasons electrical weeding can be valuable, but it depends on the weed, soil, moisture, contact quality, speed, and operating protocol, so we avoid promising complete root kill in every case."

## Caveats

Do not claim universal root kill, guaranteed one-pass control, or permanent elimination of all roots. Some weeds can regrow if the dose is too low, contact is poor, the plant is shielded, the growing point is protected, the root system is large, or field conditions reduce useful energy delivery.

Do not describe the effect as leaves only. That understates the mechanism and weakens the sales explanation. The better wording is that electricity starts at contacted plant tissue and can affect the plant's internal recovery pathways, including roots where the current path and dose are sufficient.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, internal control logic, unpublished test data, customer-specific results, certification details, pricing, or sensitive engineering designs.
