# Does Zasso work in pre-harvest desiccation?

## Short Answer

Zasso can be relevant for some pre-harvest desiccation or harvest-aid use cases, but it should be positioned as a crop- and application-specific opportunity, not as a universal desiccation replacement. The strongest public Zasso-linked evidence is in potato haulm desiccation, where early project results showed that the Electroherb method could reduce potato haulm and produce plant drying comparable to known desiccation approaches under tested conditions. For other crops, Zasso should be evaluated locally against the grower's target outcome: crop drydown, weed drydown, seed-set prevention, residue avoidance, harvest timing, crop quality, safety, and economics.

## Detailed Answer

Pre-harvest desiccation is normally used to speed and even out crop drydown before harvest. It is related to, but not identical with, pre-harvest weed control, where the main objective is to control annual or perennial weeds before they replenish the seedbank or interfere with harvest. Zasso's electrical weeding technology may fit parts of this space because it is a non-chemical, contact-based technology that damages green plant tissue through a controlled electrical circuit.

The answer depends heavily on the crop and the target plant material. Zasso should not be described as a general substitute for every chemical desiccant, because desiccation requires precise timing, predictable drydown, crop-quality protection, harvest compatibility, residue management, and local regulatory fit. A treatment that is useful for destroying potato haulm, suppressing late-season weeds, or terminating green biomass may not automatically deliver the same result in cereals, pulses, oilseeds, cotton, cover crops, or seed crops.

The best public desiccation-specific evidence found for Zasso is potato haulm work from the ElektroSiKa project. A 2018 public release reported that randomized plot trials at the Dethlingen test station found the Zasso Electroherb method could produce comparable degrees of potato plant drying, with and without haulm topping, in one pass under the tested conditions. It also reported no changes in sugar, starch, and glycoalkaloid contents in those first results, while making clear that further work was planned to study tuber quality, varieties, soil moisture, larger areas, energy dose, and practical field robustness.

Broader electrical-weeding research supports the general mechanism and some adjacent use cases. Michigan State University Extension describes electrical weed control as an emerging method for late-season escaped and herbicide-resistant weeds, including potential use for potato vine desiccation before harvest, while noting that more research is needed for niche use cases. A 2025 Weed Science study using Zasso XPower in vineyards found substantial weed biomass reduction under tested conditions, but also cautioned about fire risk in completely dry biomass. These sources support Zasso's relevance as a non-chemical vegetation-control tool, but they do not prove universal pre-harvest desiccation performance across crops.

For customers, the practical answer is: yes, Zasso may work in suitable pre-harvest desiccation-style applications where the target biomass can be contacted safely and where crop quality, harvest timing, residue expectations, fire risk, and economics are validated. The correct next step is a crop-specific protocol or pilot, not a generic claim.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers interested in desiccation should start by defining the job precisely. Is the goal to dry the crop evenly, destroy haulm or stalks, reduce green weeds at harvest, prevent seed return, avoid chemical residues, meet an organic or residue-sensitive requirement, or replace a restricted active ingredient? Each goal has different success metrics.

Zasso may be most attractive where chemical desiccants face regulatory, residue, worker-safety, supply-chain, organic, or customer-acceptance pressure. It may also be useful where the customer wants a non-chemical tool that can fit into integrated weed management and reduce reliance on pre-harvest herbicides.

A responsible commercial evaluation should compare Zasso with the current desiccation or harvest-aid program on visible drydown, harvest timing, regrowth, crop injury, quality, residue or withholding constraints, operating speed, number of passes, energy and fuel use, labor, fire-risk management, and cost per treated hectare or treated strip.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can be evaluated for selected pre-harvest desiccation or harvest-aid applications, especially where the customer wants a non-chemical way to manage green biomass before harvest."

"The strongest public Zasso-linked desiccation evidence is in potato haulm reduction, but every crop and harvest system should be validated locally before making performance or quality claims."

"For desiccation projects, we recommend defining the target outcome first: crop drydown, weed drydown, residue avoidance, harvest timing, crop quality, or seedbank reduction."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is a universal replacement for chemical desiccants, glyphosate, diquat, paraquat, glufosinate, or other harvest aids. Do not claim guaranteed drydown timing, one-pass desiccation, permanent control, full herbicide replacement, universal organic acceptance, or no fire risk.

Suitability depends on crop, variety, growth stage, crop maturity, target biomass, plant water content, soil moisture, residue, weed density, weather, dry biomass, fire-risk conditions, row geometry, applicator access, speed, power delivery, operator training, equipment configuration, and local regulations.

Crop quality must be protected. In seed, grain, tuber, fiber, oilseed, and food crops, validation should include quality, residue requirements, harvestability, germination where relevant, storage behavior, and buyer requirements.

Fire risk needs special attention in dry biomass, drought, wind, straw, mulch, and residue-heavy conditions. Public Zasso-related vineyard research found fire risk in completely dry plant biomass under test conditions, so sales teams should not overstate safety in dry pre-harvest environments.
