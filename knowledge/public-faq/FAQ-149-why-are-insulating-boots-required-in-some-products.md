# Why are insulating boots required in some products?

## Short Answer

Insulating boots are required in some Zasso products because they are part of the product-specific safety concept for working near high-voltage electrical weeding equipment. They help reduce the risk that the operator's feet become part of an unintended electrical path through the ground, wet surfaces, vegetation, or conductive objects. Boots do not make the system risk-free; they must be used together with the approved manual, training, exclusion zone, inspection, shutdown procedures, and local safety rules.

## Detailed Answer

Some Zasso systems use high-voltage energy near the ground, vegetation, electrodes, machine structures, and field surfaces. In those products, insulating or dielectric boots may be specified to add a protective layer between the operator and the ground. This matters because electrical exposure is not limited to touching an electrode with a hand. In abnormal or misuse conditions, risk can also involve foot-to-ground contact, step voltage, wet surfaces, conductive debris, metal objects, damaged insulation, or a person standing near an active high-voltage area.

Insulating boots are therefore a risk-reduction measure. They are intended to reduce the chance that current can pass through the operator via the feet or through a conductive contact path at ground level. In some product concepts, boots may also be tied to the operating procedure, for example through inspection requirements, operator authorization, product startup logic, or a defined safe operating position.

The requirement is product-specific. A handheld or pedestrian-operated product may need different PPE from a tractor-mounted or fully enclosed system because the operator's position, proximity to the electrodes, contact with the ground, exposure to wet or conductive surfaces, and available guarding can be different. Customers should not assume that boots are required for every Zasso product, or that ordinary work boots provide the same protection as approved insulating footwear.

Insulating boots also do not replace the rest of the safety system. Operators must still keep the safety zone clear, avoid wet or unsuitable conditions where the manual prohibits use, inspect the boots before use, check maintenance or certification intervals where applicable, stop output when the zone is breached, and follow the approved deactivation and maintenance procedure before approaching high-voltage parts.

## What This Means for Customers

If the product manual or training requires insulating boots, customers should treat them as mandatory PPE. The machine should not be operated without the specified boots, and damaged, expired, wet, punctured, cracked, contaminated, or unapproved footwear should not be used as a substitute.

In practice, this means customers need a PPE routine. Operators should know which boots are approved, how to inspect them, how to keep them clean and dry, when they need replacement or certified inspection, and where they must be worn. Supervisors should also make sure boots are available in the correct sizes and that visitors or untrained staff do not enter the operating zone because an operator is wearing PPE.

The customer benefit is disciplined, repeatable safety. Instead of relying on judgment in the moment, the team follows the product-specific rule: wear the specified boots when required, keep the zone controlled, stop when conditions are unsafe, and follow the manual before restarting or maintaining the equipment.

## Safe Sales Wording

"For products where Zasso specifies insulating boots, they are mandatory PPE and part of the product's approved safety concept. They help reduce foot-to-ground electrical exposure risk, but they must be used together with training, safe distance, inspection, and the manual."

"Insulating boots are not a shortcut for approaching energized parts. They are one protective layer in a controlled operating procedure for high-voltage equipment."

"Customers should use only the footwear specified in the approved Zasso documentation for their machine, and should inspect and maintain it as instructed."

## Caveats

Do not claim that insulating boots eliminate electrical risk or make it safe to touch electrodes, enter the danger zone, work in prohibited wet conditions, bypass safety devices, or perform maintenance while the system may be energized.

Do not claim that ordinary rubber boots, safety shoes, or non-approved footwear are equivalent to the insulating or dielectric boots specified by Zasso. The required footwear type, rating, inspection routine, cleaning procedure, and replacement interval must come from the approved product documentation and local requirements.

PPE requirements may vary by product, applicator, operator position, voltage architecture, guarding, grounding or return-path design, local certification, work environment, soil and surface moisture, nearby metal, maintenance task, and local regulation. The approved manual and training always take priority over this general FAQ.
