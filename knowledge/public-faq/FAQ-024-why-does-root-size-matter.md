# Why does root size matter?

## Short Answer

Root size matters because lasting electrical weed control depends on damaging the plant tissues that allow recovery, not only drying the leaves. Plants with larger roots, crowns, rhizomes, tubers, stolons, or other underground reserves may need more useful energy, better contact, slower travel, or repeat treatment to reduce regrowth. Smaller root systems can be easier to control when the applicator contacts the plant well, but results still depend on species, moisture, soil conditions, weed density, equipment setup, and trained operation.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's technology works by making the target plant part of an electrical circuit. Energy enters through contacted leaves, stems, or other above-ground tissue and can move through the plant toward the crown, roots, soil, and return path. Because living plant tissue has electrical resistance, part of that energy is converted into cellular and vascular damage.

Root size is important because roots are part of the plant's recovery system. A small annual seedling may have limited underground reserves and fewer recovery points. A mature perennial, grass, vine, woody weed, or rhizomatous plant can have a larger root system and protected growing tissues that help it survive top injury and regrow. In those cases, visible leaf burn is not enough to prove durable control.

In practical terms, a larger or more complex root system usually raises the treatment challenge. The application may need more contact time, more suitable electrode geometry, slower speed, a second pass, mowing before treatment in dense vegetation, or integration with other weed-management practices. The best operating choice depends on the actual weed spectrum, root architecture, growth stage, plant water content, soil moisture, soil conductivity, field conditions, and product configuration.

The sales-safe message is that Zasso is designed to support root-oriented electrical treatment where conditions are suitable, but root effect is not automatic or universal. Root size is one of the main reasons field assessment, operator training, and follow-up scouting matter.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should expect root size to influence the treatment plan and the follow-up expectations. Young annual weeds with small root systems may be controlled more readily when contact is good. Larger perennial weeds, grasses with protected growing points, vines, plants with rhizomes or tubers, and dense mature patches may need a more deliberate protocol.

For operations teams, root size should be assessed together with plant height, density, species, growth stage, moisture, and accessibility. If the root system is large or the weed is known for regrowth, customers should plan for careful speed selection, good electrode contact, possible repeat passes, and follow-up scouting over days or weeks. This helps avoid judging success only by early leaf symptoms.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso always kills all roots, permanently eliminates every perennial, or guarantees one-pass control. Root effect depends on contact quality, delivered energy, plant and soil conditions, species morphology, root reserves, equipment configuration, speed, and operator practice.

Avoid giving universal root-size thresholds, speed settings, dose values, or pass-number recommendations without product-specific and crop-specific guidance. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, control logic, unpublished test data, customer-specific results, certification details, pricing, or sensitive engineering information.
