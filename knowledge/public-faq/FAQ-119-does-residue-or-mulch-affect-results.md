# Does residue or mulch affect results?

## Short Answer

Yes, residue or mulch can affect Zasso results, mainly by changing how well the electrodes contact the target weeds. Light or well-settled residue is usually less of a concern when weeds are exposed and contactable, but thick mulch, crop residue, straw, plastic weed mat, or layered biomass can shield weeds, lift electrodes away from plants, alter moisture conditions, and increase fire risk when dry. The practical rule is simple: Zasso works best when the target vegetation is visible, reachable, and safely contacted by the applicator.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology is contact-based. Electrical energy is delivered when the applicator contacts living target vegetation and completes an electrical pathway through the plant and return circuit. Residue and mulch matter when they interfere with that pathway or with the physical movement of the applicator.

In practical field terms, residue can affect results in several ways. A layer of straw, cut biomass, crop residue, sawdust, plastic mulch, weed mat, leaves, or pruning material can hide smaller weeds, physically separate the electrodes from green tissue, reduce contact time, or cause the applicator to ride over the target instead of touching it. Residue can also create uneven working height, especially in bands, ridges, wheel tracks, under canopy, or dense perennial crop rows. Where weeds grow through residue and are still exposed above it, Zasso can still be effective, but the field setup needs to ensure good electrode contact.

Mulch systems are not automatically unsuitable. Zasso-relevant blueberry research in organic fields included sawdust mulch and plastic weed mat systems, and electrical weed control was evaluated in that context. The key was not the mere presence of mulch, but the ability to protect crop structures, avoid irrigation lines, and contact weeds at an appropriate speed and applicator setup. In dense or tall weed stands, mowing before electrical treatment improved contact in the Oregon blueberry work, while mowing after treatment did not improve results. That supports a careful operational message: residue management can sometimes improve results when it improves access to the lower stems and target foliage.

Dry residue needs special caution. Public Australian research using Zasso-related XPower equipment reported low fire risk in winter and spring conditions, but significant fire risk when operating in completely dry residue during summer or early autumn. The risk also varied by residue type. For customers, dry straw, dry mulch, dry cut biomass, wind, heat, and drought should trigger a field-specific safety review before application.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should assess residue and mulch as part of the pre-application field check. The question is not simply whether residue is present; it is whether the residue blocks electrode contact, hides the target weeds, creates uneven applicator movement, contacts sensitive crop or irrigation components, or creates an unsafe dry-biomass condition.

Good candidates are fields where weeds are growing through or above the residue layer and the applicator can maintain stable contact with the target vegetation. More difficult situations include thick straw, loose prunings, heavy cut biomass, dense mulch bands, weeds hidden below residue, plastic or synthetic covers, irrigation lines near the electrode path, and very dry residue with elevated fire risk.

If residue is limiting contact, customers may need to adjust timing, applicator height, travel speed, pass strategy, mowing sequence, or residue management. In some cases, the right answer is not to force the application, but to wait for safer conditions or use a complementary weed-management step.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Residue or mulch does not automatically prevent Zasso from working, but it can affect results if it blocks contact between the electrodes and the target weeds. We check whether weeds are exposed, reachable, and safe to treat before making a recommendation."

"Zasso works best when the target vegetation is visible and contactable. Heavy residue, thick mulch, plastic covers, or dry cut biomass may require a different timing, setup, or residue-management step."

"In dry-residue conditions, fire risk must be evaluated carefully before operation. We do not recommend treating high-risk residue situations without an approved field-safety assessment."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso works equally well through all mulch or residue layers. The system needs contact with living target vegetation.

Do not claim that residue has no safety implications. Dry straw, dry mulch, dry cut biomass, wind, heat, and drought can increase fire risk and may restrict operation.

Do not make universal recommendations for mowing, residue removal, speed, pass number, or applicator height without crop-, product-, region-, and field-specific validation. Avoid disclosing confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, certification details, internal safety analysis, or private meeting content.
