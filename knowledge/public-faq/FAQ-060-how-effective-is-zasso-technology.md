# How effective is Zasso technology?

## Short Answer

Zasso technology can be highly effective when it is applied to the right weeds, at the right stage, with good electrode contact, suitable speed, adequate energy delivery, and trained operation. Public and internal evidence shows strong weed suppression in several use cases, including organic blueberry, vineyard, soybean, cotton, potato, sugar beet, and other field-trial contexts. Results should still be described as condition-dependent rather than guaranteed, because efficacy varies with weed species, plant size, density, soil moisture, soil conductivity, operating speed, number of passes, and follow-up management.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology uses controlled electrical energy to damage unwanted plants through a physical, non-chemical mode of action. In practical terms, efficacy depends on whether enough useful energy reaches the target plant tissues and recovery structures through a good plant-soil electrical path. This is why field results are affected by contact quality, application speed, plant size, weed density, soil condition, moisture, operator training, and the treatment program.

The best customer-facing answer is that Zasso can deliver strong weed control in suitable applications, but it should not be sold as a universal one-pass guarantee. In some trials, electrical weed control has achieved high visual control or major biomass reduction, especially at slower speeds, with repeated passes, or when combined with complementary practices such as mowing or mulch. Other situations, such as dense infestations, mature grasses, deep-rooted perennials, dry or highly variable soil conditions, poor contact, or fast travel speed, may require adjusted timing, slower operation, sequential applications, or integration with other weed-management methods.

Zasso should therefore be positioned as a professional weed-management system, not a simple headline percentage. The technology is most credible when presented as part of a planned program: assess the target weeds and field conditions, choose the right product and applicator, train operators, apply within the approved safety and operating envelope, and monitor regrowth after treatment.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should expect Zasso performance to be validated against their own crop, weed spectrum, soil, safety constraints, operating window, and business model. A good trial should define the baseline method, target weeds, weed stage, speed, number of passes, weather and soil conditions, crop-safety requirements, and how success will be measured.

For many customers, Zasso's value is strongest where chemical options are restricted, herbicide resistance is a problem, residue avoidance matters, soil disturbance should be reduced, or organic and regenerative programs need non-chemical tools. The technology can help reduce reliance on herbicides in suitable programs, but customers should plan for agronomic judgment, trained operators, and follow-up scouting rather than assuming every field will respond the same way.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can deliver strong weed control when the product, target weeds, field conditions, operating speed, contact quality, and treatment program are correctly matched."

"The strongest results come from controlled energy delivery, good electrode contact, trained operation, and the right treatment timing; it is not a universal one-pass guarantee."

"In suitable programs, Zasso can be an effective non-chemical tool to reduce reliance on herbicides and support integrated weed management."

## Caveats

Do not promise guaranteed control, complete root kill, permanent prevention of regrowth, one-pass success, or the same percentage efficacy across all crops, weeds, soils, climates, or product configurations. Avoid saying Zasso is always equal or superior to herbicides or mechanical weeding; the fair comparison depends on the target weeds, local restrictions, operating cost, soil disturbance, safety requirements, residue goals, and program design.

Do not disclose confidential field data, unpublished customer results, raw trial files, customer names, pricing, power-module settings, control logic, telemetry details, certification timelines, or product-specific operating thresholds unless they have been approved for external use.
