# Does Zasso work on monocotyledons?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on monocotyledon weeds when the plants are properly contacted and the treatment protocol is matched to their species, stage, density, moisture, and regrowth potential. In practical weed-control language, many important monocot targets are grasses, and Zasso evidence supports treating grasses in suitable conditions. The careful claim is that monocotyledons are within Zasso's treatable target spectrum, not that every monocot species or growth stage will be controlled equally or in one pass.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology uses controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation. Because the mode of action is physical rather than herbicide-based, a plant is not protected simply because it is a monocotyledon. If electrodes make good contact and enough useful energy is delivered through the plant and plant-soil circuit, monocotyledon weeds can be damaged and controlled.

The main practical issue is morphology. Many monocot weeds, especially grasses, have narrow leaves, protected growing points, tillers, dense stands, fibrous roots, rhizomes, stolons, or strong regrowth reserves. These traits can make contact and durable control more demanding than for some upright broadleaf weeds. Dense grass mats may shield smaller plants, divide the electrical load across many contact points, and increase the need for slower operation, repeated passes, mowing or other preparation, or integration with additional weed-management tactics.

Internal Zasso plant-susceptibility material states that Electroherb susceptibility is mainly driven by physical applicator contact, plant size, delivered energy, and the plant-soil current path rather than monocot-versus-dicot identity. That supports customer-facing confidence that monocots can be treated. However, external field evidence also shows that grasses are often more variable than broadleaf weeds, so monocot control should be framed as condition-dependent and validated for the customer's species and field conditions.

For customers, the right discussion is not botanical classification alone. It should identify the target monocot species, whether it is annual or perennial, whether it has tillers, rhizomes, stolons, bulbs, tubers, or protected growing points, the growth stage, plant height, density, moisture, soil condition, crop proximity, operating speed, pass strategy, and acceptable regrowth threshold.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers dealing with monocot weeds such as ryegrass, kikuyu, couch grass, wild oats, annual veldt grass, Brachiaria, Johnsongrass, volunteer cereals, or other grass-like targets should consider Zasso as a possible non-chemical tool. It can be especially relevant where herbicide resistance, residue concerns, soil-disturbance reduction, organic or reduced-chemical programs, or sensitive-area management matter.

The best trial should assess monocot weeds separately from broadleaf weeds. Record the species, stage, density, tillering, plant moisture, soil moisture, whether the stand is upright or lodged, mowing history, speed, pass count, and regrowth over the following weeks. For perennial or rhizomatous monocots, success should be measured beyond immediate visible injury, because recovery from belowground reserves may be the main performance question.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can treat monocotyledon weeds, including many grasses, when contact quality, growth stage, density, moisture, speed, and follow-up strategy are properly managed."

"Monocot control is less about the botanical label and more about whether the applicator can make good contact and deliver enough useful energy to the plant structures that allow regrowth."

"For dense, mature, perennial, rhizomatous, or stoloniferous monocots, we recommend validating the protocol and planning for slower speeds, sequential passes, or integration with other weed-management practices where appropriate."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso controls all monocotyledons, controls monocots as easily as broadleaf weeds, eliminates rhizomes or stolons in every case, or guarantees one-pass control. Monocot species, growth stage, density, tillering, lodging, plant moisture, soil moisture, crop proximity, contact quality, operating speed, pass count, and regrowth potential all matter.

Be especially careful with mature grasses, dense mats, perennial grasses, rhizomatous or stoloniferous species, dry biomass, protected growing points, and high-fire-risk conditions. In those situations, credible recommendations may include slower operation, repeat passes, mowing or other preparation, seasonal timing, or integration with chemical, mechanical, cultural, or scouting-based practices where allowed.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished customer results, raw trial data, control logic, telemetry details, pricing, certification plans, or private meeting content unless explicitly approved for external use.
