# Does it affect earthworms?

## Short Answer

Based on current Zasso materials and available external research, Zasso's electrical weeding is not expected to meaningfully harm earthworm populations when used under validated operating conditions. Several Zasso-related studies and public summaries report no significant or lasting reduction in earthworm populations after tested applications, and the technology does not add chemical residues to the soil. However, the evidence should be presented carefully because at least one published study reported reduced earthworm biomass and visible skin effects under specific field-forage conversion conditions, so local soil conditions, dose, timing, and repeated use still matter.

## Detailed Answer

Earthworms are important soil organisms. They help mix organic matter, support soil structure, improve infiltration, contribute to nutrient cycling, and are often used as practical indicators of soil biological health. Because electrical weeding uses high voltage and some current can pass through the plant-root-soil circuit, customers are right to ask whether earthworms could be affected.

The best current answer is cautiously positive, not absolute. Zasso's Electroherb approach is a non-chemical physical treatment that targets contacted weeds during application. It is not designed to sterilize the soil, fumigate the root zone, or leave herbicide residues. Current is concentrated through the treated plant and return path, while exposure of soil organisms depends on factors such as soil moisture, conductivity, root contact, depth, organism position, application energy, speed, and repeated passes.

Zasso-held ecotoxicology material summarizes grassland, agricultural-field, orchard, and soil-biology evidence. In that internal synthesis, BioChem agrar work reported no severe or lasting population-level effects on earthworms under realistic Electroherb application conditions, and CTIFL orchard testing found no acute lethal effect on earthworms one day after repeated XPS/XPO applications in apple orchard conditions. Public literature also includes studies where earthworm populations after Zasso XPower treatments were comparable to untreated controls and more stable than after some mechanical or chemical treatments.

The wider literature is not perfectly uniform. A 2023 Soil & Tillage Research study reported lower endogeic earthworm biomass two weeks after an electrical treatment in a field-fodder conversion context, and a Weed Science review notes both reassuring studies and reports of visible earthworm skin changes at treatment time in some cases. This means Zasso should avoid claiming that earthworms are never affected in any circumstance. The safer and more credible claim is that tested Zasso applications under validated conditions have not shown meaningful or lasting population-level harm, while more long-term and site-specific monitoring remains useful.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers focused on soil health, Zasso can be positioned as a residue-free, non-chemical weed-control option that may reduce reliance on herbicides and soil-disturbing mechanical operations in suitable applications. This can be especially relevant in orchards, vineyards, organic systems, regenerative programs, and areas where preserving soil structure and soil biology is a priority.

Where earthworm protection is a formal requirement or a customer concern, the responsible approach is to evaluate Zasso under local conditions. Monitoring should compare treated and untreated areas using consistent sampling depth, season, moisture status, soil type, crop system, weed density, application settings, and repeated-use history. Avoid comparing electrical weeding only to an untreated control when the real management choice is often between electrical, mechanical, chemical, or mixed programs.

## Evidence and Context

Zasso's internal ecotoxicology chapter summarizes evidence from BioChem agrar, CTIFL, UNESP, and related contexts. It reports no significant adverse effects on earthworms, springtails, soil microarthropods, or microbial activity under realistic Electroherb application conditions, while noting that the evidence remains an internal synthesis rather than a single independent meta-analysis.

A Zasso SharePoint ecotoxicology introduction states that field and laboratory studies across geographies and crop systems found no measurable, lasting harm to soil fauna or microbial activity under real operating conditions, including stable earthworm populations.

The 2023 Weed Science review, "Exploring the potential of electric weed control," describes electrical weed control as a developing non-chemical technology and summarizes mixed soil-biota findings. It reports studies where Zasso XPower treatments did not significantly reduce Lumbricina populations, while also noting conflicting results and the need for more research on short-term, long-term, and site-specific effects: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/exploring-the-potential-of-electric-weed-control-a-review/231EE50C385EF8962CDC46055C264237

A 2024 DPIRD/Agronomy Australia paper using Zasso XPower focused on a soil biological indicator, Rhizoctonia solani AG8, and found no effect even at slow application speed and repeated applications at the same site. The paper also cites prior studies reporting no significant differences in earthworm populations after Zasso XPower treatments, while acknowledging other research that found reductions or visible effects: https://library.dpird.wa.gov.au/conf_papers/345/

A 2023 Soil & Tillage Research study reported reduced endogeic earthworm biomass two weeks after an electrical treatment in a field-fodder conversion setting and concluded that more research is needed before broad market introduction in that context: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167198723001137

GROW Integrated Weed Management summarized preliminary U.S. research reporting no difference in health or survival of red wiggler earthworms from electrocuted and non-electrocuted plots in a Weed Zapper study, and no observed ill effects on soil-dwelling arthropods or microbial respiration in preliminary multi-state Zasso evaluations: https://growiwm.org/after-the-volts-is-weed-electrocution-safe-for-the-soil/

Zasso's public technology page positions Electroherb as a direct, non-chemical, residue-free physical mode of action: https://zasso.com/technology/

## Safe Sales Wording

"Based on current Zasso materials and available external studies, tested Zasso applications have not shown meaningful or lasting population-level harm to earthworms under validated conditions. We still recommend evaluating soil-biology questions in the customer's local crop, soil, and operating context."

"Zasso is a non-chemical, residue-free physical treatment. The responsible claim is earthworm compatibility under tested and validated conditions, not that every earthworm in every soil will be unaffected in every scenario."

## Caveats

Do not say that Zasso has zero effect on earthworms in all conditions. Earthworm response may depend on species, life stage, depth, soil moisture, soil conductivity, root contact, organic matter, season, application speed, energy dose, repeated passes, and prior management.

Avoid claiming that Zasso improves earthworm populations unless supported by a specific local study. The evidence base is encouraging but still developing, and at least one published study reported negative earthworm-biomass effects in a specific use case. For sensitive projects, recommend local validation or monitoring.
