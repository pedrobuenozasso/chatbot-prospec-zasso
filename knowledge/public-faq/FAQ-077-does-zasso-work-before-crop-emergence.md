# Does Zasso work before crop emergence?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work before crop emergence when the target weeds have already emerged and can be contacted by the electrodes. In that situation, electrical weeding can act as a non-chemical pre-emergence or stale-seedbed treatment before the crop is visible above the soil. It should not be described as a residual pre-emergence herbicide, because it does not prevent ungerminated weed seeds from emerging later.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology works on living, contactable plant tissue. That means a pre-crop-emergence application can be useful when weeds, volunteer plants, cover crop residue, or early flushes are present above the soil surface before the crop emerges. The treatment is physical and non-chemical: electrical energy is delivered through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil electrical path, damaging plant tissues and reducing early weed pressure.

The strongest Zasso-specific evidence for this timing comes from sugar beet. A 2019 German field trial compared pre-emergence weed-control methods in sugar beet, including Zasso Electroherb / XPower at 3 and 5 km/h with 72 kW nominal power. In the no-glyphosate comparison, the XPower variants left 5 to 11 weeds per 10 m2, while non-electrical variants left 54 to 97 weeds per 10 m2. This supports the careful claim that Zasso can provide strong early weed-density reduction before crop emergence under suitable conditions.

However, this use case is not the same as a soil-residual herbicide. Pre-emergence herbicides can act in the germination zone and suppress weeds before they appear. Electrical weeding requires contact with emerged vegetation, so weeds that germinate after the electrical pass may still need follow-up control. Application timing, field traffic, crop row position, soil condition, electrode setup, guidance accuracy, and crop emergence stage all matter.

For customers, the safest framing is that Zasso can fit into pre-emergence or stale-seedbed programs where emerged weeds are present before the crop comes up, especially where chemical reduction, herbicide resistance, erosion-sensitive systems, or organic and reduced-residue programs are priorities. It should be planned as part of an integrated weed-management program, not sold as a universal replacement for residual chemistry.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers can consider Zasso before crop emergence when they need to clean up emerged weeds before the crop appears, reduce early competition, avoid additional chemical burndown in suitable situations, or manage fields where mechanical disturbance is undesirable. This can be particularly relevant in crops or systems where early weed competition is costly and where there is a clear application window before the crop is vulnerable above ground.

The field should be assessed before treatment. The key question is whether the target weeds are present, living, reachable, and large enough for reliable electrode contact. If most of the weed pressure is still in the seed bank or has not emerged, an electrical pass will not provide residual protection.

Pre-emergence use also requires operational care. Traffic over planted rows, crop emergence timing, RTK guidance, tire configuration, soil moisture, residue, speed, and product configuration can affect both weed-control results and crop safety. Local validation is especially important where the crop is close to emergence or where rows are not clearly protected from machine traffic.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can be used before crop emergence when the target weeds are already emerged and contactable. It is best understood as a non-chemical cleanup or stale-seedbed tool, not as a residual herbicide."

"In a sugar beet pre-emergence field trial, Zasso's electrical treatment strongly reduced early weed density under the tested conditions, but timing, traffic management, crop stage, and local validation are important."

"For pre-emergence programs, we position Zasso as part of integrated weed management: it can reduce emerged weeds before the crop comes up, while later germinating weeds may still require follow-up control."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso controls ungerminated weed seeds, provides residual soil activity, or replaces every pre-emergence herbicide program. Electrical weeding needs emerged, living, contactable plant tissue.

Do not claim universal crop safety before emergence. Crop traffic, row position, soil condition, timing close to emergence, guidance accuracy, and equipment configuration can affect risk. In the sugar beet source, tractor rollover effects on emerging seedlings were specifically noted as an operational caution.

Avoid promising yield benefit, one-pass control, season-long control, or herbicide replacement unless supported by crop-specific, locally validated evidence. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial details, customer data, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
