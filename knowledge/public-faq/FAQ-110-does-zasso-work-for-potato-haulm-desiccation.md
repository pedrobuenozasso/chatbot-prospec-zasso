# Does Zasso work for potato haulm desiccation?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso's Electroherb technology has been tested for potato haulm desiccation and can be a relevant non-chemical option in suitable potato programs. The best-supported use is not an early, universal vine-kill claim; performance depends strongly on potato variety, plant vigor, timing, growth stage, moisture conditions, applicator setup, and whether a second pass or complementary treatment is used. Customers should treat potato haulm desiccation as a validated-pilot application, with tuber quality, regrowth, harvest timing, safety, and economics measured locally.

## Detailed Answer

Potato haulm desiccation is used to stop haulm growth, support tuber skin set and detachment, manage harvest timing, reduce regrowth, and limit late-season disease or virus risks. It is a demanding application because the grower wants reliable canopy and stem drydown without compromising tuber quality, storage behavior, marketability, or harvest logistics.

Zasso's technology is relevant because it applies electrical energy directly to contacted plant tissue. In potato haulm, the goal is to damage the green biomass enough to accelerate desiccation while avoiding unacceptable crop-quality or operating risks. Historical Zasso-linked public materials and internal Zasso research summaries indicate that Electroherb can desiccate potato haulm effectively when the application is timed around the right crop-development stage, especially as the crop approaches natural senescence.

The strongest practical message is that timing matters. Applications made when potato plants are still very vigorous are more likely to need a second treatment, a slower or more intensive treatment strategy, or integration with another locally accepted desiccation method. Applications closer to natural senescence are more favorable, but they still require the right protocol, equipment setup, operator training, safety controls, and local agronomic validation.

For customers, this means Zasso can be discussed as a promising physical haulm-desiccation tool, particularly where chemical desiccants are restricted, residue-sensitive, costly, or commercially unattractive. It should not be sold as a guaranteed one-pass replacement for every potato desiccation program.

## What This Means for Customers

Potato growers can consider Zasso where they need a non-chemical or reduced-chemical route to manage haulm before harvest. This may be most attractive in markets affected by diquat restrictions, residue expectations, organic or low-residue positioning, processor sustainability goals, or the need to reduce dependence on conventional chemical desiccants.

A good customer evaluation should compare Zasso against the current local program, such as flailing plus chemical desiccation, chemical-only desiccation where allowed, haulm topping, flaming, mechanical approaches, or untreated controls. The evaluation should measure visible drydown, stem kill, regrowth, tuber detachment, skin set, harvest readiness, tuber defects, storage quality, operating speed, number of passes, energy use, labor, safety, and total cost.

The best commercial framing is: Zasso may help growers add a physical, residue-free mode of action to potato haulm management, but the operating protocol must be adapted to local variety, growth stage, weather, soil and plant moisture, and harvest objectives.

## Caveats

Do not claim universal one-pass potato haulm kill, guaranteed replacement of diquat or other desiccants, guaranteed harvest timing, guaranteed tuber quality, or suitability in all varieties and conditions.

Performance depends on potato variety, plant vigor, BBCH or local growth stage, natural senescence, haulm density, stem condition, plant and soil moisture, soil conductivity, field trafficability, applicator height, electrode contact, driving speed, power delivery, number of passes, operator training, safety conditions, and the local desiccation program.

Earlier desiccation, very vigorous crops, wet or difficult field conditions, dense haulm, and strong regrowth potential require extra caution. In some systems, Zasso may be best used with a second pass, mechanical topping, or another locally accepted method rather than as a standalone treatment.

Any public claim about organic acceptance, regulatory status, crop quality, storage behavior, or processor approval should be checked locally before use.
