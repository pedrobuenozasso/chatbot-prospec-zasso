# Does Zasso work when herbicides fail?

## Short Answer

Often, yes, but it depends on why the herbicide failed. Zasso can be especially useful when herbicide performance is reduced by resistance, drift limits, residue concerns, public-use restrictions, or repeated dependence on the same chemistry, because Electroherb uses a physical electrical mode of action instead of a biochemical herbicide mode of action. But if the failure was caused by very large weeds, dense shielding, missed contact, poor timing, or unsuitable field conditions, Zasso also needs to be validated carefully and may require slower speed, repeat passes, mowing, or other integrated tactics.

## Detailed Answer

The phrase "when herbicides fail" can describe several very different problems. Sometimes weeds survive because they have evolved resistance to one or more herbicide modes of action. In other cases, the herbicide program may have been limited by drift risk, weather, timing, application coverage, crop constraints, public scrutiny, site sensitivity, or the simple fact that the target weeds were too large, too dense, or too difficult to hit consistently.

Zasso is most credible when the herbicide problem is linked to chemistry itself or to the practical limits of spraying. Electroherb delivers controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and injures the plant physically rather than relying on the biochemical pathway targeted by a herbicide active ingredient. That means a weed that survives a herbicide because of resistance is not automatically protected from electrical treatment.

However, Zasso should not be framed as a universal rescue pass for every failed spray program. If weeds escaped because they were hidden, overly mature, deeply perennial, shielded by dense biomass, or difficult to contact well, electrical treatment may still require operating adjustments and may not solve the problem in one pass. Likewise, if conditions are too dry, too wet, too dense, or operationally unsafe, results can be reduced.

The strongest customer-facing position is therefore practical: Zasso can work when herbicides fail, especially when customers need a different mode of action or a non-chemical option, but the fit still depends on species, growth stage, density, contact quality, moisture, speed, number of passes, and the broader management plan.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the first question should be why the herbicide failed. If the failure reflects resistance, repeated use of the same chemistry, or spray-related constraints, Zasso may be a strong next option because it changes the mode of action entirely. If the failure reflects difficult weed biology or poor access to the target, Zasso may still help, but the operating plan matters more and integrated follow-up may be needed.

In practice, Zasso is often most valuable for escapes, resistant populations, non-selective jobs in sensitive areas, perennial-crop rows, fence lines, and other use cases where customers want another serious tool besides repeating the same spray logic. The best commercial approach is to validate one clearly defined job, not to promise that every failed herbicide pass becomes an easy electrical success.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can work when herbicides fail, especially when the customer needs a different mode of action rather than more of the same chemistry."

"The key question is why the herbicide failed. If resistance or spray limitations are the issue, Zasso may be a strong next option, but it still needs to fit the weed biology and operating conditions."

"Zasso is strongest as part of integrated weed management, particularly for escapes, resistant populations, and jobs where repeating the same spray program is no longer robust."

## Caveats

Do not claim that every herbicide failure automatically means Zasso will succeed. Some causes of poor herbicide performance, such as very large weeds, dense shielding, poor access, or difficult perennial structures, can also limit electrical control or require repeat work.

Do not imply one-pass guaranteed control, universal herbicide replacement, or automatic cost advantage. Those claims are not supported.

Be especially careful when weeds are overly mature, very dense, shielded, dry enough to create fire risk, or present in conditions that reduce safe and effective contact. In those cases, mowing, slower travel speed, repeat passes, or broader integrated programs may still be needed.

Do not disclose confidential field data, private customer examples, internal settings, private meeting content, or unapproved regulatory claims.
