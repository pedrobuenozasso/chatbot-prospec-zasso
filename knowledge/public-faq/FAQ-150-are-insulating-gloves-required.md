# Are insulating gloves required?

## Short Answer

Insulating gloves are required when the approved Zasso manual, training, local procedure, or maintenance task specifies them. They may be required for some products, service activities, inspection steps, or work near potential electrical hazards, but customers should not assume the same glove rule applies to every Zasso machine or every operating mode. Gloves are only one protective layer; they do not make it safe to touch energized electrodes, bypass safety systems, enter the danger zone, or perform maintenance without shutdown and verification.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso equipment uses high-voltage electrical energy, so hand protection must be treated as a product-specific and task-specific safety requirement. For some products and tasks, gloves may be required as part of the personal protective equipment package, especially where hands could be exposed to sharp electrodes, hot surfaces, moving parts, arcing risk, or electrical hazards during operation, setup, cleaning, inspection, or maintenance.

The key point is that the glove requirement comes from the approved documentation and training for the specific machine. An operator using a handheld or pedestrian system may face different exposure than a tractor operator inside a cab. A trained service technician replacing electrodes or inspecting connectors may need different gloves from an operator walking behind the equipment. Cut-resistant gloves may be appropriate for mechanical or sharp-edge risks, while voltage-rated insulating gloves are only appropriate when they are specified for electrical shock protection and are correctly rated, inspected, maintained, and used with any required protectors.

Insulating gloves should never be presented as a substitute for engineering controls. Safe operation still depends on the exclusion zone, two-hand controls or other interlocks where fitted, warnings, insulation, grounding or return-path design, trained operation, pre-use inspection, shutdown, discharge, and maintenance procedures. If there is uncertainty about whether gloves are required, what rating is needed, or whether the gloves are still valid for use, the conservative action is to stop and confirm the approved instruction before operating or servicing the equipment.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should follow the PPE list in the manual and training for their exact Zasso product. If insulating gloves are listed, they should be treated as mandatory for the relevant task, not optional comfort equipment. Gloves should be available in the correct sizes, inspected before use, kept clean and dry, protected from cuts and punctures, and replaced or retested according to the applicable standard, manufacturer instruction, and local regulation.

Customers should also distinguish between glove types. Ordinary work gloves, gardening gloves, chemical gloves, or generic rubber gloves should not be assumed to provide electrical protection. When electrical protection is required, the glove type, voltage class, protector glove, inspection routine, and testing interval must come from the approved safety documentation and applicable local requirements.

The practical customer rule is simple: use the specified gloves for the specified task, keep hands away from energized parts, stop the machine before approaching the work area, and follow the approved shutdown and maintenance procedure before touching electrodes, connectors, cables, guards, or other safety-critical components.

## Caveats

Do not claim that insulating gloves are required for every Zasso product, every operator, or every task. The requirement depends on the machine, applicator, operator position, task, hazard assessment, local regulation, and approved documentation.

Do not claim that gloves eliminate electrical risk or make it safe to touch electrodes, work on energized components, enter the danger zone, override interlocks, use the equipment in prohibited wet conditions, or perform maintenance before shutdown and verification.

Do not treat ordinary work gloves, generic rubber gloves, gardening gloves, or chemical gloves as equivalent to voltage-rated insulating gloves. If electrical shock protection is required, the gloves must be suitable for the voltage and task, inspected before use, maintained correctly, and retested or replaced according to the applicable rules.

Product-specific glove ratings, testing intervals, approved models, service procedures, certification details, and internal risk analyses should only be shared from approved Zasso documentation.
