# How often should safety systems be inspected?

## Short Answer

Safety systems should be checked before each use, inspected again after any abnormal event, and reviewed at the scheduled intervals defined in the approved Zasso manual for the specific product. Some safety-critical inspections may need to be performed only by Zasso, an authorized dealer, or a trained high-voltage technician. Customers should not treat one interval as universal across all Zasso equipment; the product manual, training, service plan, local law, and operating conditions are the authoritative sources.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso equipment uses controlled high-voltage energy, so safety inspection has to be treated as an operating routine, not as an occasional administrative task. A practical customer-facing rule is to think in four layers: pre-use checks, routine cleaning and maintenance checks, event-based checks, and formal technical inspections.

Before each use, the operator should perform the checks required by the product manual and training. At a high level, this normally means looking for obvious damage, contamination, loose or damaged connectors, cable or insulation problems, abnormal electrode condition, blocked or dirty sensors, missing guards or warnings, emergency-stop issues, alarm messages, unsafe grounding or return-path conditions, unsafe weather or site conditions, and any reason the machine should not be energized.

Routine checks should also follow the manual's maintenance table and cleaning intervals. Depending on the product, this may include checking and cleaning electrodes, sensors, housings, filters, warning devices, plugs, high-voltage connectors, safety markings, protective equipment, and other safety-relevant parts. More technical tasks, especially insulation checks, high-voltage component inspections, safety-device checks, repairs, and annual or periodic inspections, should be performed only by authorized or qualified personnel.

Event-based inspection is equally important. If the machine contacts metal, water, buried infrastructure, irrigation equipment, fences, stones, abnormal vegetation loads, damaged cables, broken electrodes, fire-risk material, or anything else that may have affected safety, the operator should stop and inspect according to the manual before restarting. The same applies after alarms, repeated faults, unexplained shutdowns, unusual sparking, transport damage, storage damage, service work, software or hardware changes, or any suspected misuse.

For some product configurations, Zasso manual material reviewed for this FAQ specifies formal periodic inspection by trained high-voltage or dealer personnel, including safety-component review and insulation checks. The exact interval and proof requirements are product-specific, so sales teams should avoid giving a blanket promise. The safe answer is: check before each use, follow the scheduled maintenance and inspection intervals in the approved manual, and use authorized service for safety-critical technical inspections.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should build safety inspection into the daily operating process. Before the machine enters a field, orchard, vineyard, urban site, roadside, or demonstration area, the operator should confirm that the machine, PPE, safety zone, warnings, emergency stop, electrodes, cables, connectors, sensors, and site conditions are fit for the planned job.

Owners and fleet managers should also keep maintenance and inspection records. This is especially important for contractors, municipalities, distributors, and farms with multiple operators or seasonal use. A machine that has been stored, transported, modified, damaged, exposed to wet or dirty conditions, or used heavily may need additional checks before it is put back into operation.

The commercial message is simple: regular inspection protects people, uptime, warranty discipline, and customer confidence. If a safety-critical item is damaged, dirty, missing, bypassed, alarming, or uncertain, the machine should stay out of high-voltage operation until the approved manual or qualified support clears it.

## Caveats

Do not give one universal inspection interval for all Zasso products. Requirements can vary by product family, applicator, market, software or hardware version, operating hours, task, storage conditions, transport, weather exposure, field conditions, local regulation, service contract, warranty terms, and the approved manual.

Do not claim that a quick visual check replaces formal inspection, electrical testing, maintenance, or authorized service. Do not imply that a machine is safe simply because it powers on, has no visible damage, or has not shown an alarm.

Do not disclose confidential inspection forms, test thresholds, insulation values, internal safety architecture, sensor logic, fault-code details, service instructions, warranty exclusions, customer contracts, or product-specific certification details unless they are already approved for external release.
