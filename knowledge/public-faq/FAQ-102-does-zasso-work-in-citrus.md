# Does Zasso work in citrus?

## Short Answer

Yes. Zasso can work in citrus where the orchard layout, weed stage, soil and plant conditions, applicator setup, and safety controls are suitable. Citrus is a direct Zasso application area: Zasso's public E.CITRUS material positions the equipment for citrus and similar wider-row crops, and internal materials continue to treat citrus as an active perennial-crop opportunity. The safest customer-facing claim is that Zasso is a promising non-chemical tool for citrus tree-row and under-tree weed management, subject to local validation rather than a universal one-pass replacement for every citrus weed-control program.

## Detailed Answer

Citrus orchards have recurring weed-control needs around tree rows, under-canopy strips, irrigation zones, borders, and row edges. Weeds can compete with young or stressed trees for water, nutrients, light, and space; they can interfere with irrigation, spraying, harvesting, and worker access; and they can become part of a broader pest and disease-management problem. For many growers, the practical challenge is to manage weeds repeatedly without relying only on herbicide strips, soil-disturbing cultivation, hand cleanup, or other expensive non-chemical methods.

Zasso's Electroherb technology is a contact-based, non-chemical method. In citrus, that means the system must physically contact the target weeds while avoiding unacceptable contact with tree trunks, low branches, roots, irrigation hardware, metal objects, workers, animals, and dry biomass. This makes orchard geometry, canopy clearance, row spacing, weed height, soil and plant moisture, operator training, and fire-risk conditions central to performance.

Zasso has a citrus-specific public product concept. The E.CITRUS page describes a herbicide-free weeding machine for citrus or similar wider-row crops, with two front applicators covering 1.2 m on each side and an application-speed range up to 3.5 km/h in the published product description. SharePoint materials reviewed for this run also include EH Citrus in the agriculture portfolio, and recent Read.AI meeting context shows citrus included in current commercial and production discussions. Those internal sources support citrus as an active Zasso use case, but the customer-facing answer should avoid unapproved ROI, deployment, cost, first-client, or customer-quote claims.

For customers, the right answer is therefore positive but bounded: Zasso can be considered for citrus tree-row and under-tree weed control, especially where the grower wants to reduce chemical reliance, manage resistant or recurring weeds, support organic or low-residue programs, reduce soil disturbance, or create a differentiated service route. A measured pilot should compare Zasso against the grower's current program, including herbicide strips or spot sprays, mowing, cultivation, hand cleanup, cover crops, mulches, organic contact products, or contractor services.

## What This Means for Customers

Citrus customers should treat Zasso as a tool for integrated orchard-floor management, not as a blanket replacement for every existing practice. The strongest use cases are commercial orchards with repeatable rows, sufficient equipment access, visible target weeds, trained operators, and a clear reason to reduce or complement herbicide use.

A good citrus pilot should measure weed control, regrowth, crop safety, irrigation safety, fire-risk conditions, operating speed, energy or fuel use, labor, number of passes, and cost per treated hectare or treated strip. Young orchards may be especially sensitive to weed competition, but they also require careful crop-safety validation. Mature orchards may offer more shade and lower weed pressure in some areas, but vines, perennial weeds, dry biomass, low branches, and irrigation infrastructure can still create practical constraints.

Where validated locally, Zasso may help citrus growers reduce selected herbicide passes, lower reliance on soil-disturbing alternatives, support residue-sensitive programs, or add a non-chemical service option. The result will depend on the crop system, weed spectrum, timing, equipment configuration, operator discipline, and local safety rules.

The GROW weed electrocution factsheet describes electrical weed control as a non-chemical method that can affect plant and root tissue with minimal soil disturbance, while emphasizing that performance depends on factors such as weed density, species, plant moisture, and soil moisture, and that electrical weed control is usually supplemental rather than standalone. It also specifically lists Zasso XPower systems for specialty crops such as citrus, orchards, and coffee: https://growiwm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Weed-Electrocution-Factsheet-PDF.pdf

Washington State University orchard weed-management guidance reinforces that orchard weed control varies by weed type, site, labor, production system, soil, irrigation, and season, and that successful orchard programs usually combine multiple strategies: https://treefruit.wsu.edu/web-article/weed-management/

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can work in citrus where the orchard geometry, weed stage, applicator setup, and safety conditions are suitable. We recommend validating performance locally against the grower's current tree-row weed-control program."

"Citrus is a direct Zasso application area, especially for tree-row and under-tree weed management in wider-row perennial orchards."

"The best citrus fit is an integrated program: Zasso can help reduce chemical reliance in suitable blocks, while mowing, cover crops, targeted herbicides, or other practices may still have a role depending on the orchard."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is selective to citrus trees. Electrical weeding is contact-based, so crop tissue, trunks, low branches, exposed roots, irrigation components, metal objects, and workers must be protected through equipment setup, training, and operating procedures.

Do not claim guaranteed control, permanent control, season-long control, one-pass control, full herbicide replacement, or lower cost in every citrus orchard. Results depend on weed species, weed size, density, plant moisture, soil moisture, soil conductivity, residue, dry biomass, speed, contact quality, equipment configuration, operator training, and local conditions.

Be careful with dry biomass, drought, wind, mulch, plastic, straw, and other flammable material. Electrical weed control can create fire-risk concerns in dry conditions, so Zasso guidance, local rules, and trained operator procedures must be followed.

Commercial availability, product configuration, regulations, and service support may vary by region. Zasso's public E.CITRUS page currently positions the equipment for Latin America, so non-LATAM customer conversations should confirm product availability and local compliance before making commitments.
