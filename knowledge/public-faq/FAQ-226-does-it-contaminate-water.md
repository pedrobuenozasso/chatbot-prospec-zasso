# Does it contaminate water?

## Short Answer

Zasso's electrical weeding does not contaminate water with herbicide active ingredients because the treatment does not apply a chemical herbicide. It uses controlled electrical energy as a physical mode of action, so there is no spray mixture, chemical drift, or herbicide residue to run off into surface water from the treatment itself. Customers should still manage near-water work carefully, because water can create electrical safety risks and local environmental rules may require buffers, site assessment, or product-specific operating limits.

## Detailed Answer

When customers ask whether Zasso contaminates water, the most important distinction is between chemical contamination and operational water-risk management. Chemical herbicides can enter surface water through runoff, leaching, sediment movement, spills, drift, or incorrect application. Zasso's technology does not work that way: it does not add a molecular herbicide active ingredient to the field, so the treatment itself does not introduce herbicide residues into ditches, drains, streams, ponds, lakes, irrigation channels, or other surface-water features.

That makes Zasso a useful option where customers want to reduce chemical load, avoid spray drift, and lower the risk of herbicide movement from treated vegetation or soil into nearby water. In suitable applications, the water-quality advantage is strongest when the comparison is with chemical weed control, especially where runoff, leaching, buffer zones, public access, or sensitive sites are important concerns.

The answer should not be overstated. "Does not contaminate water with herbicides" is not the same as "can be used in any wet or aquatic environment" or "has zero water-related risk." Zasso equipment uses high-voltage electricity and should not be operated in standing water, active irrigation, waterlogged ground, or near wet conductive infrastructure unless the applicable product manual and trained site procedure explicitly allow it. Treated biomass, soil particles, dust, fuel, lubricants, equipment maintenance, erosion, or site runoff are still normal field-management topics and should be handled through good operating practice.

For customer-facing use, the safest statement is that Zasso does not add chemical herbicide contaminants to water, while near-water operations still require conservative safety controls, site assessment, trained operators, and compliance with local environmental and water-protection requirements.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, Zasso can help reduce one major pathway of water contamination: the movement of herbicide active ingredients or metabolites from weed-control operations into surface water. This can be relevant for farms, municipalities, vineyards, orchards, public areas, organic systems, and sensitive sites where chemical drift, runoff, leaching, or water-body buffers are a concern.

Customers should still plan near-water work carefully. Before operating, they should identify ditches, drains, streams, ponds, canals, irrigation systems, wet ground, public access points, and sensitive habitats. If water is inside or close to the working area, the operation should follow the product manual, trained operator procedure, local regulation, and any site-specific environmental controls.

"Zasso does not apply a chemical herbicide, so the treatment itself does not add herbicide active ingredients or chemical residues to nearby water."

"That is different from saying water can be ignored: near-water work still needs trained operation, the correct product procedure, and local environmental controls."

"For customers concerned about runoff, leaching, or spray drift, Zasso can be a useful non-chemical option in suitable applications."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso has zero environmental impact, that it can be operated in or directly beside water without restrictions, or that it eliminates all water-protection obligations. Water can create electrical safety risks, change current paths, affect traction and soil conditions, and trigger local buffer or access requirements.

Do not use this FAQ to answer groundwater contamination, aquatic-life effects, wetlands use, food-residue compliance, organic certification, or site-specific environmental permitting. Those topics need their own evidence and local review.

Avoid disclosing confidential enclosure ratings, insulation ratings, water-ingress specifications, sensor thresholds, incident history, customer sites, or unpublished water-related test data unless Zasso has approved the information for public use.
