# What does a successful application look like after 1 day, 7 days, 14 days, and 30 days?

## Short Answer

A successful Zasso application should show a clear progression: early wilting or loss of vigor after 1 day, stronger dry-down and collapse by about 7 days, reduced regrowth pressure by about 14 days, and durable suppression or a clear need for follow-up by about 30 days. These are practical observation windows, not guaranteed deadlines. The exact timing depends on weed species, growth stage, moisture, soil conductivity, contact quality, application speed, dose, and whether the target plant has protected regrowth structures.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding starts working during application, when electrical energy moves through the contacted plant and the plant-soil circuit. A good result is not defined only by an instant visual effect. It is better judged as a sequence of symptoms and follow-up checks that show whether the plant has been injured deeply enough to dry down and avoid recovery.

After about 1 day, a successful application often shows visible stress: wilting, loss of turgor, darkening, tissue collapse, bending stems, or early browning. Some plants show symptoms faster, while others need longer before damage is obvious. At this stage, the main question is whether the electrodes made good contact and delivered enough energy to the target plants.

After about 7 days, successful treatment should usually show more advanced dry-down. Leaves and stems may be browned, brittle, collapsed, or clearly losing biomass. In a demonstration or customer trial, this is a useful moment for photos and side-by-side comparison with untreated areas. However, 7 days may still be too early to confirm final control in perennial weeds, grasses, woody plants, dense stands, or plants with large crowns, rhizomes, tubers, or other recovery tissues.

After about 14 days, the assessment should shift from visible injury to regrowth risk. A strong result shows limited new green growth, continued desiccation, and lower competitive pressure from treated weeds. If new shoots are appearing from crowns or underground reserves, that does not necessarily mean the treatment failed, but it may indicate that operating speed, contact quality, timing, dose, target size, or the number of passes should be reviewed.

After about 30 days, a successful application should show durable suppression in the treated strip or area under the conditions tested. This is the point where customers can better judge whether the application reduced weed pressure long enough to meet the agronomic or operational objective. If regrowth is present, the 30-day check helps decide whether a second pass, earlier treatment timing, mowing strategy, different applicator setup, or integration with other weed-management practices is needed.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should plan follow-up observations before the application starts. Good trial practice is to take photos and notes at treatment, then revisit the site around 1, 7, 14, and 30 days when practical. This gives a fairer view than judging only from the moment the machine passes.

The timeline also helps set realistic expectations. Zasso treatment can cause fast internal injury, but commercial success is about reduced recovery and lower weed pressure over time. A plant that looks injured after 1 day but regrows strongly by 30 days may need a different protocol. A plant that looks progressively drier at 7 and 14 days, with limited regrowth at 30 days, is a much stronger sign of successful control.

For demonstrations, these observation windows should be adapted to the crop, weed, weather, season, and operating objective. Annual weeds may be assessed sooner. Perennial grasses, woody weeds, large plants, or weeds with underground reserves often need longer monitoring and may require repeat applications as part of an integrated weed-management program.

## Safe Sales Wording

"For a good application, we look for progression: visible stress after the first day, stronger dry-down after about a week, limited regrowth around two weeks, and durable suppression or a clear follow-up decision by about a month."

"The exact timeline depends on the weed, soil and moisture conditions, contact quality, speed, and operating setup, so we prefer planned follow-up photos instead of judging only from the first pass."

"A strong Zasso result is not just surface browning; it is reduced recovery over the following days and weeks under the right application conditions."

## Caveats

Do not present 1, 7, 14, and 30 days as guaranteed biological deadlines. They are practical observation windows. Different weeds, crops, soils, seasons, and machine setups can shift the visible response and the regrowth window.

Do not promise one-pass control, complete root kill, no regrowth, or the same result across all conditions. Perennial weeds, grasses, woody plants, dense infestations, large plants, or weeds with underground reserves may require longer monitoring, better timing, adjusted speed/contact settings, or repeat passes.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, customer-specific trial data, unpublished telemetry, proprietary control logic, pricing, certification plans, or private customer details.
