# Is the efficacy comparable to herbicides?

## Short Answer

Zasso's efficacy can be comparable to herbicides in selected, well-matched applications, but it should not be presented as universally equivalent to every herbicide program. Public field evidence and internal Zasso materials show that electrical weeding can achieve strong control in specific crops and weed situations, including some trials where results were similar to herbicide treatments. The right comparison depends on the target weed, crop, growth stage, contact quality, soil and moisture conditions, operating speed, pass number, safety constraints, local regulation, and whether the customer needs residual control or a non-chemical physical treatment.

## Detailed Answer

Herbicides are a broad category, and their performance varies by active ingredient, mode of action, weed species, resistance status, weather, timing, label restrictions, and whether the treatment has residual soil activity. Zasso's Electroherb technology is different: it uses controlled electrical energy to damage the treated plant through a physical, non-chemical mode of action. That means Zasso can be highly effective where the target weeds can be contacted properly and enough useful energy is delivered, but it does not behave like a residual herbicide and should not be described as a blanket replacement for all chemical programs.

The most credible customer-facing answer is that Zasso can match herbicide-like control in some validated use cases, especially where the job is to treat emerged weeds, herbicide-resistant survivors, organic production weeds, field edges, inter-row targets, or situations where chemical use is restricted or undesirable. In other cases, herbicides may still be faster, cheaper per hectare, more selective, or able to provide residual pre-emergence control that electrical weeding does not provide.

For sales and technical discussions, the comparison should be made against the customer's real alternative: a specific herbicide program, rescue spray, mowing pass, cultivation pass, hand weeding, desiccation treatment, or integrated weed-management program. Zasso is strongest when the customer values non-chemical control, residue avoidance, reduced soil disturbance, resistance management, organic compatibility where approved, or treatment of escapes where another herbicide mode of action is not enough.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not ask only whether Zasso is "as good as herbicide" in the abstract. They should ask whether Zasso can deliver the required level of control for their crop, weed spectrum, timing, safety envelope, operating window, and business case.

A good comparison trial should define the herbicide benchmark, target weeds, weed stage, crop stage, treatment timing, soil and moisture conditions, speed, pass number, control rating dates, regrowth, crop response, cost, labor, fuel or energy use, and any residue, drift, re-entry, withholding, or certification constraints. In suitable use cases, Zasso can be a strong herbicide alternative or complement; in others, the best program may combine Zasso with chemical, mechanical, cultural, or scouting-based tactics.

## Evidence and Context

GitHub repository research reviewed FAQ-060 and FAQ-061, adjacent efficacy and power-delivery FAQ materials, soybean herbicide-resistance customer-profile material, and Zasso white-paper notes on organic soybean and inter-row field trials. These sources consistently support conditional language: electrical weed control can deliver strong control, but results depend on field conditions, operating settings, contact, crop geometry, and program design.

The GitHub white paper on organic soybean summarizes peer-reviewed Brazilian field research where electrical discharge controlled inter-row weeds in organic no-till soybean. Under the reported conditions, the strongest setting reached about 90% control at 20 days in one experiment and 100% control at 20 days in another, with the important caveat that the paper did not include a direct chemical herbicide treatment and used historical Eletroherb equipment rather than current Zasso machines.

The GitHub inter-row weed-control field-trial note reports research comparing electric weed control, targeted tillage, and post-emergent herbicide in wheat inter-row management. It found comparable performance among in-crop electric, tillage, and herbicide tactics in the tested context, while also warning that the studied electric setup was not purpose-built for that in-crop use and that more field work is needed for dedicated inter-row units.

SharePoint research reviewed Zasso's Chapter 09 material on publications about electrical-weeding efficacy. It summarizes field evidence across cotton, hazelnut, potato, sugar beet, soybean, and highbush blueberry. The material includes examples where electrical weeding reduced biomass, seed viability, or regrowth and, in some contexts, performed comparably to herbicide or mechanical treatments; it also emphasizes power, speed, pass number, species response, and integration with other methods.

## Safe Sales Wording

"In selected, validated applications, Zasso can deliver weed control that is comparable to herbicide treatments, but the right comparison depends on the target weeds, crop, timing, operating conditions, and treatment objective."

"Zasso is best presented as a non-chemical physical control tool that can replace certain herbicide applications or complement herbicide programs where conditions and regulations allow."

"We recommend comparing Zasso against the customer's real benchmark in a field-specific protocol, including control, regrowth, crop response, cost, safety, and operational constraints."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is always equal or superior to herbicides, replaces every herbicide pass, provides residual pre-emergence control, guarantees complete kill, or works the same across all crops, weeds, soils, climates, and products. Avoid direct percentage claims unless the crop, weed, method, timing, and source are named.

Herbicide comparisons are especially sensitive because herbicides vary widely in selectivity, residual activity, cost, speed, regulation, resistance status, drift risk, re-entry intervals, withholding periods, and crop safety. Zasso comparisons should be specific to the customer's field and should include local regulatory and safety requirements.

Do not disclose confidential customer results, unpublished trial data, product settings, telemetry details, pricing, certification timelines, contract details, or private meeting content unless explicitly approved for external use.
