# Does mowing before treatment improve results?

## Short Answer

Yes, mowing before Zasso treatment can improve results when it helps the electrodes reach the target weeds more evenly. This is most relevant in tall, dense, tangled, or lodged vegetation where upper growth shields lower stems, smaller weeds, or regrowth points from contact. Mowing is not automatically helpful in every field: if it removes too much living, connected plant tissue or leaves dry residue that blocks contact, it can reduce treatment quality or increase operational risk.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology depends on physical contact between the applicator and living target vegetation. Electrical energy must enter the plant, move through conductive tissue, and interact with the plant-soil return circuit. Mowing changes that target by reducing height, opening the canopy, and changing how much living tissue remains available for contact.

Mowing before treatment can be useful when vegetation is too tall, dense, tangled, or uneven for reliable electrode contact. In those cases, mowing can reduce shielding, expose lower stems and new growth, and make the electrical pass more consistent. This is why pre-mowing can be part of a good integrated program for dense perennial weeds, grasses, or high-biomass stands, especially where a direct electrical pass would otherwise ride over the canopy or miss the lower plant structure.

However, mowing is not a universal requirement and should not be treated as a guarantee. If mowing is too low, too close to treatment, or performed under dry conditions, it can remove the upright conductive tissue needed for effective contact, leave loose residue between the electrodes and the weed, or create a field condition that is harder and less safe to treat. For perennial weeds, the best timing may be to mow, allow suitable regrowth, and then apply Zasso when the regrowth is green, reachable, and connected to the plant's recovery structures.

The practical answer is therefore conditional: mowing before treatment can improve results when it improves contact with living target vegetation and is matched to the crop, weed species, growth stage, mowing height, residue level, soil and plant moisture, applicator setup, safety conditions, and follow-up plan.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should consider mowing before Zasso treatment when weeds are tall, dense, lodged, or shielding each other. The goal is not simply to cut the weeds short; the goal is to prepare the stand so the electrodes can contact the right living tissue more consistently.

After mowing, the field should be checked before electrical treatment. Useful questions include: Are the target weeds still visible and reachable? Is there enough green, connected tissue for contact? Is loose residue blocking the electrodes? Are crop stems, irrigation lines, mulch, or dry biomass creating safety or contact concerns? Is regrowth timing appropriate for the target species?

For annual weeds, mowing may help reduce biomass and improve uniformity where the stand is very dense. For grasses, rhizomatous weeds, and perennials, mowing may need to be paired with regrowth timing or sequential electrical passes. Local validation remains important because the best mowing-plus-Zasso strategy can vary by crop, product configuration, weed biology, soil condition, climate, and operating objective.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Mowing before Zasso treatment can improve results when it opens dense vegetation and helps the electrodes contact living weeds more consistently. We treat it as a field-preparation option, not as a universal requirement."

"The key is to leave enough green, connected plant tissue for the electrical treatment to work. If mowing leaves only dry residue or very short stubble, the treatment plan may need different timing or a follow-up pass."

"For tall, dense, grassy, or perennial weeds, mowing and Zasso should be planned together with regrowth timing, residue management, speed, safety conditions, and follow-up scouting."

## Caveats

Do not claim that mowing before Zasso always improves results. Mowing can help when it improves electrode contact, but it can also reduce performance if it removes too much living conductive tissue, creates loose residue, exposes dry biomass, or leaves weeds too short to contact effectively.

Do not promise one-pass control, guaranteed root kill, or a universal mowing height, waiting period, speed, pass number, or treatment interval. Recommendations must remain product-, crop-, weed-, region-, and field-specific.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, certification timelines, internal safety analysis, control logic, or private meeting content.
