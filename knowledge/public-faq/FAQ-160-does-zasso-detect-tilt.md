# Does Zasso detect tilt?

## Short Answer

Based on current Zasso materials, tilt sensing is part of Zasso's safety and control know-how for relevant equipment, but it should not be described as a universal feature on every product or configuration. Where fitted and approved for a specific machine, tilt or inclination detection can help inhibit or stop high-voltage operation when the equipment is outside its intended operating position or stability envelope. Customers should always follow the product-specific manual, training, terrain limits, alarms, and restart procedure.

## Detailed Answer

Tilt detection is best explained as one possible layer in a broader safety architecture. Zasso equipment may operate on uneven terrain, slopes, headlands, terraces, orchards, vineyards, roadsides, or other sites where machine angle, applicator position, electrode contact, and operator control can change quickly. In those situations, excessive tilt can matter for two reasons: it may affect safe machine operation, and it may affect whether applicators maintain the intended contact with target vegetation and soil.

In customer-facing language, tilt sensing can be used to monitor whether the machine, applicator, or relevant assembly is within a defined operating range. If the detected angle is outside the approved range, the control system may warn the operator, inhibit high voltage, stop treatment output, or require a controlled reset, depending on the product design. This should be framed as a safety and operating-control function, not as a guarantee that the machine cannot tip, slide, lose contact, or be used safely on any slope.

Tilt detection also does not replace basic machine-safety practice. The operator still needs to assess terrain, soil condition, traction, obstacles, crop layout, turning areas, implement stability, tractor configuration, ballast, speed, and weather before operation. Steep, wet, unstable, rutted, terraced, or obstacle-rich terrain may require slower operation, a different direction of travel, local validation, a site-specific work plan, or a decision not to treat that area.

The safest sales answer is that Zasso can include tilt or inclination detection in relevant systems, but the exact capability, threshold, alarm behavior, high-voltage inhibit behavior, reset rule, and approved slope limit are product-specific and must come from the approved manual, training, and Zasso technical documentation for that machine.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat tilt detection as an additional safeguard and operating aid, not as permission to work beyond the limits of the tractor, implement, applicator, site, or manual. If a tilt-related alarm or shutdown occurs, the operator should stop, keep the area controlled, understand the cause, and restart only when the approved procedure allows it.

For farms, contractors, municipalities, and demonstration teams, this means slope and uneven ground should be included in the pre-job risk assessment. The team should confirm that the equipment can maintain stable travel, reliable applicator contact, safe turning, controlled access, and compliance with the product manual. Where the site is marginal, a local pilot or technical review is more responsible than a broad claim that the machine can operate on all slopes.

Tilt sensing may also help with operational discipline: it can turn an unsafe or out-of-position condition into an alarm, inhibit, or stop event. However, the customer's practical control remains the same: trained operation, terrain assessment, safety distance, inspection, maintenance, and manual-based restart behavior.

## Caveats

Do not claim that every Zasso product has tilt detection, that tilt detection prevents rollovers, or that it makes operation safe on all slopes. Do not publish tilt thresholds, slope limits, sensor type, sensor location, software logic, response time, safety rating, certification basis, alarm codes, validation data, or reset behavior unless that language is already approved for external use.

Tilt detection depends on product configuration, sensor health, calibration, mounting position, terrain, soil condition, traction, speed, turning behavior, tractor and implement setup, ballast, crop layout, obstacles, maintenance state, weather, operator response, and local regulation. The approved product manual and training always take priority over this general FAQ.

Avoid using this FAQ as legal, certification, or machinery-stability advice. Customers needing numerical operating limits should use product-approved documentation or request a Zasso technical review for the specific machine and site.
