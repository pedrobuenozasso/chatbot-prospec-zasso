# Can Zasso be used in herbicide-resistant areas?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can be used in herbicide-resistant areas when the target weeds can be contacted effectively and the application fits the crop, site, and operating conditions. Herbicide resistance affects a weed's response to chemical modes of action; Zasso's Electroherb technology uses a physical electrical mode of action instead. That makes it a credible non-chemical option in resistant-weed programs, but it should be positioned as part of integrated weed management rather than as a universal one-pass replacement for herbicides.

## Detailed Answer

Herbicide-resistant areas are places where repeated herbicide use has selected weed populations that are no longer controlled reliably by one or more chemical modes of action. In those situations, growers and land managers usually need more diversity in their weed-control program, not just more of the same chemistry.

Zasso can fit these areas because it does not rely on the biochemical pathway that resistant weeds have adapted to survive. Electroherb applies controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation, damaging plant tissues physically and disrupting the plant's ability to function and regrow. In practical terms, that means herbicide resistance does not automatically make a weed tolerant to electrical treatment.

That said, resistant areas are not all the same. Results still depend on the weed species present, whether the problem is annual or perennial, plant size and architecture, moisture, density, contact quality, travel speed, number of passes, operator training, and the broader management plan. Mature grasses, dense infestations, deep-rooted perennials, or shielded weeds may still require slower operation, repeat passes, mowing before treatment, or combination with other tactics.

The safest commercial position is that Zasso is well suited for resistant-weed areas where customers need a non-chemical mode of action, want to reduce repeated herbicide dependence, or need another tool for escapes, fence lines, perennial crops, non-crop zones, and integrated weed-management programs. It should not be sold as meaning chemistry is no longer needed everywhere.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers dealing with herbicide resistance, Zasso can add a different control layer without relying on the chemistry that has already lost performance. This can help reduce repeated exposure to the same herbicide groups, manage escapes, support residue-conscious programs, and diversify control options in places where resistance pressure is already high.

A good customer validation plan should identify the resistant weed species, growth stage, density, crop or site constraints, application speed, number of passes, moisture conditions, and the real comparison the customer cares about: another herbicide pass, mowing, cultivation, hand removal, or letting escapes set seed. The strongest fit is where Zasso becomes one useful part of a broader integrated program.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can be used in herbicide-resistant areas because it adds a physical electrical mode of action instead of relying on the chemistry the weeds may already resist."

"The strongest fit is as part of integrated weed management, especially where customers want to reduce repeated herbicide dependence, manage escapes, or add a non-chemical control layer."

"Performance still depends on weed species, growth stage, contact quality, speed, moisture, density, and follow-up management, so resistant-area claims should be validated in the customer's field conditions."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso solves every resistant-weed problem, eliminates the need for herbicides everywhere, or guarantees complete control in one pass. Herbicide resistance does not automatically protect weeds from electrical treatment, but biology and operating conditions still determine results.

Be especially careful with dense mature grasses, deep-rooted perennials, shielded weeds, heavy regrowth pressure, and sites where a single tactic will not be enough. In some resistant areas, the most credible recommendation will still include repeat passes, mowing, seed-production control, scouting, sanitation, or remaining effective herbicides where allowed.

Do not disclose confidential field results, raw trial data, internal machine settings, product-roadmap details, private customer names, pricing, or private meeting content.
