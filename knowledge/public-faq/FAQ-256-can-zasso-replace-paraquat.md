# Can Zasso replace paraquat?

## Short Answer

Sometimes, yes, but only in defined use cases. Zasso can replace selected paraquat applications with a non-chemical electrical treatment when direct plant contact is practical, the target vegetation is suitable, and the customer values avoiding the handling, residue, drift, certification, or regulatory burden associated with paraquat. It should not be sold as a universal one-for-one replacement for every paraquat use, especially where customers depend on very fast, broad-area burndown or crop-desiccation timing that still needs local validation.

## Detailed Answer

Paraquat is valued because it is fast, non-selective at the treatment point, and useful for burndown, desiccation, and vegetation knockdown in a range of systems. But paraquat is also a highly restricted or prohibited herbicide in many settings, and its practical use can carry major training, handling, worker-safety, and regulatory burdens.

Zasso addresses the same commercial problem from a different direction. Electroherb uses controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation instead of a sprayed herbicide active ingredient. That means Zasso can be a credible substitute for some paraquat jobs where growers or land managers want non-chemical, broad-spectrum vegetation control and where the application geometry allows reliable electrode contact.

The strongest fit is usually not "replace paraquat everywhere." It is "replace the paraquat passes that are hardest to justify or sustain." That may include directed applications in orchards, vineyards, berries, nuts, plantations, and non-crop zones; resistant-weed patches; field margins; organic or residue-sensitive programs; markets where paraquat is banned; and customers that want to lower worker-exposure, certification, or public-perception risk around a highly hazardous herbicide.

At the same time, paraquat and electrical treatment are not agronomically identical. Paraquat is a contact herbicide with fast visible injury and established workflows in some regions. Zasso depends on contact quality, weed architecture, plant size, moisture conditions, speed, pass setup, operator training, and safety management. Very large-area burndown, some desiccation or harvest-aid uses, dense regrowth, and applications where customers expect paraquat-like speed and simplicity still require careful local validation before promising substitution.

So the safe answer is yes for selected paraquat applications in suitable conditions, but no for a blanket claim that Zasso universally replaces paraquat as a category.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical question is not "Can Zasso do everything paraquat does?" It is "Which paraquat application is causing the most pain, risk, or limitation, and can Zasso replace that specific pass?"

That framing matters commercially. In some countries, paraquat is already prohibited, so the real issue is not herbicide choice but whether Zasso can provide a usable non-chemical substitute for the target job. In other markets, paraquat remains legal but highly controlled, which means customers may value a replacement not only because of weed control, but because it can reduce restricted-use handling burdens, training constraints, and the reputational sensitivity of using a highly hazardous herbicide.

Customers should still evaluate Zasso against the real field objective: top-kill speed, regrowth, root impact, seed-set prevention, harvest timing, crop safety, work rate, fire-risk conditions, and cost per treated area. In some cases Zasso may replace the paraquat pass directly. In others it may work better as part of an integrated program that reduces, rather than fully eliminates, chemical dependence.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can replace selected paraquat applications in suitable use cases, especially where customers want a non-chemical option or need to move away from a highly restricted herbicide."

"The right commercial question is which paraquat pass Zasso can credibly replace for this customer, not whether paraquat disappears everywhere at once."

"Where paraquat is banned, highly restricted, residue-sensitive, or commercially sensitive, Zasso can be evaluated as a practical physical alternative, subject to local validation of performance, speed, and operating fit."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is a universal one-for-one replacement for paraquat across all crops, weeds, timings, desiccation uses, or countries. That is not supported.

Do not imply that Zasso always matches paraquat on speed, simplicity, work rate, cost per hectare, or visible top-kill timing. Those points must be validated in the real use case.

Be especially careful with harvest-aid or crop-desiccation claims, dense infestations, large broad-acre burndown expectations, and customers who need very fast operational turnaround. Those may require dedicated local trials before making direct substitution claims.

Do not overstate regulatory simplification. Zasso avoids applying paraquat during the treatment itself, but it still requires trained operation, electrical-safety discipline, and fire-risk management under dry conditions.
