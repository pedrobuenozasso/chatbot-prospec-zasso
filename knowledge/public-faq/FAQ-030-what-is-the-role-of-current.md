# What is the role of current?

## Short Answer

Current is the actual flow of electrical charge through the weed and the surrounding plant-soil circuit. It matters because plant injury occurs when enough useful electrical energy passes through plant tissues for long enough to damage cells, vascular pathways, crowns, and recovery structures. However, more current is not automatically better: effective control depends on where the current flows, how long it flows, how power is controlled, how good the electrode contact is, and whether soil and plant conditions help or divert the current path.

## Detailed Answer

In electrical weeding, voltage helps establish the electrical path, but current is the flow that carries energy through that path. Once Zasso applicators contact target vegetation and the circuit closes through the plant-soil system, current can pass through stems, leaves, crowns, roots, nearby soil, and the return path. The resistance of plant tissue converts part of that electrical energy into heat and other cellular stress, which can disrupt membranes, water transport, vascular function, and the plant's ability to recover.

The important question is not simply "how much current does the machine produce?" It is "how much useful current passes through the target plant tissues rather than around them?" Current can be split across many weeds in dense vegetation, diverted into wet or conductive soil, reduced by poor electrode contact, blocked by residue or shielding, or concentrated in ways that increase safety or fire risk without improving weed control. A system therefore needs controlled power delivery, suitable applicator contact, appropriate speed, and trained operation so current is directed through the biological target rather than wasted in easier bypass paths.

Current also changes during treatment. Plant and soil impedance are variable, and plant tissue can become more conductive or less continuous as it is damaged. That is why Zasso should be presented as a controlled power-delivery technology, not as a fixed-current or brute-force approach. The practical goal is to deliver enough useful energy through the plant-soil circuit to achieve the treatment objective while staying within the product's safety architecture and operating guidance.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should think of current as part of dose. The biological effect depends on current, voltage, power, contact time, electrode contact, travel speed, plant size, plant water content, root structure, soil moisture, soil conductivity, and weed density. Good results come from matching the machine and operating window to the field conditions, not from chasing a single headline current value.

This also explains why application guidance matters. In a sparse stand with good contact, useful current may pass through each target weed more effectively. In dense or wet conditions, current may divide across multiple paths or bypass roots, reducing the energy that reaches each plant. Follow-up scouting remains important because visible leaf damage does not always prove that enough current and energy reached the crown or root system to prevent regrowth.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Current is the flow of electricity through the weed and plant-soil circuit. Zasso's goal is not just to create current, but to direct controlled energy through the target plant where it can cause useful biological damage."

"A higher current number by itself does not guarantee better weed control. What matters is where the current goes, how long contact lasts, how power is controlled, and whether field conditions help the energy reach the target tissues."

"Current should be discussed together with voltage, power, contact time, electrode contact, soil condition, weed stage, and safety procedures."

## Caveats

Do not claim that all current passes through the plant, that higher current always improves control, or that a specific current level is universally ideal. Current follows available conductive paths, and some energy can be diverted through soil, residue, bare-ground contact, other plants, or fault paths.

Do not promise guaranteed root kill, one-pass control, or universal performance. The outcome depends on plant species, plant size, water content, root architecture, weed density, soil moisture, soil conductivity, electrode contact, travel speed, power delivery, machine setup, and operator training.

Do not disclose confidential current limits, module settings, control algorithms, fault thresholds, certification details, customer-specific results, pricing, or unpublished engineering information.
