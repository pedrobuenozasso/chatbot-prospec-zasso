# What happens if the electrodes contact buried metal?

## Short Answer

If energized Zasso electrodes contact buried metal, the operator should treat it as an abnormal and potentially unsafe condition. Buried metal such as pipes, cables, rods, anchors, scrap, rebar, grounding conductors, or other conductive infrastructure can create an unintended current path and may make nearby metal, soil, or wet areas unsafe to touch. The safe response is to stop high-voltage output, keep people and animals away, avoid touching or digging around the object, and restart only after the equipment and site have been checked according to the approved manual, training, and local utility-safety procedures.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso electrical weeding is designed to deliver controlled high-voltage energy through applicators or electrodes into target plants and the intended plant-soil pathway. Buried metal can change that pathway. Unlike a visible fence wire or irrigation component, buried metal may be unknown before treatment and may connect to a larger conductive system underground or to nearby structures.

Examples can include buried irrigation pipe, drainage pipe, utility conduit, grounding rods, metal stakes, anchors, trellis foundations, rebar, old wire, scrap metal, abandoned infrastructure, or other conductive material. If an energized electrode contacts or strongly couples with buried metal, the outcome depends on the product, applicator design, object size and depth, grounding, soil moisture, soil conductivity, vegetation contact, insulation condition, available energy, and protective-system response. Possible outcomes include alarm or shutdown behavior, loss of treatment quality, arcing or localized heating, damage to electrodes or underground infrastructure, an unintended current path, touch-voltage risk on connected metal, or step-voltage risk near the contact area.

The conservative customer-facing rule is simple: buried metal should not be treated as a normal target. If buried metal is known or suspected, operators should avoid the area or follow the site-specific plan before applying high voltage. If contact is suspected during operation, stop high-voltage output using the approved controls, keep people and animals outside the safety zone, and do not touch the electrodes, cables, tractor, wet vegetation, exposed metal, ground around the object, or any connected infrastructure until the system has been shut down and confirmed safe by trained personnel. If the buried object may be a utility, pipe, grounding system, or other infrastructure, local owner and utility procedures should take priority.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should identify and manage buried metal risks before treatment where practical. This matters in orchards, vineyards, municipal areas, industrial sites, roadsides, rail corridors, old farms, renovated fields, fields with irrigation or drainage networks, and areas near buildings, pumps, utilities, fences, trellis systems, or grounding installations.

Good worksite planning can include reviewing site maps, asking the landowner about known buried infrastructure, marking sensitive zones, avoiding utility corridors, and using local locate-before-you-dig or infrastructure-identification processes where relevant. Operators should be trained to stop if they see signs of abnormal contact, repeated alarms, arcing, unusual noise, unexpected machine behavior, damaged electrodes, exposed metal, or disturbed soil suggesting buried objects.

For customers, this is part of using high-voltage field equipment professionally. Zasso technology should be operated with trained users, exclusion zones, pre-work inspection, site planning, monitoring, shutdown discipline, maintenance, and product-specific manuals. Where buried infrastructure may exist, the safest commercial answer is to identify it, avoid it, and follow the approved procedure rather than trying to treat through it.

## Caveats

Do not say that contact with buried metal is harmless, that protective systems make every contact automatically safe, or that the machine can always continue operating normally. The risk depends on the product, applicator, buried object's size, depth, material, grounding, connection to other infrastructure, soil moisture, soil conductivity, vegetation contact, insulation condition, machine state, and protective response.

Do not provide improvised rescue, digging, utility-location, electrical repair, restart, or troubleshooting instructions beyond the approved Zasso manual, local site procedure, and qualified utility or electrical guidance. If there is arcing, smoke, fire, shock, equipment damage, a broken electrode, damaged cable, exposed insulation, repeated alarms, or suspected contact with utilities or buried conductive infrastructure, stop operation, keep others away, and follow the product-specific emergency and service process.

Do not disclose confidential voltage, current, power, pulse, frequency, grounding, bonding, insulation-monitoring, alarm, sensor, shutdown-threshold, discharge-time, firmware, certification, incident, customer, supplier, warranty, or test-result details unless already approved for public use.
