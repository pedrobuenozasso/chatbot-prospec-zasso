# Does it harm aquatic life?

## Short Answer

Zasso's electrical weeding does not apply herbicide active ingredients, surfactants, or chemical residues that would wash into water and expose aquatic life after treatment. That can reduce an important aquatic-risk pathway compared with chemical weed control in suitable terrestrial applications. However, Zasso should not be described as automatically harmless to aquatic life, and standard equipment should not be treated as an aquatic weed-control tool; work near water requires trained operation, buffers where required, and local environmental review.

## Detailed Answer

Aquatic-life risk is usually discussed in two different ways: chemical exposure and operational impact. Zasso's Electroherb technology is a physical, contact-based weed-control method. It uses electrical energy delivered through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit rather than applying a chemical herbicide to plants, soil, or water. As a result, the treatment itself does not create a herbicide-residue pathway into streams, ponds, canals, wetlands, lakes, drainage ditches, or groundwater-fed aquatic habitats.

This is a meaningful customer benefit when the comparison is herbicide-based weed control. Herbicides can reach water through drift, runoff, leaching, sediment movement, spills, or incorrect use. Once present in water at relevant concentrations, herbicides may affect aquatic plants directly and may indirectly affect fish, amphibians, invertebrates, and wider aquatic food webs by changing habitat or food availability. By avoiding herbicide chemistry, Zasso can help reduce chemical load and residue-related exposure concerns in suitable applications.

The responsible answer is still not an absolute "no harm" claim. Aquatic habitats are sensitive, and high-voltage equipment creates specific safety and control issues around standing water, saturated ground, drainage channels, wet infrastructure, and water edges. Direct electrical treatment in water can harm aquatic organisms; internal research context even shows that electricity can be biologically active in water under experimental conditions. That reinforces the need to keep customer claims carefully bounded.

For customer-facing use, the safest position is: Zasso can reduce chemical exposure pathways for aquatic life because it is non-chemical and residue-free, but operators should avoid treating in water or directly on aquatic habitat unless a specific product, protocol, approval, and local permit allow it. Near-water work should follow the product manual, trained operator procedure, site assessment, and applicable water-protection or biodiversity rules.

## What This Means for Customers

For farms, municipalities, orchards, vineyards, infrastructure sites, and public-area operators, Zasso may be attractive where chemical runoff, spray drift, residues, public perception, or water-body buffers are important concerns. It can be part of a program to reduce reliance on herbicides near sensitive areas, provided the application is terrestrial, controlled, and locally validated.

Customers should still map water features before treatment. Streams, ponds, canals, ditches, wetlands, irrigation channels, drains, wells, waterlogged soil, riparian vegetation, and protected habitats should be identified during planning. In sensitive areas, the operating plan should define no-treatment zones, access control, weather limits, emergency procedures, habitat protection, and any required regulatory approvals.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso does not apply herbicide chemistry, so the treatment itself does not add herbicide residues, surfactants, or active ingredients that can wash into water and expose aquatic life."

"Near water, we still treat the site conservatively: trained operators, product procedures, local buffers, and environmental rules all matter."

"Zasso can help reduce chemical pressure on sensitive aquatic environments in suitable terrestrial applications, but it should not be presented as unrestricted aquatic use."

## Caveats

Do not claim "no harm to aquatic life," "aquatic-safe," "zero environmental impact," or "safe for wetlands" as absolute statements. The stronger supported claim is narrower: Zasso avoids herbicide-residue exposure pathways from the treatment itself.

Do not imply that standard Zasso equipment can be operated in standing water, wetlands, drainage channels, rivers, ponds, lakes, or saturated ground without product-specific authorization, site review, trained procedure, and local regulatory approval.

Do not disclose confidential water-related tests, experimental setups, electrical parameters, product ratings, incident history, customer sites, or unpublished ecotoxicology data unless Zasso has approved the information for public release.
