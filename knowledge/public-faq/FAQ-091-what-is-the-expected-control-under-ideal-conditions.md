# What is the expected control under ideal conditions?

## Short Answer

Under ideal conditions, Zasso technology can deliver high weed control, and public field evidence includes results above 80% control in optimized electrical-weeding trials. The best results are expected when weeds are actively growing, accessible to the electrodes, at a manageable stage and density, and when the machine is operated at the right speed with good contact and trained setup. Even under ideal conditions, Zasso should not be sold as a guaranteed fixed percentage, because weed species, regrowth biology, soil conditions, crop constraints, pass strategy, and evaluation timing all influence the result.

## Detailed Answer

"Ideal conditions" means the electrical treatment has a good chance to deliver enough useful energy into the target plant and, where relevant, into the plant-soil pathway connected to its recovery tissues. In practice, this usually means good electrode contact, suitable application speed, adequate contact time, appropriate power delivery, manageable weed density, plants with enough moisture to conduct current, and soil conditions that allow a stable electrical path without creating avoidable safety or fire risk.

In these conditions, customers can reasonably expect strong visual control and meaningful biomass reduction. Public Oregon State University and Frontiers in Agronomy work in organic highbush blueberry reported more than 80% weed control under optimized slower-speed treatments, with biomass reduction up to 73% compared with untreated plots. Oregon State Extension also reports weed control up to 89% in its blueberry field studies at slower operating speeds. Zasso public vineyard validation has also reported more than 80% control in a tested vineyard context.

The responsible customer-facing message is therefore: ideal conditions can support high control, often in the 80%+ range in documented trial contexts, but the exact result must be validated for the specific crop, weed spectrum, equipment configuration, operating protocol, and commercial goal. A young, upright annual broadleaf weed with good electrode contact may respond differently from a mature grass, a deep-rooted perennial, a prostrate species, a dense mixed canopy, or a weed protected by crop architecture or residue.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should use ideal-condition results as a performance target, not as a universal guarantee. A good field protocol should define the target weed, growth stage, acceptable control threshold, application speed, pass number, soil and plant moisture, electrode setup, safety conditions, and evaluation timing.

Where the field matches the ideal profile, Zasso can be a strong non-chemical tool for reducing emerged weeds, lowering reliance on herbicides in suitable programs, and supporting integrated weed management. Where the field is less ideal, customers may need slower operation, mowing before treatment, a second pass, sequential applications, or integration with other weed-control tactics.

## Safe Sales Wording

"In ideal conditions, Zasso can deliver high weed control, with public trial evidence showing results above 80% in optimized use cases. We still validate the expected result for the customer's crop, weed spectrum, field condition, speed, contact quality, and treatment plan."

"The strongest results come when weeds are accessible, actively growing, at a suitable stage, and treated with the right applicator setup, speed, and operator protocol."

"We prefer to set a field-specific target rather than promise one fixed control percentage for every crop, weed, and season."

## Caveats

Do not promise guaranteed control, permanent control, complete root kill, one-pass success, or the same percentage across all weeds, crops, soils, seasons, and product configurations. Avoid presenting 80%+ public trial results as a universal Zasso guarantee; they are evidence of strong performance in specific optimized contexts.

Ideal conditions for efficacy must also remain compatible with safe operation. Do not recommend unsafe operation in rain, high-fire-risk conditions, unsuitable terrain, poor visibility, untrained operation, or outside the approved product manual and local regulatory requirements.
