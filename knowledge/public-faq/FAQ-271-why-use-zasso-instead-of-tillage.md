# Why use Zasso instead of tillage?

## Short Answer

Use Zasso instead of tillage when the goal is to control emerged weeds without moving, inverting, or loosening the soil. In suitable applications, Zasso can provide non-chemical weed control while preserving soil cover and reducing the soil-disturbance side effects that often come with tillage, such as erosion exposure, residue disruption, and disturbance around crop roots. The safe commercial position is not that Zasso replaces tillage in every situation, but that it can be a strong alternative when the customer wants weed control without a soil-working pass.

## Detailed Answer

Tillage is a powerful and familiar weed-management tool. It can sever, uproot, bury, or desiccate weeds, and in some systems it also helps with seedbed preparation, residue incorporation, crust breaking, or field leveling. Those functions matter, so tillage should not be treated as if it exists only for weed control.

The reason customers consider Zasso instead of tillage is that the two methods solve the weed problem differently. Tillage controls weeds by disturbing the soil. Zasso's Electroherb technology is designed to control contacted vegetation by sending controlled electrical energy through the plant and plant-soil circuit, without depending on cultivation, inversion, or soil mixing. That can make Zasso attractive where the customer wants to preserve soil structure, maintain surface cover, avoid bringing more buried weed seeds to the germination zone, reduce erosion exposure, or avoid repeated mechanical disturbance in permanent crops and conservation-oriented systems.

This difference is especially relevant in orchards, vineyards, citrus, berries, olives, coffee, and other systems where repeated under-row tillage can create operational and agronomic tradeoffs. If the customer's goal is simply weed suppression without soil movement, Zasso may offer a better fit than tillage. If the customer also needs seedbed preparation, residue incorporation, compaction management, or another true soil-working function, tillage may still have a role and Zasso may complement rather than replace it.

The safest customer-facing comparison is therefore practical: use tillage when soil work is genuinely needed; use Zasso when the main need is non-chemical weed control with less direct soil disturbance, subject to species, growth stage, residue, moisture, crop geometry, safety setup, and local validation.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, Zasso can be valuable where weed control is needed but soil movement is undesirable. That may include erosion-sensitive fields, no-till or reduced-till programs, perennial crops, shallow-rooted crops, residue-conserving systems, and areas where repeated cultivation would create extra labor or agronomic risk.

The decision should still be made against the customer's actual tillage pass. If the current pass is mainly a weed-control pass, Zasso may be a credible replacement candidate. If the pass also prepares the bed, incorporates amendments, breaks crusts, or resets the field physically, then the comparison is not one-to-one and Zasso should be positioned as a different tool, not a universal substitute.

Customers should also validate operating fit: target weeds, weed stage, soil and plant moisture, residue cover, stones, slope, equipment width, speed, regrowth pressure, crop proximity, and traffic management all matter. In some programs, the best answer may be to reduce selected tillage passes rather than eliminate tillage entirely.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Use Zasso instead of tillage when the customer wants non-chemical weed control without disturbing, inverting, or cultivating the soil."

"Zasso is often strongest where the current tillage pass is mainly for weed control and the customer wants to preserve soil cover, reduce erosion exposure, or avoid repeated soil disturbance."

"If the customer also needs true soil-working functions such as seedbed preparation or residue incorporation, tillage may still have a role and Zasso should be positioned as a complementary tool rather than a blanket replacement."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso always replaces tillage, eliminates erosion risk, improves soil health in every field, or delivers the same agronomic outcome as a soil-working implement. Tillage is sometimes used for more than weed control, and those extra functions may still matter.

Do not imply that Zasso is the right answer in every crop or soil condition. Performance depends on weed species, growth stage, residue, soil and plant moisture, speed, contact quality, crop geometry, fire-risk conditions, operator training, and safety protocols.

Do not overstate weed-seedbank claims. Reducing soil movement can help avoid some of the side effects associated with repeated tillage, but weed emergence patterns are field-specific and should be discussed carefully.
