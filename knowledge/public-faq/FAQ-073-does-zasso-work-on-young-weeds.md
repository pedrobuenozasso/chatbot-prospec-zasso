# Does Zasso work on young weeds?

## Short Answer

Yes. Zasso can work well on young weeds when they are already emerged, physically accessible, and contacted properly by the electrodes. Young weeds are often favorable targets because they usually have less biomass, thinner tissues, and smaller recovery reserves than mature plants. Results still depend on weed species, growth stage, weed height, density, crop or residue shielding, plant and soil moisture, electrode contact, application speed, pass strategy, and trained operation.

## Detailed Answer

Young weeds are generally a strong fit for electrical weeding, but the important word is emerged. Zasso's Electroherb technology acts on plants that are present and contacted during application. It is not a residual herbicide, so it does not prevent later germination from the seedbank and it does not control seedlings that have not emerged or cannot be reached by the applicator.

When young weeds are contactable, they can be easier to treat than mature weeds. Seedlings and early-stage weeds usually have smaller shoots, less lignified tissue, shallower or smaller root systems, and fewer stored reserves for regrowth. That means a suitable electrical treatment can damage the plant's shoot, vascular tissues, root-shoot connection, and recovery tissues before the weed has built a larger survival system.

There are still practical limits. Very small seedlings can be missed if they sit below the electrode contact zone, are hidden under crop residue or mulch, are shielded by a crop canopy, or are protected by taller neighboring weeds. Dense flushes can also divide contact and energy across many plants, and small grasses may be more parameter-sensitive than upright broadleaf seedlings because of narrow leaves, growing-point position, tillering, and contact geometry.

The safest customer-facing position is that young emerged weeds are often a preferred timing window for Zasso, provided the machine is set up and operated so the electrodes actually contact the target plants. For sales and agronomy discussions, this should be framed as early, non-chemical, post-emergence control with follow-up scouting, not as a pre-emergence or residual claim.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should consider Zasso when young weeds are visible, accessible, and likely to be contacted evenly by the applicator. Treating earlier can reduce the challenge of large biomass, deep reserves, dense shading, seed production, and difficult regrowth. It can also support programs that aim to reduce herbicide reliance, manage resistant weeds, avoid residues or drift, or operate in organic and integrated weed-management systems.

A good field plan should record the target species, weed stage, weed height, density, soil and plant moisture, residue or mulch cover, operating speed, pass number, and whether new seedlings emerge after treatment. If the field has a rolling emergence pattern, customers may need follow-up scouting and possibly a later pass because Zasso controls contacted emerged plants rather than future germination.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso provides pre-emergence or residual control. It does not stop future germination and does not control weeds that are not contacted during treatment.

Do not claim that all young weeds are easy. Very small seedlings, low-growing weeds, grasses with protected growing points, dense flushes, weeds under residue or mulch, and weeds shielded by crop canopy or taller vegetation may require adjusted timing, slower speed, better contact, repeat treatment, or integration with other weed-management practices.

Avoid universal height, speed, dose, or pass-number recommendations without product-specific and crop-specific guidance. Do not disclose confidential settings, control logic, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, telemetry details, certification status, or private meeting content.
