# Does the plant die immediately?

## Short Answer

Not always. Zasso treatment can produce visible injury quickly, sometimes within minutes to hours, but plant death is normally confirmed over hours, days, and in some cases weeks. Wilting, collapse, browning, or desiccation shortly after treatment are signs that the process is working; durable control depends on whether enough energy reaches the plant tissues that allow regrowth.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding is a fast physical treatment, but plant death is still a biological process. When the electrode contacts the weed, electrical current can move through the plant and the plant-soil circuit. The plant's resistance converts part of that energy into heat and internal stress, damaging cells, membranes, water transport tissues, and sometimes the crown or root-zone tissues that support recovery.

Some effects can appear quickly. A treated plant may wilt, darken, collapse, or lose turgor soon after application, especially where contact is good and the target plant is conductive and actively growing. However, the final result is not always instant. The plant may continue to dry down over the next several hours or days, and in perennial weeds or grasses the important question is whether protected growing points, rhizomes, crowns, tubers, or roots remain viable enough to regrow.

For that reason, Zasso should not describe the treatment as an instant-death process. A better customer explanation is that the treatment causes immediate internal injury, while visible dry-down and confirmed mortality develop over time. Follow-up assessment is especially important where weeds are large, dense, woody, waxy, dry, shielded by other plants, or supported by large underground reserves.

A practical field assessment should separate early symptoms from final control. Early symptoms show that energy was delivered. Longer-term observation shows whether the application was strong enough to suppress regrowth. Depending on species, growth stage, moisture, soil conductivity, contact quality, application speed, dose, and operator setup, a second pass or different timing may be needed.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should expect fast visible effects in many suitable applications, but they should judge success over an appropriate observation window rather than immediately after the machine passes. Wilting or collapse shortly after treatment is encouraging, but the commercial value comes from reduced regrowth and lower weed pressure over subsequent days and weeks.

This matters for operations and expectations. For small annual weeds, the field response may be rapid and straightforward. For perennial weeds, dense infestations, grasses, or plants with underground reserves, visible injury may occur before the grower knows whether the plant has lost its ability to recover.

The best operating message is to combine good agronomy with trained application: good electrode contact, suitable speed, correct applicator setup, appropriate target stage, and awareness of soil and plant moisture all influence how quickly symptoms appear and how durable the result will be.

## Caveats

Do not promise immediate plant death, complete root kill, or one-pass control in all conditions. Visible injury may appear quickly, but some plants can regrow if the treatment does not sufficiently affect crowns, roots, rhizomes, tubers, or other recovery tissues.

Do not define success only by what is visible immediately after treatment. Some plants need hours or days to show full dry-down, and perennial weeds may need longer observation or sequential applications.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, telemetry, customer-specific results, pricing, control algorithms, electrode design details, or certification plans.
