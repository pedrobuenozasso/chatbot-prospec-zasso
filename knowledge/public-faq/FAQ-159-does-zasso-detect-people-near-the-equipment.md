# Does Zasso detect people near the equipment?

## Short Answer

In some Zasso systems or configurations, people or animal presence detection may be used as part of the safety architecture, but it should not be described as a universal feature on every product. Customer-facing wording should say that Zasso uses layered safety controls and that presence detection, where fitted and approved for the specific machine, can help stop or inhibit high-voltage operation if someone enters a defined danger zone. Operators must still maintain the required safety distance and follow the approved manual, training, warnings, and stop-work procedures.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso equipment uses high-voltage energy near plants, soil, electrodes, moving machinery, conductive objects, and sometimes bystanders, workers, animals, fences, trellises, irrigation hardware, or wet vegetation. For that reason, safe operation cannot rely on a single safeguard. It depends on a layered safety concept that may include exclusion zones, visual and acoustic warnings, trained operation, emergency stop functions, high-voltage inhibit logic, insulation monitoring, grounding or earthing checks, electrode-position monitoring, fault detection, and product-specific shutdown behavior.

Presence detection should be explained as one possible layer in that system. Internal safety material describes the concept of monitoring a high-voltage danger zone with sensing technologies, such as camera-based systems or other suitable sensors, so that entry by a person or animal can lead to automatic shutdown or prevent operation from starting. That supports the answer that Zasso can use people-detection concepts where the product design, sensor coverage, validation, and approved documentation include them.

However, this should not be sold as a blanket guarantee. A sensor can only detect defined conditions within its intended coverage and operating limits. Dust, vegetation, terrain, lighting, weather, mounting position, calibration, blind spots, sensor health, software configuration, and local approval can all affect what a system can reliably detect. Even when presence detection is fitted, it does not replace the exclusion zone, operator supervision, site control, training, PPE where required, maintenance, shutdown and discharge procedures, or the rule that people and animals must stay out of the protected area while high voltage may be active.

The safest sales answer is: Zasso designs relevant systems with layered safety controls, and people or animal detection may be included on specific products or configurations. The exact capability, sensor type, coverage, shutdown behavior, reset rules, and customer procedure must come from the approved product manual, training, and Zasso technical documentation for that machine.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not assume that the machine will always see every person, animal, vehicle, or object near the equipment. They should plan the work area so people and animals remain outside the required safety distance before high voltage is enabled. If the site includes public access, demonstrations, workers moving nearby, livestock, visitors, roadsides, orchards, vineyards, or other complex access risks, the customer should use appropriate site controls such as briefings, barriers, signage, cones, spotters, and a clear stop-work rule.

Where presence detection is included, customers should treat it as an additional protective layer rather than permission to stand closer to the equipment. If the system alarms, inhibits operation, or shuts down because of a detected presence or safety-zone event, the operator should keep the area clear, identify the cause, and restart only when the approved procedure allows it.

For fleet owners, contractors, municipalities, and farms, this means training should cover both the human rule and the machine behavior: who may approach, when high voltage must be disabled, what the alarm means, when restart is allowed, and when service or technical support is needed.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Some Zasso systems or configurations may include people or animal presence detection as part of a layered safety concept, but the exact capability is product-specific and must come from the approved manual and training."

"Presence detection, where fitted, is an additional safety layer. It does not replace the required safety distance, trained operation, site control, or shutdown procedures."

"If a person or animal enters the protected zone, the operator should stop high-voltage output and follow the approved restart procedure before continuing."

## Caveats

Do not claim that every Zasso product detects people near the equipment. Do not claim that presence detection is guaranteed, has full coverage, detects every posture or object, works in all weather and field conditions, or makes the machine safe for untrained users or bystanders.

Do not disclose sensor layouts, sensor suppliers, algorithms, camera coverage, detection thresholds, software logic, safety ratings, response times, validation data, fault codes, reset rules, certification status, or product-specific procedures unless that language is already approved for external use.

Presence detection depends on product configuration, sensor type, field of view, vegetation, terrain, lighting, dust, rain, fog, mud, sensor cleanliness, calibration, maintenance state, software configuration, local regulation, and operator response. The approved manual and training always take priority over this general FAQ.
