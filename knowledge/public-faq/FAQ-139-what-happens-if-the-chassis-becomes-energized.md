# What happens if the chassis becomes energized?

## Short Answer

If the chassis becomes energized, it should be treated as an abnormal fault condition and a potential electric-shock hazard. The operator should stop high voltage, keep people and animals away from the machine and treatment area, and follow the approved Zasso shutdown, emergency, inspection, and restart procedure. The customer-safe message is that the chassis is not intended to be an exposed high-voltage pathway, and any suspected chassis energization must be handled by trained personnel according to the product manual.

## Detailed Answer

In normal operation, Zasso electrical weeding equipment is designed to direct controlled high-voltage energy through the applicators or electrodes into the target vegetation and the intended plant-soil return path. The chassis, guards, brackets, tractor structure, and other accessible conductive parts should not become the treatment path. Chassis energization means that a conductive part of the machine has reached an unintended electrical potential because of a fault or abnormal conductive bridge.

Possible causes can include damaged insulation, damaged cables, contaminated connectors, moisture or mud bridging conductive parts, contact with metal objects, conductive plant material touching both an electrode and a machine structure, maintenance errors, abnormal grounding or bonding behavior, or another unresolved fault. The specific cause and protective response are product-specific, so this FAQ should not be used to diagnose the machine in the field.

The main risk is touch potential: a person standing on the ground and touching an energized chassis could become part of an unintended electrical path. Step potential can also matter near energized equipment or fault-current paths, depending on the site conditions. The severity of any exposure depends on current path, available energy, exposure time, footwear, ground and soil moisture, body contact, protective systems, and how quickly the equipment detects and stops the abnormal condition.

Zasso manages this risk through a combination of product design, insulation, grounding or return-path management where applicable, monitoring, fault handling, safe shutdown behavior, operator training, exclusion zones, inspection, and maintenance procedures. For customers, the practical rule is simple: do not approach, touch, clear, or service the machine during a suspected energized-chassis condition unless the system has been shut down, made safe, and handled by trained personnel under the approved procedure.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat suspected chassis energization as a stop-work event. If there is an alarm, unusual behavior, arcing, shock sensation, damaged insulation, cable damage, water or mud bridging, metal contact, or any reason to suspect that machine parts may be energized, the operator should stop high voltage, maintain the safety zone, prevent bystanders or animals from approaching, and follow the product-specific emergency and service process.

This does not mean that the chassis is normally energized during operation. It means high-voltage field equipment must be managed conservatively if an abnormal electrical path is suspected. Operators, contractors, distributors, and service teams should rely on Zasso training and the approved manual for inspection checks, PPE, discharge procedures, restart rules, and escalation to technical support.

For demonstrations and commercial operation, the important customer practice is disciplined site control: keep people away from the active treatment area, inspect the machine before use, stop for alarms or abnormal conditions, avoid operation through unsafe water or metal-contact situations, and never allow untrained people to troubleshoot high-voltage equipment.

## Caveats

Do not say that an energized chassis is harmless, impossible, always automatically safe, or safe to touch. Do not say that tires, dry soil, ordinary shoes, operator experience, or visual inspection alone make contact safe. Risk depends on product configuration, machine condition, grounding or bonding design, insulation condition, current path, moisture, soil conditions, nearby conductive objects, exposure time, protective response, and operator behavior.

Do not provide improvised electrical rescue, diagnostic, reset, repair, grounding, bonding, or maintenance instructions beyond the approved product manual, Zasso training, local electrical-safety rules, and emergency procedures. If electric shock, arcing, smoke, fire, repeated alarms, damaged insulation, or suspected energized metal occurs, stop operation and follow the approved emergency and service process.

Do not disclose confidential voltage, current, power, frequency, pulse, insulation-monitoring, grounding, bonding, sensor, diagnostic, trip, shutdown-time, discharge-time, firmware, telemetry, certification, incident, customer, supplier, or test-result details unless they are already approved for public use.
