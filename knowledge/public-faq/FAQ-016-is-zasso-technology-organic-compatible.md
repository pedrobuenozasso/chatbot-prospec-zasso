# Is Zasso technology organic-compatible?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso technology can be compatible with organic production because it is a non-chemical, physical weed-control method rather than a synthetic herbicide input. In organic systems, it should be positioned as one tool within integrated weed management, alongside prevention, crop rotation, mulching, mowing, cultivation, hand weeding, and other approved practices. The practical answer still depends on the country, crop, certifier, product configuration, operating procedure, cleaning requirements, records, and the farm's organic system plan.

## Detailed Answer

Organic compatibility is not the same as a universal certification guarantee. Organic rules are set by jurisdiction and interpreted through certifiers, private standards, buyers, and farm-specific organic system plans. For that reason, the safest customer-facing statement is that Zasso's electric-weeding technology is designed to support organic-compatible weed management where the specific use is accepted by the applicable certifier and local rules.

Zasso technology does not control weeds by applying a conventional synthetic herbicide. It applies electrical energy through contacted plant tissue, injuring or killing weeds as a physical treatment. That makes it a natural fit for growers who are certified organic, transitioning to organic, working under chemical-free specifications, or trying to reduce reliance on prohibited herbicide inputs.

In the United States, USDA organic rules require producers to use preventive, cultural, mechanical, and physical weed-management practices before relying on allowed input substances. USDA guidance also describes mechanical and physical practices as a normal part of organic pest, weed, and disease management. In the European Union, organic production similarly aims to reduce external inputs, and plant-protection substances used in organic agriculture must be pre-approved.

Independent and public evidence also supports the organic-use case. Oregon State University Extension published 2026 guidance on electrical weed control in certified-organic blueberry fields using Zasso equipment, describing it as a promising nonchemical tool for organic growers when operated with the right speed, timing, crop shielding, soil moisture, and fire-risk precautions. Zasso public materials position electric weed control as a non-chemical alternative to herbicides, while internal Zasso materials and SharePoint R&D references frame electrical weeding as a physical tool that can support organic and herbicide-reduction systems.

## What This Means for Customers

For certified organic growers, Zasso can help add a non-chemical weed-control option to the farm's weed-management toolbox. That can be especially useful where conventional synthetic herbicides are not allowed, where hand labor is expensive or scarce, where repeated cultivation risks crop roots or soil structure, or where customers and buyers value residue-conscious production.

Customers should still treat organic compliance as a documented farm-management question, not only a machine question. Before adoption, the grower or service provider should confirm the intended Zasso use with the certifier or advisor, document it as a physical weed-control practice where required, follow operator training and safety procedures, prevent contamination from previous uses or residues, and keep the application records expected under the relevant organic standard.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso is a non-chemical physical weed-control technology, so it can fit well in organic and transition-to-organic systems where the local certifier accepts the use and the grower documents it properly."

"For organic growers, Zasso should be presented as an additional tool inside integrated weed management, not as a blanket replacement for every cultural, mechanical, or preventive weed-control practice."

"Before use in a certified organic operation, we recommend confirming the specific application with the farm's certifier or organic advisor and following Zasso's training, safety, cleaning, and recordkeeping procedures."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is automatically certified organic in every country, crop, or use case. Organic compatibility depends on the applicable standard, certifier interpretation, machine configuration, application procedure, equipment cleaning, records, and farm context.

Do not claim that Zasso alone satisfies organic weed-management requirements. Organic systems normally require prevention, rotation, soil and crop management, cultural practices, mechanical or physical methods, and documentation.

Do not overstate performance. Electrical weeding can be effective in suitable conditions, but results depend on weed species, plant size, soil moisture, contact quality, application speed, pass number, crop shielding, operator training, and safety conditions.
