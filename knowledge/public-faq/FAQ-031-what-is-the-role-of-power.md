# What is the role of power?

## Short Answer

Power is the rate at which electrical energy is delivered into the weed and the plant-soil circuit. It matters because plant injury depends not only on voltage or current, but on how much useful energy reaches the target tissues during the time the electrodes are in contact. In Zasso's context, power should be explained as controlled energy delivery: enough to damage the plant's recovery structures under suitable conditions, but managed through the machine's safety architecture, contact quality, operating speed, and field guidance.

## Detailed Answer

In electrical weeding, voltage helps establish the electrical path and current is the flow through that path. Power combines those two ideas: it describes how quickly energy is being transferred through the load. In simple electrical terms, power is related to voltage multiplied by current, but in the field the useful question is not just the machine's nominal kilowatt rating. The useful question is how much of that power is actually delivered through the target plant tissues rather than lost through poor contact, wet or conductive soil, bare-ground contact, residue, parallel weeds, or other easier current paths.

This is why power is central to treatment dose. A plant needs enough useful energy over enough contact time to disrupt cells, vascular tissues, crowns, roots, or other recovery structures. If delivered power is too low for the actual plant-soil impedance, the result may be visible leaf injury without durable control. If power is applied without proper control, contact discipline, safety systems, or operating guidance, it can waste energy, increase stress on the equipment, raise fire or safety risk, or still fail to reach the biological target.

Zasso should therefore be positioned as a controlled power-delivery technology, not as a brute-force high-voltage device. Field conditions change continuously: plant size, plant water content, weed density, soil moisture, soil conductivity, electrode contact, and travel speed all influence how much useful power reaches each plant. The practical goal is to deliver enough energy into the right pathway, for the right contact duration, while keeping the system within its validated operating and safety envelope.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not evaluate electrical weeding only by asking for the highest voltage, highest current, or largest headline power number. A higher power rating can help only if the system can direct that energy into the target weed under real operating conditions. The better customer question is whether the machine, applicator, training, and operating protocol can deliver controlled, repeatable energy into the plant-soil circuit for the target crop, weed stage, soil condition, and working speed.

Power also explains why speed and contact time matter. At a given operating condition, faster travel usually reduces the time available to transfer energy into each plant, while slower travel can increase dose but may reduce productivity. Dense vegetation can split available power across many conductive paths, and wet or highly conductive soil can divert energy away from the plant. Good results therefore depend on matching power delivery, speed, electrode contact, and field conditions rather than treating power as a standalone specification.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Power is the rate at which useful electrical energy is delivered into the weed. Zasso focuses on controlled energy delivery through the plant-soil circuit, not just on a headline voltage or current number."

"More power is only valuable when it reaches the target plant under suitable conditions. That is why electrode contact, travel speed, soil condition, weed stage, and training all matter."

"The goal is to deliver enough controlled energy to damage the plant's recovery structures while operating within the product's safety and application guidance."

## Caveats

Do not claim that higher power always means better weed control, that a specific kilowatt rating is universally ideal, or that power alone guarantees root kill. Delivered dose depends on voltage, current, contact time, impedance, electrode geometry, plant species, plant size, plant water content, weed density, soil moisture, soil conductivity, residue, speed, equipment setup, and operator training.

Do not disclose confidential power-module settings, control algorithms, electrical limits, fault thresholds, unpublished field data, certification details, customer-specific results, prices, or engineering design details. Public wording should remain at the level of controlled energy delivery and practical operating variables.
