# Does it affect beneficial fungi?

## Short Answer

Based on current Zasso materials and available external research, Zasso's electrical weeding is not expected to meaningfully harm beneficial soil fungi when used according to validated operating protocols. The strongest available evidence is broader soil-biota and microbial-community evidence, including work showing no observed damage to fungal indicators and no meaningful suppression of microbial activity after tested applications. The claim should remain qualified because fungal communities vary by soil, crop, season, moisture, organic matter, sampling depth, dose, and repeated-use program.

## Detailed Answer

Beneficial fungi are part of the living soil microbiome. They contribute to decomposition, nutrient cycling, soil structure, plant-root interactions, and disease-suppressive soil functions. Customers ask about fungi because Zasso's technology sends electrical energy through the target plant and the plant-soil circuit, so it is reasonable to ask whether organisms close to roots could be affected.

Zasso's technology is designed as a non-chemical physical weed-control method. It is not intended to sterilize, fumigate, disinfect, or broadly heat the soil. Some current can pass through the soil return path, but the available evidence does not indicate a meaningful adverse effect on beneficial fungi or broader microbial function under realistic tested applications.

The most relevant public evidence includes DPIRD work with the Zasso XPower platform in Australia. Using Rhizoctonia solani AG8, a soil fungus associated with root disease, as an indicator species, researchers reported that electric weed control did not influence the Rhizoctonia population even at a slow 1.4 km/h application speed intended to maximize the electrical dose, and even after repeated applications to the same site. While Rhizoctonia is not a beneficial fungus, the result is useful because it tested a root-associated fungal organism under deliberately severe conditions. DPIRD public summaries also reported no negative impact on the Rhizoctonia fungus and no evidence of damage to soil biota in those trials.

Broader microbial evidence points in the same direction. Zasso-held ecotoxicology material summarizes microbial activity work using basal respiration, microbial biomass carbon, and dehydrogenase activity, with no meaningful suppression under tested conditions. Public summaries from GROW Integrated Weed Management also report preliminary research showing no detected differences in beneficial bacteria or fungi in weed-electrocution plots and no ill effects on microbial respiration in referenced Zasso electric-weeding work.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers focused on soil biology, the practical message is that Zasso can be positioned as a non-chemical weed-control option that is compatible with soil-health goals when applied under validated conditions. It does not add herbicide active ingredients or chemical residues to the soil, and it can reduce reliance on soil-disturbing mechanical methods in suitable applications.

Where beneficial fungi are a specific customer concern, especially in organic, regenerative, orchard, vineyard, or high-value crop systems, local monitoring can be used to build confidence. A fair evaluation should compare treated and untreated areas using consistent sampling depth, timing, soil moisture, crop stage, laboratory method, and repeated-use history.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Based on the evidence available today, Zasso's electrical weeding is not expected to meaningfully harm beneficial soil fungi when used under validated conditions. Available soil-biota and microbial-community studies have not shown meaningful fungal or microbial suppression, but local soil conditions and repeated-use programs should still be monitored."

"The responsible claim is that tested applications show encouraging compatibility with soil microbial life, including fungal indicators, not that every fungus in every soil will be completely unaffected under every possible use pattern."

## Caveats

Do not claim that beneficial fungi are unaffected in every soil, crop, dose, season, or repeated-use scenario. Fungal communities are naturally variable and can be influenced by soil moisture, temperature, organic matter, pH, crop roots, residues, tillage, herbicide history, compaction, sampling depth, laboratory method, and seasonal timing.

Avoid claiming that Zasso improves beneficial fungi unless a specific local study supports that claim. The safer position is that available evidence has not shown meaningful harm under tested conditions, and that local monitoring is appropriate where fungal health is a key customer metric.
