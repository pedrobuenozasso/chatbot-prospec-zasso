# Is it compatible with cover cropping?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can be compatible with cover cropping when it is used to support the cover-crop plan rather than accidentally remove desired vegetation. Its strongest fit is as a non-chemical tool for cover-crop termination, termination cleanup, or pre-plant vegetation management in suitable systems. Compatibility depends on cover-crop species, biomass, growth stage, moisture, residue level, fire risk, planting schedule, equipment setup, and local validation.

## Detailed Answer

Cover crops are planted intentionally to protect soil, suppress weeds, cycle nutrients, support soil biology, reduce erosion, manage water, or add biodiversity. Because they are intentional vegetation, Zasso should not be described as automatically compatible with cover cropping in every use case. The key question is whether the electrical treatment supports the grower's cover-crop objective.

Zasso's electrical weeding is non-selective on contacted plants. That makes it useful when the goal is to terminate a cover crop before planting, manage regrowth after incomplete termination, or clean up green patches after rolling, mowing, winterkill, grazing, or herbicide termination does not finish the job. It may be especially relevant where growers want to reduce chemical termination, avoid tillage, preserve soil cover, or test non-chemical options in organic or reduced-herbicide systems.

The fit is weaker where living cover must be preserved, where crop seedlings are already present and could be contacted, or where dense residue and uneven biomass prevent good electrode contact. Cover-crop mixtures can also respond differently by species. Grasses, legumes, brassicas, and mixed stands may require different timing, speed, biomass management, or repeat passes.

Moisture and residue conditions matter. Green, conductive plant tissue is generally more suitable for electrical treatment than dry plant material, while excessive soil moisture can change how current moves through the system. Dry standing or mowed biomass can also increase fire-risk concerns, so weather, wind, dew, residue, and local safety procedures must be considered before application.

The safest position is that Zasso is cover-crop-compatible in suitable termination or cleanup scenarios, but it is not a replacement for agronomic planning. It should be integrated with species selection, planting date, termination timing, planter setup, residue management, scouting, and backup plans.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers using cover crops, Zasso may add another option between herbicide termination, roller-crimping, mowing, grazing, and tillage. This can be valuable when the farm wants to keep soil disturbance low, reduce selected chemical use, or improve reliability after partial termination.

Customers should decide first whether the target vegetation is supposed to stay alive or be terminated. If the cover crop is still providing desired living-root, soil-cover, grazing, pollinator, or habitat functions, Zasso should be kept away from those areas. If the goal is termination, customers should validate the timing, species mix, biomass, moisture, treatment speed, regrowth risk, fire-risk conditions, and whether a second pass or complementary method is needed.

A practical adoption path is to start with a small, well-documented local trial. Measure termination percentage, regrowth, cash-crop emergence, planter performance, residue handling, weeds after planting, operating speed, energy use, number of passes, and any herbicide or tillage pass avoided.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can fit cover-cropping systems when the objective is to terminate or clean up cover-crop biomass without relying only on herbicides or soil-disturbing tillage."

"We position Zasso as a non-chemical option for suitable cover-crop termination and rescue scenarios, not as a blanket solution for every cover crop, species mix, or field condition."

"The best results come from planning around species, biomass, moisture, residue, timing before planting, regrowth risk, and local safety conditions."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is selective between cover crops, cash crops, weeds, and desired vegetation. Contacted plants may be injured, so treatment zones and timing must be managed carefully.

Do not claim universal one-pass termination. Some cover-crop species or mixtures may require repeat passes or complementary practices, and regrowth can occur depending on species, root system, timing, and conditions.

Do not ignore fire risk. Dry cover-crop biomass, mowed residue, wind, low humidity, and high fire-risk periods require conservative operating decisions and local safety procedures.

Do not promise compatibility with organic, regenerative, carbon, conservation, or incentive programs without checking the relevant standard, certifier, buyer, or program rules.
