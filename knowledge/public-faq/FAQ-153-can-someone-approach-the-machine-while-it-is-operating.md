# Can someone approach the machine while it is operating?

## Short Answer

No. People should not approach Zasso equipment while it is operating, while high voltage may be active, or while the machine has not been made safe according to the approved product procedure. Anyone who needs to speak with the operator, inspect the implement, move an object, clear vegetation, or approach the treatment area should wait until the operator has stopped the application and followed the required shutdown and safety steps.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso electrical weeding equipment uses high-voltage energy to treat plants through applicators or electrodes and the intended plant-soil pathway. During operation, the area around the implement is not a normal pedestrian area. People should stay outside the required safety zone and should not approach electrodes, applicators, cables, conductive vegetation, crop infrastructure, wet ground, metal objects, the implement, or any part of the treatment path while high voltage may be active.

The main concern is unintended electrical exposure. A person approaching the machine could make direct contact with energized electrodes, touch conductive material connected to the treatment path, touch a metal object that has become energized during an abnormal condition, or enter an area where soil voltage gradients, wet conditions, damaged insulation, arcing, or a fault could create touch-voltage or step-voltage risk. The hazard can depend on product configuration, applicator geometry, site condition, soil moisture, vegetation, nearby metal, water, weather, machine state, and whether the approved procedure is followed.

For customers, the practical rule is simple: do not let anyone approach the operating machine. If someone enters the required safety zone or tries to approach, the operator should stop high-voltage output and follow the approved product procedure before allowing access or restarting. Demonstrations, farms, municipalities, contractors, orchards, vineyards, and public-area operations should plan site control so visitors, workers, pedestrians, vehicles, animals, and tools stay clear while the equipment is active.

This does not mean the equipment is unsafe when used correctly. It means Zasso equipment should be treated as professional high-voltage agricultural or industrial equipment. Safe use depends on trained operation, product-specific safety distances, controlled access, pre-use inspection, alarms and warnings, emergency-stop discipline, maintenance condition, and the approved manual and training.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should brief everyone on site before operation starts. No one should walk toward the machine to ask a question, inspect treated weeds, take a close photo, adjust the implement, move tools, touch crop wires, handle irrigation parts, guide animals, or cross near the applicators while the machine is operating.

In closed agricultural fields, site control may be handled through supervision and communication with workers. In demonstrations, orchards, vineyards, roadsides, municipalities, public land, or contractor work, stronger controls may be needed, such as signage, cones, barriers, spotters, radio communication, traffic or pedestrian control, and clear stop-work rules.

The customer benefit is a safer and more repeatable operating routine: keep the operating zone clear, stop if the zone is breached, make the equipment safe before anyone approaches, and restart only when the site is controlled again.

## Caveats

Do not say that someone can safely approach the machine during operation if they avoid touching the electrodes. Do not imply that visual distance, common sense, rubber tires, dry soil, or the absence of sparks is enough to make approach safe.

Do not publish product-specific safety distances, discharge times, PPE ratings, interlock logic, sensor thresholds, emergency procedures, fault-current thresholds, voltage or current values, certification claims, or maintenance steps unless they are taken from approved customer-facing product documentation.

Approach rules can vary by product, applicator type, operating mode, crop, terrain, soil moisture, wet vegetation, nearby metal, water, fences, trellises, irrigation equipment, public access, animals, weather, machine condition, training level, and local regulation. The approved Zasso manual and training always take priority.
