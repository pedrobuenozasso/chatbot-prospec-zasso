# Can the operator leave the tractor while the equipment is on?

## Short Answer

No. The operator should not leave the tractor while Zasso equipment is operating, while high voltage may be active, or while the implement has not been shut down and made safe according to the approved product procedure. If the operator needs to leave the tractor, the application must be stopped first, the equipment must be shut down as instructed, and the operator must follow the product-specific safety, discharge, PPE, and verification steps before approaching the machine.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso electrical weeding equipment uses high-voltage energy to treat plants through applicators, electrodes, vegetation, and the intended plant-soil pathway. During application, the safe operating position for a tractor-mounted system is the approved tractor/operator position, not the area around the implement, electrodes, cables, applicators, crop wires, metal objects, wet ground, or treated vegetation.

Leaving the tractor while the equipment is on can create several hazards. The operator may enter the application danger zone, approach energized electrodes, touch conductive vegetation or crop infrastructure, step into an area with possible step voltage, contact metal objects that have been energized by an applicator, or create a situation where the machine is moving, powered, or able to restart unexpectedly. For that reason, the safe rule is simple: do not dismount during application or while high voltage may still be present.

If the operator must leave the tractor, the operator should first stop the application and follow the approved shutdown sequence in the product manual and training. Product-specific steps can include switching off high voltage, stopping the PTO or power source where applicable, lowering or positioning the implement as instructed, securing against restart, using required PPE, waiting for or verifying discharge, and confirming that electrodes and other relevant parts are de-energized before approach or contact. The exact steps and timing depend on the product and must come from the approved Zasso documentation.

This answer should be understood as an operating rule, not just a legal disclaimer. High-voltage agricultural equipment can be safe only when the operator remains in the correct position during operation and treats shutdown, discharge, verification, and access control as mandatory parts of the job.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should train operators to stay in the tractor or approved operator position while the machine is applying. If there is an alarm, blockage, abnormal behavior, bystander issue, fire risk, metal-object concern, or need to inspect the implement, the operator should stop the application and follow the full shutdown and safety procedure before leaving the tractor.

Supervisors should make this a practical field rule: no one approaches the implement, electrodes, applicators, cables, crop wires, metal objects, or treatment zone until the equipment has been made safe. Demonstrations, service work, and commercial operations should include clear stop-work rules, exclusion-zone control, emergency-stop awareness, trained operators, and product-specific instructions for shutdown and verification.

This also means customers should not rely on informal shortcuts such as stepping down quickly, assuming the cab or tires make everything safe, assuming the machine is safe because it has stopped moving, or assuming electrodes are safe immediately after pressing a button. The correct process is the one in the manual and training for that specific machine.

## Caveats

Do not tell customers that an operator may leave the tractor while the equipment is on, while high voltage may be active, or while the machine has not been made safe. Do not imply that pressing one control, stopping forward movement, turning off the PTO, or waiting briefly is always sufficient unless that is exactly what the approved product procedure says for that product and state.

Do not publish product-specific discharge times, safety distances, PPE ratings, voltage thresholds, emergency procedures, interlock logic, fault-detection behavior, or service steps unless they come from approved customer-facing Zasso documentation.

Requirements can vary by product, applicator, power architecture, tractor configuration, operating mode, local regulation, site condition, weather, soil moisture, crop infrastructure, nearby metal objects, and whether the task is normal operation, inspection, clearing, cleaning, service, transport, or maintenance.
