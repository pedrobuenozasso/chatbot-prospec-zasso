# What safety procedures are mandatory before maintenance?

## Short Answer

Before maintenance, Zasso equipment must be stopped, switched off, secured against restart, and made safe according to the approved product manual and training for that specific machine. Maintenance should be performed only by trained and authorized personnel using the required PPE, correct tools, approved parts, and the product-specific shutdown, discharge, inspection, and verification steps. No one should touch electrodes, cables, connectors, applicators, guards, or other safety-critical parts until the equipment has been de-energized and verified safe for the task.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso equipment uses controlled high-voltage electrical energy to treat weeds through applicators, electrodes, vegetation, and the intended plant-soil pathway. That means maintenance cannot be treated like ordinary adjustment of a low-risk farm implement. Before inspection, cleaning, clearing, electrode replacement, connector checks, service work, or troubleshooting, the machine must be placed into a controlled safe state.

At a customer-facing level, the mandatory maintenance procedure should include these categories: stop the application, switch off or deactivate the equipment, isolate the relevant energy sources where applicable, secure the machine against unexpected restart, allow or perform the product-specific discharge step, verify that the relevant parts are safe to approach or touch, use the required PPE, keep unauthorized people away, and perform only the maintenance tasks allowed for that user's role.

The exact sequence depends on the product, applicator, power architecture, battery or generator system, task, and local rules. For some machines, an operator may be allowed to perform limited tasks such as visual checks, cleaning, checking connectors, or replacing specified wear parts. Electrical troubleshooting, high-voltage diagnostics, insulation checks, safety-system work, repairs, annual inspections, or work inside protected areas should be performed only by Zasso, an authorized dealer, or qualified personnel trained for that product.

Maintenance must also include a return-to-service discipline. After work is complete, guards, insulation, electrodes, protective mats, connectors, safety devices, warning systems, emergency-stop functions, and other safety-critical parts should be checked as instructed before the machine is used again. If damage, wet contamination, abnormal alarms, damaged insulation, missing parts, non-approved modifications, or uncertainty remain, the machine should stay out of service until checked by authorized support.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat maintenance as a planned safety activity, not as an informal field repair. Supervisors should make sure the right people are authorized for the task, the manual is available, PPE is used where required, the work area is controlled, and the machine is not restarted until the approved procedure is complete.

Operators should not improvise around high voltage. If the task involves electrodes, cables, insulation, safety systems, high-voltage components, damaged parts, alarms, unusual sparking, wet conditions, fire risk, or uncertainty about whether the equipment is safe, the correct action is to stop and involve trained Zasso service or qualified support.

This approach protects people and keeps the machine reliable. It also helps customers distinguish routine operator maintenance from service work that requires additional authorization, tools, testing, documentation, or annual inspection.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Before maintenance, Zasso equipment must be stopped, switched off, secured against restart, discharged and verified where applicable, and handled only by trained and authorized personnel following the approved manual."

"Routine operator checks and electrical service are not the same. Operators may perform only the maintenance tasks allowed in the manual, while high-voltage diagnostics, repairs, safety-system work, and annual inspections should be handled by authorized or qualified personnel."

"If there is damage, an alarm, wet contamination, abnormal behavior, or uncertainty about whether the machine is safe, the machine should stay out of service until trained support has checked it."

## Caveats

Do not use this FAQ as a substitute for the product manual, training, site risk assessment, employer safety rules, or local regulation. Product-specific steps, PPE requirements, discharge methods, inspection intervals, lockout details, and authorization levels may vary by machine and market.

Do not publish exact discharge times, voltage or current thresholds, stored-energy values, PPE ratings, lockout steps, high-voltage test procedures, interlock logic, wiring details, sensor thresholds, service manuals, or certification claims unless they are approved for customer-facing release.

Do not imply that switching the machine off casually, waiting briefly, wearing ordinary shoes, using dry soil, seeing no spark, or having general machinery experience is enough to make maintenance safe. The safe condition must be created and verified through the approved procedure.
