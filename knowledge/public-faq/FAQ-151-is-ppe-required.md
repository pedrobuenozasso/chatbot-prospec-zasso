# Is PPE required?

## Short Answer

Yes, PPE is required when the approved Zasso manual, training, product label, risk assessment, or local regulation specifies it. Because Zasso equipment uses high-voltage electrical energy, PPE must be treated as part of the safety system for the specific machine and task, not as optional comfort equipment. The exact PPE may vary by product and activity, but it can include items such as protective footwear, gloves, eye or face protection, hearing protection, high-visibility clothing, cut-resistant gloves, fire-related PPE, or voltage-rated insulating PPE where required.

## Detailed Answer

PPE requirements for Zasso equipment are product-specific and task-specific. A pedestrian or handheld unit, a tractor-mounted applicator, a maintenance task, a cleaning activity, and a service intervention can expose people to different hazards. For that reason, the correct answer is not a universal list that applies to every machine; the correct PPE list is the one in the approved manual, training, site procedure, and applicable local safety rules for the exact product and task.

In normal operation, PPE helps manage residual risks that remain after the machine's engineering controls, warnings, interlocks, exclusion zone, grounding or return-path concept, insulation, and operating procedures are applied. Depending on the product, these risks can include electrical shock, step or touch voltage, contact with high-voltage electrodes, arc or spark exposure, fire risk, hot surfaces, sharp edges, moving parts, noise, dust, poor visibility, and uneven field conditions.

PPE should never be used as a substitute for safe operation. Wearing PPE does not make it acceptable to touch energized electrodes, enter the danger zone, bypass safety devices, operate in prohibited conditions, allow untrained people near the machine, or perform maintenance before shutdown and verification. It is one layer in a wider safety system that also includes trained operators, pre-use inspection, access control, safe distance, proper deactivation, maintenance discipline, and following the manual.

For some products, the approved PPE may include insulating boots, insulating gloves, or other voltage-rated protective equipment. For other tasks, ordinary work PPE such as cut-resistant gloves, safety shoes, eye protection, or high-visibility clothing may be the relevant protection. Customers should not assume that ordinary rubber boots, gardening gloves, or generic work gloves provide electrical protection unless they are explicitly approved and rated for the task.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat PPE as mandatory whenever it is listed in Zasso documentation, training, local procedures, or local regulation. Operators and supervisors should know which PPE is required for operation, inspection, cleaning, transport, electrode handling, maintenance, fire-response readiness, and service work.

A practical customer routine should include making the required PPE available in the right sizes, inspecting it before use, keeping it clean and dry where required, replacing damaged or expired equipment, and ensuring that operators understand when each item is required. Voltage-rated PPE, if specified, should be suitable for the relevant hazard and maintained, tested, or replaced according to the applicable standard, manufacturer instruction, and local regulation.

The most important customer message is discipline: use the required PPE, keep people out of the danger zone, stop the machine when conditions are unsafe, and follow the approved shutdown and maintenance procedure before approaching electrodes, cables, connectors, guards, or other safety-critical parts.

## Caveats

Do not publish a universal PPE list as if it applies to every Zasso product, every country, or every task. The required PPE depends on the product, operator position, applicator, voltage architecture, guarding, operating mode, maintenance task, work environment, site risk assessment, and local regulation.

Do not claim that PPE eliminates electrical, fire, mechanical, or operational risk. PPE reduces exposure only when selected, used, inspected, and maintained correctly, and only as part of the wider safety system.

Do not imply that ordinary rubber boots, work gloves, gardening gloves, chemical gloves, or generic safety shoes are equivalent to voltage-rated PPE. If electrical protection is required, the rating, inspection routine, testing interval, and approved use must come from the product documentation and applicable rules.

Specific PPE models, voltage ratings, testing intervals, internal risk analyses, service procedures, and certification details should only be shared from approved Zasso documentation.
