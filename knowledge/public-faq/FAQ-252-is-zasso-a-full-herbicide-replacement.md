# Is Zasso a full herbicide replacement?

## Short Answer

No, Zasso should not be presented as a full herbicide replacement across all crops, weeds, geographies, and operating conditions. In suitable applications, it can replace specific herbicide passes or reduce overall herbicide use, but the stronger and safer positioning is that Zasso is a non-chemical tool within integrated weed management. The right question is not whether herbicides disappear everywhere, but where Zasso can credibly replace, reduce, or improve a chemical program.

## Detailed Answer

Herbicides remain important because they are familiar, scalable, and often cost-effective. Zasso's value is not that every herbicide program becomes obsolete. Its value is that Electroherb adds a different, physical mode of action based on controlled electrical energy applied through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit, without applying a herbicide active ingredient.

That means Zasso can substitute for herbicides in selected situations, especially where a customer has a clear reason to avoid or reduce chemical use. Examples may include resistant-weed pressure, drift-sensitive locations, residue-sensitive production, some permanent-crop operations, public or infrastructure sites, organic-compatible programs where locally accepted, or specific non-selective weed-control tasks where the equipment, operating conditions, economics, and safety controls are suitable.

But a full replacement claim goes too far. Weed spectrum, plant size, root system, moisture, density, crop layout, operating speed, number of passes, fire-risk conditions, machine fit, local rules, and operator training all affect results. In many conventional systems, herbicides may still remain part of the most practical program. In other systems, Zasso may replace one herbicide pass, reduce chemical frequency, or become the primary non-chemical tool for a defined task.

The commercially safe conclusion is that Zasso can be a replacement for some herbicide operations, but it should not be sold as a universal replacement for all herbicides. The best framing is use-case specific and tied to integrated weed management.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should evaluate Zasso against a real weed-control task, not against an abstract idea of replacing all herbicides. A useful commercial discussion asks which herbicide pass is being replaced or reduced, which weeds are targeted, how many hectares will be treated, what performance is needed, what safety controls apply, and how the economics compare in that exact use case.

For some customers, Zasso may become the main weed-control tool in selected zones or seasons. For others, it may be used alongside herbicides, mowing, cultivation, scouting, and prevention tactics. That is still valuable, because reducing dependence on one approach can improve resilience, help address resistant weeds, reduce spray-drift exposure from avoided spray passes, and create more flexibility in sensitive areas.

The strongest customer-facing message is practical rather than ideological: Zasso can replace herbicides where validated, reduce them where that makes sense, and complement them where that remains the best program design.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso is not a blanket replacement for all herbicides, but it can replace specific herbicide passes or reduce chemical use in suitable applications."

"The right positioning is use-case specific: where resistance, drift, residues, sensitive sites, or chemical-reduction goals matter, Zasso can become a strong non-chemical part of the program."

"We should talk about which herbicide operation Zasso can replace or reduce for this customer, not claim that every herbicide disappears from every system."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso fully replaces herbicides in all crops, weeds, countries, or seasons. That is not supported.

Do not imply that herbicides are obsolete, unnecessary, or ineffective everywhere. Many customers will still use them as part of a broader weed-management program.

Do not make crop-specific replacement claims without local evidence on target weeds, operating protocol, product fit, safety controls, economics, and local regulatory or certification context.

Do not overstate environmental or safety claims. Zasso avoids applying herbicide active ingredients, but it still requires trained operation, correct setup, suitable conditions, and attention to fire-risk and electrical-safety procedures.
