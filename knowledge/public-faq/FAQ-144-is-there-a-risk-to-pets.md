# Is there a risk to pets?

## Short Answer

Yes, pets can be at risk if they enter the active treatment area, touch electrodes, touch conductive vegetation or objects connected to the applicators, or become part of an unintended electrical path. Pets should be kept completely away from Zasso equipment during operation, just like other bystanders and animals. If a pet enters the safety zone, the operator should stop high-voltage output and follow the approved restart procedure before continuing.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso electrical weeding uses controlled high-voltage energy to treat target plants through applicators or electrodes and the intended plant-soil pathway. Pets are not intended to be anywhere near that pathway. The key risk is unintended electrical exposure: a dog, cat, or other domestic animal could approach quickly, touch an energized part, step into a conductive wet area, contact vegetation touching the electrodes, or interact with metal objects, fences, water, or machine parts during an abnormal condition.

Pets create a particular site-control challenge because they may move unpredictably, ignore warnings, run ahead of an operator, follow people or vehicles, sniff or bite objects, walk under equipment, or be attracted by vegetation, water, noise, shade, or movement. For that reason, the safe operating assumption is that pets should be physically excluded from the work area before high voltage is enabled.

Customers should not rely on calling the animal back, judging distance by eye, or assuming that a pet is safe because it is small, fast, dry, or not touching the visible electrode. Safe operation requires keeping pets outside the product-specific safety zone and stopping work immediately if access control is lost.

This does not mean Zasso equipment cannot be used on farms, estates, orchards, vineyards, municipal sites, roadside areas, or other places where pets may be nearby. It means operators must plan the job so pets cannot approach the machine during active operation. Gates, leads, kennels, indoor holding areas, temporary barriers, spotters, signage, or rescheduling may be needed depending on the site.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat pets as protected bystanders. Before starting, operators should check whether dogs, cats, horses used as companion animals, working dogs, or other domestic animals can access the treatment area. If they can, the operator should secure the animal or the area before enabling high voltage.

For contractors, municipalities, estates, and farms near homes or public paths, this is especially important. The worksite may need clear communication with property owners, staff, residents, handlers, or visitors so pets are kept away until the machine is shut down and the approved procedure says the area is safe to re-enter.

The practical customer message is simple: pets should not be allowed near operating Zasso equipment. If a pet approaches, stop the high-voltage output, keep people from trying to retrieve the pet inside the active zone while the machine may be energized, and follow the product manual, training, and local procedures.

## Evidence and Context

Zasso's public technology material explains that high-voltage electricity is generated locally, transferred through applicators into plants and soil, and acts through physical contact with target plants at the time of application. This supports explaining pet safety around preventing unintended contact with energized applicators or conductive paths: https://zasso.com/technology/

Existing Zasso FAQ files in the GitHub repository, especially FAQ-132, FAQ-141, FAQ-142, and FAQ-143, consistently frame safety as a layered system involving trained operation, exclusion zones, controlled access, shutdown procedures, insulation, grounding or return-path management, field conditions, and product-specific manuals. The pet answer follows the same conservative logic as the bystander and animal answers.

Internal Zasso safety and electrical-weeding guide material reviewed for this FAQ discusses high-voltage operation, direct-contact risks, application-zone control, step-voltage considerations, conductive materials, insulation monitoring, emergency shutoff, and the need to keep people or animals outside hazardous zones. That material supports the high-level customer wording here, while product-specific distances, sensor details, thresholds, and procedures should remain in approved manuals and training.

Michigan State University Extension describes electrical weed control as using charged electrodes and high-voltage electricity to treat weeds, with results and behavior influenced by electrode contact, soil moisture, plant condition, voltage, current, and speed: https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/basics-of-electrical-weed-control

GROW IWM's weed-electrocution guidance states that electrical weed-control equipment operates at high voltage and that improper handling can create severe shock risks for operators or nearby individuals. It recommends training, safe distances, inspection, maintenance, protective gear where required, and caution around wet or rainy conditions: https://growiwm.org/weed-electrocution/ and https://growiwm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Weed-Electrocution-Factsheet-PDF.pdf

General farm electrical-safety guidance on stray voltage also supports the principle that animals can be affected when they contact two surfaces with a voltage difference or become part of a conductive path. While stray-voltage guidance is not specific to Zasso equipment or pets, it reinforces the conservative recommendation to prevent domestic animals from contacting energized or faulted conductive paths: https://www.alliantenergy.com/safety/farm-safety/stray-voltage

## Safe Sales Wording

"Pets should be kept completely outside the active treatment area whenever Zasso equipment is operating. If a pet approaches the safety zone, the operator should stop high-voltage output and follow the approved procedure before restarting."

"The equipment is designed for trained professional operation, not for operation around uncontrolled pets. Site control is part of safe use."

"Exact safety distances, restart rules, and access-control requirements are product-specific, so customers should follow the Zasso manual, training, and local rules for their machine and application."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso equipment is harmless to pets, safe for pets to approach, safe around loose dogs or cats, or safe simply because the treatment is non-chemical. Do not imply that pets can walk through the treated area while high voltage may be active.

Do not provide veterinary advice, animal rescue instructions, product-specific safety distances, discharge times, PPE rules, sensor thresholds, electrical limits, certification claims, or emergency procedures unless they come from approved customer-facing product documentation.

Pet risk can vary by product, applicator type, site layout, animal behavior, soil moisture, rain, irrigation, puddles, nearby metal objects, fencing, terrain, vegetation, machine condition, local regulation, and operator behavior. Customers should rely on approved manuals, training, site controls, and local Zasso support for exact requirements.
