# Does dry soil reduce efficacy?

## Short Answer

Dry soil can reduce efficacy in some situations because it usually has higher electrical resistance and can make the plant-soil return path less reliable. However, dry soil does not automatically mean poor control, and wet soil is not automatically better. Zasso performance depends on the full plant-soil circuit, including soil moisture, soil conductivity, plant water content, weed stage, electrode contact, speed, density, residue, and fire-risk conditions.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding works by making the target plant part of an electrical circuit. For lasting control, enough useful energy must pass through the contacted plant and its recovery structures, including the crown and root zone, rather than being lost through easier paths or divided across too many plants.

Dry soil matters because water and dissolved minerals are major contributors to soil conductivity. When the soil is very dry, resistance can increase, the return path through the soil may become less stable, and current flow through the plant-soil circuit may be less consistent. In practical terms, this can reduce effective dose, especially when weeds are also dry, drought-stressed, sparse, small, shielded by residue, or difficult for the electrodes to contact reliably.

That said, the relationship is not one-directional. Slightly moist soil can help conductivity and grounding in some conditions, but overly wet soil can allow current to dissipate through soil or surface water instead of staying concentrated in the target plant. Public trials and Zasso internal materials both support treating moisture as an operating window, not as a simple rule that drier or wetter is always better.

In dry conditions, the operator should also separate efficacy from safety. Dry soil may affect conductivity, while dry plant biomass, straw, mulch, and hot or windy weather can increase fire risk. A dry-soil application may still be suitable if plants are contactable, the circuit is stable, residue and fire risk are managed, and speed and setup are appropriate. In unsuitable dry conditions, postponing treatment or changing the operating plan may be the right decision.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not treat dry soil as an automatic stop, but they should treat it as a condition to assess before application. Field teams should check whether the soil is merely dry at the surface, extremely dry through the active root zone, sandy or low-conductivity, covered with dry residue, dusty, recently mowed, or variable across the field.

If dry soil reduces conductivity or contact quality, operators may need to adjust timing, travel speed, applicator setup, or follow-up scouting expectations. Where dry biomass or weather creates elevated fire risk, safety procedures and local restrictions take priority over efficacy goals.

The practical message is that Zasso is designed to manage energy through a dynamic plant-soil system. Best results come from matching the application window to local soil, plant, weed, residue, and safety conditions rather than relying on a fixed moisture rule.

## Evidence and Context

Zasso's existing FAQ material explains electrical weeding as a plant-soil circuit. FAQ-025 explains that soil moisture changes how easily current moves through the circuit, FAQ-026 explains soil conductivity as a key current-path variable, FAQ-074 cautions that dry weeds and dry biomass require careful qualification, and FAQ-113 explains that soil moisture can either improve or reduce efficacy depending on conditions.

SharePoint R&D material on plant electrocution and efficacy parameters describes Electroherb performance as dependent on the target plant becoming part of the electrical circuit and receiving enough energy for lasting tissue and root injury. The same material identifies plant species, morphology, root volume, age, soil type, and soil moisture as efficacy variables, and it notes that higher soil resistance can diminish efficacy while excessive soil moisture can also increase unwanted current losses through soil paths.

Read.AI meeting context reviewed for this run did not add a new technical soil-moisture specification. It did reinforce that Zasso needs clear educational material for customers asking about soil effects and that operator training, setup, and correct field use are important for safe performance.

DPIRD's public conference paper on soil moisture and electric weed control using a Zasso XPower/XPU system explains that wet soil has lower resistance than dry soil and may allow current to dissipate out of the roots. In its 2022 trial, a narrow moisture range did not show a clear moisture effect; in its 2023 trial, annual ryegrass control declined as soil moisture increased. This supports a nuanced answer rather than a universal dry-soil rule: https://library.dpird.wa.gov.au/conf_papers/299/

Oregon State University Extension states that slightly moist soil can provide better conductivity, while efficiency can drop if soil is too dry or too wet. It also flags fire risk in dry conditions and emphasizes speed, timing, and correct electrode contact: https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9716-how-speed-timing-affect-electrical-weed-control-organic-blueberry-fields

A Weed Science review and vineyard study using Zasso XPower reports that electric weed-control efficacy is affected by plant species, plant age, soil type, and weather conditions, and that soil moisture can influence current dispersal from roots into surrounding soil. ## Safe Sales Wording

"Very dry soil can make the electrical return path less conductive, so it may reduce efficacy in some situations. We assess soil moisture together with plant moisture, weed stage, contact quality, speed, residue, and fire risk before recommending the application window."

"The best operating condition is not simply the driest or the wettest soil. Zasso performance depends on directing enough useful energy through the target plant while avoiding losses through soil, water, residue, or poor contact."

## Caveats

Do not claim that dry soil always prevents control or that wet soil always improves control. Very dry soil can reduce current flow and contact reliability, while very wet soil can dissipate energy away from the target plant.

Do not promise one-pass control, guaranteed root kill, or a universal soil-moisture threshold. The useful application window depends on soil texture, conductivity, salinity, compaction, plant water content, weed species, root architecture, density, residue, electrode contact, speed, and local safety rules.

Do not recommend operation in high fire-risk dry conditions without appropriate local procedures, residue assessment, weather checks, training, and any applicable fire restrictions. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer-specific results, internal control logic, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
