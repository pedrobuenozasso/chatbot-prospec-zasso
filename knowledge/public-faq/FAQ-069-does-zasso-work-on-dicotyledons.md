# Does Zasso work on dicotyledons?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on dicotyledons when the plants are properly contacted and the application protocol is matched to the species, growth stage, density, moisture, and regrowth risk. In weed-control language, many dicotyledon weeds are broadleaf weeds, and Zasso evidence supports treating broadleaf weeds in suitable conditions. The careful claim is that dicotyledons are within Zasso's treatable target spectrum, not that every dicot species or growth stage will be controlled equally or in one pass.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology uses controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation. Because the mode of action is physical rather than herbicide-based, dicotyledon weeds are not treated through a chemical target site. If the applicator makes good contact and enough useful energy is delivered through the plant and plant-soil circuit, dicotyledon weeds can be damaged and controlled.

Dicot morphology can often be favorable for electrical weeding. Many dicot weeds have broader leaves, exposed stems, branching architecture, and above-ground surfaces that can support good electrode contact. These traits can help the equipment transfer energy into plant tissues. This is why broadleaf weeds are often a strong practical category for Zasso discussions.

That said, botanical class alone is not enough to predict control. A young, upright annual dicot with good leaf and stem contact is usually a more favorable target than a mature, deep-rooted, woody, rosette-forming, or perennial dicot with protected crowns, taproots, tubers, rhizomes, or other regrowth reserves. Dense stands can also shield smaller plants from contact and may require slower speeds, better contact discipline, repeat passes, mowing or other preparation, or integration with other weed-management practices.

For customers, the best question is not simply whether the weed is a dicot. The practical evaluation should identify the target species, growth stage, plant height, density, architecture, root or crown reserves, soil and plant moisture, crop proximity, operating speed, number of passes, and the customer's acceptable regrowth threshold.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers with dicotyledon or broadleaf weeds should include Zasso in the evaluation, especially where chemical reduction, herbicide resistance management, residue avoidance, soil disturbance reduction, organic production, or sensitive-area vegetation management are priorities. Dicotyledon weeds may be favorable targets when the applicator can contact enough leaf and stem area to move energy through the plant.

A customer trial should separate dicotyledon or broadleaf weeds from grasses and other monocot targets. The trial should record the dominant species, growth stage, density, moisture, whether plants are upright or rosette-forming, soil condition, application speed, pass count, and follow-up regrowth. For perennial dicots or species with strong below-ground reserves, performance should be judged after enough time has passed for regrowth pressure to appear.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can treat dicotyledon weeds, including many broadleaf weeds, when contact quality, growth stage, density, moisture, speed, and follow-up strategy are properly managed."

"Dicotyledons are often favorable targets because their leaf and stem structure can support good electrode contact, but we still validate the protocol for the customer's species and conditions."

"For mature, perennial, rosette-forming, woody, or deep-rooted dicots, Zasso should be positioned as part of an integrated program with appropriate speed, contact, follow-up, and repeat-pass planning."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso controls every dicotyledon, controls all dicots in one pass, or performs independently of species, growth stage, contact, plant moisture, soil moisture, density, or operating speed. Dicotyledon weeds with strong taproots, crowns, tubers, rhizomes, stolons, woody tissue, dense canopies, or protected regrowth points may need more careful protocol design and follow-up.

Do not equate visible burn-down with confirmed control. For many customers, the important result is not first-day discoloration but reduced regrowth over the following days or weeks. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished customer results, raw trial data, control logic, telemetry details, pricing, certification plans, or private meeting content unless explicitly approved for external use.
