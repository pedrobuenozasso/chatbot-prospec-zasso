# Does Zasso work on resistant weeds?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on herbicide-resistant weeds when the target weeds can be contacted properly and the field conditions support effective electrical treatment. Herbicide resistance usually affects how a plant responds to a chemical mode of action; Zasso's Electroherb technology uses a physical electrical mode of action instead. That makes it a useful tool for resistant-weed programs, but results still depend on species, growth stage, plant size, moisture, weed density, contact quality, speed, repeat timing, and follow-up management.

## Detailed Answer

Herbicide-resistant weeds are plants that survive herbicide treatments that previously controlled them. This resistance is typically linked to biochemical or physiological mechanisms, such as changes at the herbicide target site, enhanced metabolism, reduced translocation, or other traits that help the weed survive a chemical active ingredient.

Zasso works differently. Instead of relying on a chemical active ingredient, Electroherb applies controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation. The treatment damages plant tissues physically, including conductive tissues that help move water and nutrients. Because the mode of action is physical rather than biochemical, a weed that is resistant to glyphosate, paraquat, ACCase inhibitors, ALS inhibitors, or another herbicide group is not automatically protected from electrical treatment.

That does not mean every resistant weed will be controlled equally. Electrical weeding is still affected by plant architecture, root system, growth stage, moisture, density, electrode contact, operating speed, energy delivery, and whether the treatment program includes a second pass or complementary practice. Mature grasses, dense stands, deep-rooted perennials, woody plants, or weeds with poor contact may require slower operation, repeated passes, mowing before treatment, or integration with other tactics.

The safest customer-facing position is that Zasso is a strong non-chemical option for resistant-weed management, especially where chemical programs are losing effectiveness or where customers want another mode of action. It should be sold as part of integrated weed management, not as a guaranteed one-pass cure for all resistant populations.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers facing herbicide resistance, Zasso can add a different control layer that does not depend on the same chemical pathway that failed. This can be valuable for managing escapes, reducing reliance on repeated herbicide applications, supporting organic or residue-conscious programs where accepted, and diversifying weed-control strategies.

A good customer trial should identify the resistant weed species, growth stage, infestation density, soil and moisture conditions, crop or site constraints, speed, number of passes, and regrowth targets. Success should be measured against the customer's real problem: survival after herbicide treatment, seed production, biomass, regrowth, operating cost, safety requirements, and fit with the broader weed-management program.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can help control herbicide-resistant weeds because it uses a physical electrical mode of action rather than relying on the chemical pathway the weed has evolved to resist."

"For resistant weeds, Zasso is best positioned as a non-chemical tool inside integrated weed management, especially for emerged weeds, escapes, and situations where repeated herbicide use is losing effectiveness."

"Performance still depends on species, growth stage, contact quality, speed, moisture, density, and follow-up management, so resistant-weed claims should be validated in the customer's field conditions."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is resistance-proof, guarantees complete control of all resistant weeds, prevents future resistance by itself, or replaces every herbicide program. Electrical treatment is non-selective and field-dependent; it must be matched to the crop or site, target weeds, safety requirements, equipment configuration, operating speed, and follow-up plan.

Avoid broad claims for specific resistant species unless Zasso has field evidence for that species, crop, growth stage, and treatment protocol. For deep-rooted perennials, mature grasses, dense infestations, or weeds with strong regrowth potential, the most credible recommendation may include sequential passes, mowing before treatment, or integration with chemical, mechanical, cultural, or scouting-based tactics where allowed.

Do not disclose confidential customer results, raw trial data, machine settings, control logic, telemetry details, pricing, contract terms, certification plans, or private meeting content unless explicitly approved for external use.
