# Can it reduce spray drift?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can help reduce spray drift when it replaces or reduces a sprayed herbicide application in a suitable weed-control program. Zasso's electrical treatment is not a spray application, so the treatment itself does not create herbicide droplets, vapor drift, spray deposits, or wind-driven off-target movement of herbicide active ingredients. The claim should stay precise: Zasso reduces spray-drift risk only for the chemical spray operation that is avoided, and it still requires its own safety, operating, crop-contact, and fire-risk controls.

## Detailed Answer

Spray drift is the off-target movement of pesticide or herbicide through the air during or shortly after application. It is usually managed through label instructions, weather restrictions, wind-speed limits, droplet size, nozzle choice, boom or release height, buffers, records, and applicator training. Drift can affect neighboring crops, organic fields, sensitive habitats, water, public areas, livestock, people, and property, and it can create regulatory, commercial, legal, and neighbor-relations risk.

Zasso changes that risk profile because the weed-control action is physical rather than chemical. The technology applies controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit. Because no herbicide liquid is sprayed and no herbicide active ingredient is applied by the Zasso treatment, there are no herbicide spray droplets from the treatment itself to move off target.

The strongest customer-facing statement is therefore conditional and operational: Zasso can reduce spray-drift exposure where it replaces a spray pass, reduces the number of herbicide applications, or treats a drift-sensitive area without adding another chemical application. This can be valuable near specialty crops, organic production, orchards, vineyards, vegetables, schools, homes, waterways, conservation areas, field edges, public spaces, roadsides, railways, utilities, and other sites where chemical drift is commercially or socially unacceptable.

This does not mean Zasso eliminates all off-target risk. Electrical weeding is non-selective on plants that are contacted or included in the effective current path. It also introduces different operating considerations, including operator training, safety distance, electrode contact, crop clearance, equipment setup, soil and plant moisture, vegetation density, operating speed, fire-risk conditions, and local rules. Zasso should be positioned as a way to reduce herbicide spray-drift risk in suitable applications, not as a guarantee of zero risk.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical value is risk reduction. If a planned herbicide pass is constrained by wind, buffers, nearby sensitive crops, organic certification concerns, public-area restrictions, water proximity, volatile chemistry, or neighbor complaints, Zasso may offer a non-chemical alternative for the target vegetation.

The economic comparison should include more than the price of the spray pass. In drift-sensitive situations, customers may also care about avoided complaints, avoided crop or residue claims, wider operating flexibility, fewer spray-window constraints, stronger neighbor relations, and the ability to control weeds without adding chemical deposits to adjacent vegetation or waterways.

A good customer plan should define which spray operation Zasso is expected to replace or reduce, which weeds are being targeted, how desirable vegetation will be protected, what operating protocol will be used, and what safety and fire-risk controls apply. The best message is not that Zasso makes every application risk-free; it is that Zasso can remove the spray-drift pathway from the avoided chemical operation.

"This is especially relevant near sensitive crops, organic fields, public areas, waterways, or neighbors where spray drift would be a major concern."

"Zasso removes the chemical spray-drift pathway from the avoided spray operation, while still requiring trained operation and site-specific safety controls."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso eliminates every off-target risk. Electrical weeding can injure desirable vegetation if it is contacted by the electrodes or becomes part of the effective current path.

Do not claim that Zasso automatically replaces all spraying or removes all regulatory obligations. Local rules, product manuals, crop protocols, safety procedures, weather conditions, fire-risk controls, and customer requirements still apply.

Do not use this FAQ as legal advice about drift liability. Drift rules, complaint procedures, compensation, insurance, and enforcement vary by jurisdiction and fact pattern.

Do not overextend the claim to chemical applications that remain in the customer's program. Zasso reduces spray drift only to the extent that it actually replaces or reduces spray operations.
