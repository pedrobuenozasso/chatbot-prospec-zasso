# Does Zasso work after mowing?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work after mowing, and mowing before electrical weeding can sometimes improve results when weeds are tall, dense, tangled, or shielding each other from electrode contact. The key is to leave enough accessible, living plant tissue for the electrodes to contact and for the electrical path to connect with the plant's recovery structures. If mowing removes too much conductive tissue or leaves only very short, dry, or poorly contacted stubble, Zasso performance may be reduced or a follow-up treatment plan may be needed.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology works by transferring controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit. Mowing changes that biological and electrical target. It can make a difficult stand easier to treat by reducing canopy height, opening access to lower stems, reducing plant shielding, and making electrode contact more uniform. This is especially relevant in tall or dense vegetation, where unmowed plants can act like an umbrella and prevent electrodes from reaching lower tissues or smaller plants.

Mowing is not automatically beneficial in every situation. If the mower cuts too close to the ground, removes the upright tissues needed for contact, leaves dry fragments instead of living connected tissue, or separates above-ground biomass from roots and crowns, electrical energy may not reach the plant's recovery tissues as effectively. In those cases, the result may be more like treating stubble or regrowth rather than treating the full plant.

The practical answer is therefore conditional: Zasso can work after mowing when the remaining vegetation is still contactable and the application is timed and configured for the target weed. For dense annual weeds, mowing before treatment may improve contact and reduce biomass. For perennial weeds, grasses, rhizomatous weeds, or plants that regrow from crowns, the best strategy may depend on whether the objective is to weaken regrowth, treat fresh regrowth after mowing, or avoid cutting away the conductive pathway too early.

For customers, mowing should be discussed as part of an application program, not as a universal requirement. The right decision depends on weed species, growth stage, height, density, moisture, mowing height, residue, regrowth potential, electrode design, speed, safety, and follow-up scouting.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers can consider mowing before Zasso treatment when vegetation is too tall, lodged, dense, or tangled for reliable electrode contact. In those conditions, mowing can improve access and make the later electrical pass more consistent.

The mowing height matters. The goal is usually not to scalp the field; it is to prepare the vegetation so the electrodes can still contact living tissue connected to the plant. After mowing, customers should check whether the remaining plants are upright or reachable, whether residue is blocking the electrodes, whether the tissue is still green and conductive, and whether enough regrowth is present for the intended treatment.

For difficult perennial or grass weeds, the best program may involve mowing, allowing suitable regrowth, and then applying Zasso at the right timing, or using sequential electrical passes. Local validation is important because the best mowing-plus-electrical strategy can vary by crop, weed species, site conditions, and product configuration.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can work after mowing, especially when mowing helps open dense vegetation and improves electrode contact. The important point is to leave enough living, connected plant tissue for the electrical treatment to work effectively."

"Mowing before Zasso is a useful preparation tool in some fields, but it should be matched to the weed species, mowing height, regrowth stage, residue level, and application setup."

"For dense, mature, grass, or perennial weeds, we recommend treating mowing and Zasso as a planned program with follow-up scouting, not as a one-size-fits-all rule."

## Caveats

Do not claim that mowing before Zasso always improves results, that Zasso always works equally well on freshly scalped stubble, or that mowing plus one electrical pass guarantees root kill. Mowing can help contact in tall or dense vegetation, but it can also remove the conductive plant structure needed for efficient treatment if done too low, too late, or under dry conditions.

Avoid universal mowing-height, waiting-period, speed, power, or pass-number recommendations without product-, crop-, and weed-specific guidance. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, customer-specific data, unpublished trial details, control logic, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
