# Can someone touch the electrodes after shutdown?

## Short Answer

Not automatically. People should not touch Zasso electrodes just because the machine has stopped or the high-voltage output has been switched off. Electrode contact should be allowed only after the equipment has been shut down, de-energized, discharged where applicable, and verified safe according to the approved Zasso manual and training for that specific product.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso electrical weeding equipment uses high-voltage energy to treat plants through electrodes or applicators and the intended plant-soil pathway. During operation, electrodes are part of the active energy-transfer system. After shutdown, they must still be treated as safety-critical parts until the product-specific procedure confirms that the machine is in a safe state.

The reason is simple: stopping forward movement, pressing a stop control, switching off high-voltage output, or hearing the machine become quiet does not by itself prove that all relevant energy has been removed. Depending on the product architecture and condition, there may be residual stored energy, fault conditions, wet or conductive material, energized metal or vegetation, damaged insulation, unexpected restart risk, or a need for a formal discharge and verification step before anyone touches electrodes, cables, applicators, conductive guards, or nearby conductive objects.

For normal operators, customers, visitors, and bystanders, the practical rule is: do not touch the electrodes. If inspection, cleaning, clearing, transport preparation, maintenance, or service requires electrode contact, it should be done only by trained and authorized personnel following the approved shutdown, isolation, discharge, PPE, lockout or securing, and verification steps for the specific machine.

This answer should not be interpreted as saying electrodes remain dangerous forever after shutdown. It means safe contact is not assumed from shutdown alone. The safe condition must be created and confirmed using the relevant Zasso procedure.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should build a simple field habit: no one touches electrodes until the equipment has been made safe according to the manual and training. This applies after normal shutdown, after an emergency stop, after an alarm, after a blockage, after contact with metal or water, after visible damage, after unusual sparking, and before cleaning or maintenance.

Operators should control the area during shutdown just as carefully as during operation. Bystanders, visitors, animals, and untrained workers should stay away from the implement until the trained operator or service person confirms the safe-access condition. If there is any uncertainty about discharge, fault state, insulation condition, wet surfaces, conductive objects, or restart risk, the correct action is to keep clear and involve trained Zasso support or qualified service personnel.

The customer benefit is a safer and more repeatable operating routine: stop the application, follow the approved shutdown procedure, confirm the machine is safe, and only then allow trained access for the task that requires contact.

## Caveats

Do not publish product-specific discharge times, stored-energy values, voltage or current thresholds, service steps, lockout details, PPE ratings, interlock logic, fault-detection thresholds, or certification claims unless they come from approved customer-facing Zasso documentation.

Do not tell customers that electrodes are safe to touch immediately after shutdown. Do not imply that an emergency stop, stopped motion, dry soil, ordinary shoes, a quiet machine, or no visible spark proves that electrode contact is safe.

Requirements can vary by product, power architecture, applicator, operating mode, soil moisture, wet vegetation, nearby metal, water, fault state, damage condition, local regulation, and whether the activity is inspection, cleaning, clearing, transport, maintenance, or service. The approved Zasso manual and training always take priority.
