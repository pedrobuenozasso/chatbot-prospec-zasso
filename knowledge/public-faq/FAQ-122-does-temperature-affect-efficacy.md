# Does temperature affect efficacy?

## Short Answer

Yes, temperature can affect Zasso efficacy indirectly, but it should not be treated as a simple hot-versus-cold rule. Temperature influences plant stress, plant water content, soil moisture, surface drying, fire risk, and the practical application window. In suitable conditions, Zasso performance depends more directly on contact quality, weed stage, plant and soil moisture, speed, dose, and safety conditions than on air temperature alone.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding works by delivering electrical energy through contacted plant tissue and the plant-soil system. Air temperature matters because it is part of the wider weather and field-condition picture. Hot weather can dry plants, soil, and residue, increase plant stress, reduce plant water content, and raise fire risk when dry biomass is present. Cool or mild weather may coincide with greener, more hydrated weeds and safer residue conditions, but low temperature alone does not guarantee better control.

The best way to explain temperature is as an operating condition that interacts with moisture and biology. A hot, dry afternoon with stressed weeds, dry residue, wind, and low soil moisture is very different from a mild morning after moderate moisture. Likewise, a cool day with saturated soil, wet residue, mud, or poor electrode contact may still be a poor application window.

Temperature also influences how quickly symptoms appear and how plants recover after treatment. Warmer growing conditions can support faster regrowth from surviving crowns, roots, rhizomes, bulbs, or protected nodes if the original dose was insufficient. Cooler conditions may slow visible growth and recovery, which can affect scouting interpretation. For this reason, follow-up assessment should consider the weather after treatment, not only the weather during treatment.

Customers should not be given a universal temperature threshold unless it comes from the specific product manual, local training, or validated local operating procedure. The practical recommendation is to evaluate temperature together with humidity, wind, soil moisture, plant water content, weed stage, residue dryness, field access, contact quality, speed, and local safety rules.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should include temperature in the pre-application field check, especially when hot weather is combined with dry vegetation, dry residue, wind, drought, or high fire-danger conditions. In those situations, the safest and most credible advice may be to postpone treatment, adjust the operating plan, or use another weed-management tactic.

Temperature should also guide expectations after application. If conditions are warm and favorable for growth, surviving perennial weeds or protected growing points may recover more quickly and need follow-up scouting. If conditions are cool, visible response and regrowth may develop more slowly, so assessment timing should be interpreted carefully.

For commercial discussions, the main point is that Zasso is a field-operation technology, not a weather-proof magic switch. Good performance comes from matching the machine, speed, electrode contact, weed stage, moisture conditions, safety procedures, and follow-up plan to the actual field environment.

Public evidence is consistent with this position. The same paper notes that meteorological conditions influence both efficacy and fire risk, and that the technology is not suitable in hot conditions where plant residue is dry: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/electric-weed-controlhow-does-it-compare-to-conventional-weed-control-methods/488C45B191B97E84E34257E80EB9CFC6

Oregon State University Extension guidance for electrical weed control in organic blueberry fields emphasizes slower speed, good electrode contact, soil moisture, and practical safety precautions. It specifically advises avoiding application over dry vegetation or on windy, hot days, and notes that slightly moist soil can provide better conductivity while efficiency can drop if soil is too dry or too wet: https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9716-how-speed-timing-affect-electrical-weed-control-organic-blueberry-fields

DPIRD public material on soil moisture and electric weed control using Zasso equipment concludes that soil moisture can affect efficiency and may require speed adjustment in wet conditions. This supports explaining temperature together with moisture and application speed rather than as an isolated variable: https://library.dpird.wa.gov.au/conf_papers/299/

A 2025 Weed Research study on electrocution-induced plant temperature effects reports that internal plant temperature increase is a major factor in early-stage weed injury. That supports the mechanism that electrical treatment causes internal heating and tissue damage, while still leaving field air temperature as an indirect operating factor rather than a simple efficacy threshold: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/wre.70031

## Safe Sales Wording

"Temperature can matter, but mostly because it changes the field environment around the application. We look at heat together with plant moisture, soil moisture, wind, dry residue, weed stage, contact quality, and safety conditions before recommending the treatment window."

"There is no single air-temperature rule that predicts Zasso performance in every field. The goal is to apply when the weeds can receive the right electrical dose and when the operation is safe for the crop, operator, equipment, and site conditions."

"In hot, dry, windy, or high-fire-risk conditions, we recommend a conservative field assessment and following the applicable product manual and local operating procedures."

## Caveats

Do not claim that high temperature always improves or reduces efficacy. Do not claim that cold weather always improves or reduces efficacy. Temperature interacts with soil moisture, plant water content, residue dryness, humidity, wind, weed species, weed stage, root system, density, contact quality, equipment setup, and speed.

Do not provide a universal maximum or minimum temperature threshold unless it is taken from the applicable Zasso product manual, local safety procedure, or validated local recommendation. Fire-risk, safety, and operational limits may depend on local regulation, crop environment, residue condition, and product configuration.

Do not promise one-pass control, guaranteed root kill, or fixed regrowth behavior based only on temperature. Warm growing conditions may speed recovery of surviving plants, while cool conditions may slow symptom development or regrowth, so follow-up scouting remains important.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, internal safety thresholds, private customer results, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
