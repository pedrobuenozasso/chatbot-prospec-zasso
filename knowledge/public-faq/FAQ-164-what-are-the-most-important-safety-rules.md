# What are the most important safety rules?

## Short Answer

The most important safety rules are: use Zasso equipment only with trained and authorized operators, keep people and animals outside the active safety zone, never touch electrodes or conductive parts while the machine may be energized, stop work for abnormal conditions, and follow the approved shutdown, discharge, PPE, inspection, and maintenance procedures for the specific product. Because Zasso technology uses high-voltage electrical energy, it should be treated as professional equipment, not as a casual farm tool. The exact rules can vary by machine, country, application, and site, so the approved Zasso manual and training always take priority.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso electrical weeding works by applying controlled high-voltage energy through applicators or electrodes into target plants and the intended plant-soil pathway. The core safety objective is to keep that energy in the intended treatment circuit and prevent people, animals, metal objects, water, damaged insulation, tools, crop infrastructure, machine structures, or bystanders from becoming part of an unintended electrical path.

The first rule is trained operation. Only trained and authorized people should operate, inspect, clean, transport, or service the equipment, and they should use the product exactly as described in the approved manual and training. Operators should understand the danger zone, startup sequence, alarms, emergency stop, shutdown steps, required PPE, and the conditions that require stopping work.

The second rule is exclusion-zone control. People, animals, visitors, workers, tools, vehicles, and unauthorized personnel must stay outside the active treatment area and away from electrodes, applicators, cables, conductive vegetation, wet surfaces, fences, irrigation components, trellises, metal objects, and other possible conductive paths. If someone enters the safety zone or site control is lost, the conservative action is to stop high-voltage output and follow the approved procedure before continuing.

The third rule is no contact unless the machine has been made safe. No one should touch electrodes, applicators, cables, connectors, guards, crop wires, metal objects, or conductive material that may be connected to the treatment circuit while high voltage may be active or before the shutdown, discharge, securing, PPE, and verification procedure has been completed. Stopping forward motion, pressing a stop control, or hearing the machine become quiet is not the same as confirming a safe state.

The fourth rule is stop for abnormal conditions. Operators should stop and reassess if there are alarms, damaged cables or insulation, broken electrodes, unusual sparking, active irrigation, puddles, heavy wet contamination, metal contact, unknown buried utilities, animals or bystanders nearby, high fire-risk dry biomass, unsafe weather, blocked applicators, abnormal machine behavior, or uncertainty about the site. Work should resume only when the condition has been controlled according to the manual and local rules.

The fifth rule is maintenance discipline. Before inspection, cleaning, clearing, adjustment, or maintenance, the equipment should be shut down, isolated or secured against restart where applicable, discharged and verified safe according to the product-specific procedure, and handled only by trained people using the required PPE. Safety-critical parts such as electrodes, insulation, cables, guards, grounding or return-path components, sensors, warning devices, emergency stops, and fault systems should be inspected and maintained as instructed.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should turn these rules into a simple operating culture: trained people only, controlled area, no approach or contact during application, stop for anything abnormal, and follow the exact shutdown and maintenance procedure. These rules are especially important for farms, municipalities, contractors, distributors, and demonstration teams that may work around visitors, workers, animals, crop infrastructure, irrigation, roadsides, or public access.

For supervisors, the practical takeaway is to build safety into planning before the machine is energized. That includes assigning a trained operator, checking the site, defining the exclusion zone, keeping animals and bystanders away, confirming PPE, inspecting the machine, knowing the emergency stop and shutdown process, and stopping work when conditions are outside the approved operating envelope.

For operators, the practical takeaway is equally direct: do not improvise around high voltage. If something is unclear, damaged, wet, metallic, crowded, burning, sparking, alarming, blocked, or outside training, stop and follow the approved Zasso procedure or call qualified support.

## Caveats

Do not present these rules as a substitute for the approved Zasso manual, training, local risk assessment, or local regulation. Product-specific requirements may vary by machine, applicator, country, certification, operating mode, crop, soil, moisture, weather, nearby infrastructure, public access, fire risk, maintenance state, and task.

Do not claim that Zasso equipment is completely safe, safe to touch, safe for untrained users, safe without PPE where required, safe around all water or metal objects, safe in all weather, safe without a safety distance, or safe after shutdown without verification.

Do not disclose product-specific safety distances, discharge times, voltage or current thresholds, PPE ratings, interlock logic, sensor thresholds, fault-detection behavior, test results, certification details, or service procedures unless they are already approved for customer-facing use.
