# What happens inside the plant during treatment?

## Short Answer

During Zasso electrical weeding, the treated plant becomes part of a controlled electrical circuit. Current moves through conductive plant tissues, and the plant's natural electrical resistance converts part of that energy into heat and cellular stress. This can rupture cells, injure membranes, damage xylem and phloem, interrupt water and nutrient transport, and lead to wilting, dry-down, and plant death when the dose and conditions are suitable.

## Detailed Answer

Inside the plant, the treatment is not just a surface burn. The applicator contacts above-ground plant tissue, but the electrical pathway can extend through leaves, stems, vascular bundles, crowns, roots, soil, and the return path. Living plant tissue contains water and dissolved ions, so it conducts electricity, while still resisting current flow. That resistance is what converts part of the electrical energy into heat inside the plant.

At cell level, the heat and electrical stress can damage cell membranes and cell walls. As internal fluids heat and expand, cells can rupture or lose their ability to regulate water and ion balance. Damage to the vascular tissues is especially important because xylem and phloem move water, nutrients, and sugars between the roots and shoots. If those pathways are sufficiently injured, the plant loses the ability to maintain normal metabolism and hydration.

The most useful treatment effect is therefore internal and functional: the plant can no longer move water and nutrients properly, growing tissues may be damaged, and recovery structures such as crowns, meristems, roots, rhizomes, or runners may lose viability. This is why visible wilting or browning is only part of the story. A plant may look damaged quickly, but durable control depends on whether enough useful energy reached the tissues that allow regrowth.

The outcome depends on weed species, growth stage, plant water content, root size, canopy density, electrode contact, operating speed, delivered energy, soil moisture, soil conductivity, and the current path. Broadleaf plants, grasses, young weeds, mature perennials, dense canopies, and plants with protected growing points can respond differently. For that reason, Zasso should describe the process as a controlled physical mode of action with condition-dependent performance, not as automatic instant death in every plant.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical value is that Zasso targets the plant's internal function rather than applying a chemical active ingredient. The technology can help reduce reliance on herbicides, avoid chemical residues and spray drift, and support integrated weed-management programs where electrical weeding is suitable.

Customers should evaluate results over time. Early visual symptoms can include wilting, darkening, collapse, or dry-down, but regrowth checks over the following days and weeks are important, especially for perennials, grasses, rhizomatous weeds, dense infestations, or difficult field conditions. Correct product selection, trained operation, good electrode contact, suitable speed, and field-condition assessment are all part of achieving reliable results.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Inside the plant, Zasso's electrical treatment damages the tissues that keep the plant alive. The current creates heat and cellular stress in conductive tissues, disrupting water and nutrient transport so the plant can wilt, dry down, and lose its ability to recover."

"The goal is not only to brown the leaves. Effective electrical weeding depends on delivering enough useful energy into the plant's internal pathways, including crown and root-zone recovery tissues where relevant."

"Results depend on the weed, field conditions, contact quality, speed, and trained operation, so Zasso recommends treating electrical weeding as a professional tool within an integrated weed-management plan."

## Caveats

Do not claim that every cell, root, rhizome, or meristem is always destroyed. The treatment can damage internal tissues and recovery pathways, but the degree of control depends on dose, contact, plant structure, moisture, soil conditions, speed, and application protocol.

Do not describe the process as only burning leaves, and do not describe it as risk-free. Electrical weeding uses high voltage and must be operated with appropriate training, safety procedures, maintenance, and attention to fire risk in dry biomass.

Do not disclose confidential power settings, control algorithms, circuit designs, telemetry details, unpublished test data, customer-specific results, certification details, pricing, or sensitive engineering information.
