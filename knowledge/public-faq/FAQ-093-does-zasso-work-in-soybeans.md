# Does Zasso work in soybeans?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can be relevant for soybeans, but the fit depends on the application window, equipment configuration, crop stage, weed height, and whether the electrodes can contact the weeds without contacting the crop. Electrical weeding is non-selective, so in soybean fields it is best discussed for pre-plant or stale-seedbed use, inter-row concepts, or escaped weeds that can be targeted safely. Zasso should not be presented as a universal in-crop soybean solution unless the specific machine, row spacing, timing, and field protocol have been locally validated.

## Detailed Answer

Soybean is a row crop where electrical weeding can make agronomic sense in selected situations, especially when growers need non-chemical tools for herbicide-resistant or escaped weeds. The key point is selectivity. Zasso's Electroherb technology controls contacted vegetation through electrical energy; it does not chemically distinguish soybean plants from weeds. Therefore, the crop must be protected by timing, geometry, height difference, shielding, inter-row positioning, or another validated application strategy.

For soybean systems, three use cases are the most credible. First, electrical weeding can be considered before crop emergence or before planting, where crop contact is not an issue. Second, it may be used in inter-row or adjacent-row scenarios where equipment is designed and operated to treat weeds between rows while avoiding soybean plants. Third, height-selective or escape-weed approaches can target weeds that grow above the soybean canopy, although this is most relevant when the weeds are already large enough to contact the electrode and before they add more seed to the seedbank.

Published soybean research with electrical weed control shows useful but conditional results. University of Missouri work using a shock-discharge system in soybean found strong potential against late-season weed escapes and reduced weed seed viability, but also showed that soybean contact can cause yield loss and that some grasses or early-stage weeds may recover. Earlier Brazilian work in organic soybean using electrical discharge reported high weed control under tested conditions, with stronger results at higher delivered power. These studies support the idea that electrical weeding can work in soybean programs, but they do not remove the need for crop-safety design, correct timing, and field-specific validation.

For Zasso specifically, current internal and public positioning supports row crops as an important development and market direction, including higher-power and inter-row platforms. Public and research reporting also describes proof-of-concept work with continuous-contact systems near corn and soybean rows, including Zasso-linked equipment, but this remains an application-specific row-crop development area rather than a blanket commercial promise for every soybean field.

## What This Means for Customers

For soybean growers, Zasso should be positioned as a potential tool inside integrated weed management, especially where herbicide resistance, organic production, residue concerns, or escaped weeds create pressure for non-chemical options. The customer benefit is not simply replacing a herbicide pass; it is adding a physical mode of action that can reduce weed biomass, suppress escapes, and help limit future seed return when the treatment is correctly timed and targeted.

A good soybean discussion should start with the customer's production system: conventional or organic, row spacing, weed species, weed stage, crop stage, canopy height, residue level, soil and plant moisture, field speed target, safety constraints, and acceptable crop-contact risk. If soybean plants are likely to be contacted, the risk of crop injury must be treated seriously. If the goal is inter-row control, the applicator must be matched to the row geometry and validated locally.

The most credible sales posture is practical and conditional: Zasso can contribute to soybean weed management where the application is designed around crop protection and weed contact. It should not be sold as an unrestricted over-the-top soybean treatment.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can be relevant in soybean weed-management programs where the application is designed to contact weeds while protecting the crop, such as pre-emergence, inter-row, or escaped-weed scenarios."

"Because electrical weeding is a physical and non-selective mode of action, soybean applications must be validated around row spacing, crop stage, weed height, applicator geometry, and operator protocol."

"For soybeans, we see the strongest fit as part of integrated weed management, especially for herbicide-resistant or escaped weeds where a non-chemical mode of action adds value."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is a universal over-the-top soybean herbicide replacement. Do not imply soybean plants can be safely contacted unless the specific product, crop stage, timing, and protocol have been validated for that use. Avoid promising one-pass control, no crop injury, guaranteed yield protection, complete seedbank control, or broadacre cost competitiveness in every soybean system.

Soybean results depend on row spacing, crop stage, canopy height, weed height, weed species, plant moisture, soil moisture, soil conductivity, residue, speed, electrode contact, applicator shielding or geometry, pass strategy, and local safety requirements. Grasses and early-stage weeds may be more difficult in some systems, and large weed escapes may already have caused crop competition before treatment.

Do not disclose confidential product-roadmap details, machine settings, pricing, customer-specific trial data, private meeting content, or non-public commercial timelines. When discussing future row-crop systems, use careful wording such as "under development," "where validated," or "in suitable applications."
