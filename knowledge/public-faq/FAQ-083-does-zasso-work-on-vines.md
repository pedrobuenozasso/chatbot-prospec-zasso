# Does Zasso work on vines?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work in vine-related situations, but the answer depends on what the customer means by "vines." Zasso has public vineyard evidence for controlling weeds under or beside grapevines when the equipment is configured to contact the unwanted vegetation while respecting the crop vine. If the target is a vining or climbing weed, performance depends on species, stem age, moisture, electrode contact, plant density, and regrowth biology, so local validation is recommended before making performance commitments.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology works through direct contact between electrodes and unwanted plants. The contacted plant becomes part of an electrical pathway, and the treatment can damage above-ground tissue and, when conditions are suitable, connected recovery tissues. This makes the technology relevant for many weeds found in vineyard, orchard, and perennial-crop systems, including weeds that grow near vine trunks or trellis structures, provided the applicator can reach the target without contacting the crop.

For vineyards, the practical question is usually under-vine weed control, not killing the grapevine itself. Zasso's XPS concept is designed for vineyards and orchards, with applicator geometry intended to treat weeds beside the inter-row area and around vine or tree stems while respecting crop integrity. Public Zasso and external research material support the view that electrical weed control can be a serious non-chemical tool in vineyards when timing, weed cover, speed, soil condition, and safety conditions are appropriate.

If the target is an unwanted vining plant, such as a climbing, trailing, or creeping weed, the answer should be more cautious. Vining weeds may have long stems, multiple contact points, woody or semi-woody tissue, protected buds, rhizomes, crowns, or large underground reserves. Some may be susceptible when green, young, moist, and well contacted; others may regrow after visible top-growth injury. Dense tangles can also shield stems from electrode contact or increase the risk of contact with nearby crop plants, posts, wires, or other structures.

For sales purposes, the safest position is that Zasso can be evaluated for vining weeds and is already relevant to weed control in vineyard systems, but it should not be sold as a universal vine-eradication method. The correct protocol should define the target species, whether the crop vine must be protected, growth stage, plant density, electrode access, operating speed, number of passes, safety constraints, and regrowth observation period.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers in vineyards may find Zasso attractive where they want to reduce herbicide reliance, avoid chemical residues or spray drift, reduce soil disturbance, and manage weeds in under-vine or near-stem zones. The value is strongest where the weed band is visible, contactable, and treated early enough that Zasso is not being used to catch up on excessive weed cover.

Customers targeting vining weeds should plan a local field test. The test should separate short-term visible injury from durable control, because vining and perennial plants may regrow from crowns, nodes, stolons, rhizomes, or roots. Follow-up scouting after treatment is important, and some situations may need sequential passes or integration with mowing, cutting, cultivation, herbicides, or other vegetation-management tools.

Crop safety also matters. Zasso is not selective if it contacts a valuable crop vine or other desired plant. In vineyards or trellised crops, applicator setup, guidance, crop architecture, operator training, and avoidance of crop contact are essential parts of the customer discussion.

Those answers support a consistent message: Zasso can be effective where target plants are contacted and the application protocol is matched to plant size, morphology, soil condition, speed, and regrowth risk, but more caution is needed for plants with woody tissue, larger reserves, protected buds, or difficult contact geometry.

The IFV material reported strong vineyard weed-control results under favorable conditions, emphasized early-season anticipation rather than catching up on neglected weed cover, and reported no observed vine damage in the tested conditions. The 2025 Weed Science vineyard study evaluated Zasso XPower/XPS in four vineyard sites and found weed biomass reduction comparable to herbicide under the tested spring conditions, with no evidence of damage to grapevine canopy development, while also identifying dry biomass as a fire-risk concern.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can be used in vineyard systems for weeds under or beside vines when the equipment is configured to contact the weeds while protecting the crop vine."

"For vining or climbing weeds, we recommend local validation by species and growth stage, because contact quality and regrowth biology can strongly affect results."

"Zasso is best positioned as a non-chemical tool in a weed-management program, not as a universal one-pass solution for every vine-like plant."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso safely contacts valuable grapevines, crop vines, or trellised crops unless the specific product setup and use case have been validated and approved. Zasso affects plants that are contacted and included in the electrical pathway.

Do not claim universal control of all vining weeds, climbing weeds, creeping weeds, woody vines, rhizomatous vines, or established perennial vines. Avoid promises of permanent eradication, guaranteed root kill, or one-pass control unless there is species-specific and site-specific evidence.

Vining weeds may be difficult when stems are woody, dry, shielded, tangled, growing through crop structures, or connected to large underground reserves. Dense or dry biomass may also raise operational and fire-risk concerns, so local safety rules and Zasso operating guidance must be followed.

Do not disclose confidential product settings, unpublished trial details, customer names, pricing, certification timelines, private meeting content, or sensitive technical parameters.
