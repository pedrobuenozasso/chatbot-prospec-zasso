# What causes regrowth?

## Short Answer

Regrowth happens when enough living recovery tissue survives after treatment. The main causes are weed biology, incomplete energy delivery, poor or uneven electrode contact, too little contact time, dense or shielded vegetation, challenging moisture or soil conditions, and treating plants at a stage where roots, crowns, rhizomes, tubers, runners, or tillers still have enough reserves to recover. Zasso is designed to reduce regrowth by delivering electrical energy through the plant, but no responsible claim should promise zero regrowth in every species, field, or operating condition.

## Detailed Answer

Regrowth is usually not caused by one single factor. It occurs when the treatment damages visible shoots but does not sufficiently disable the plant structures that can restart growth. These structures may include crowns, basal growing points, rhizomes, tubers, bulbs, stolons, runners, tillers, woody tissues, or surviving root systems. Perennial weeds, grasses, vines, woody plants, and mature weeds are more likely to regrow because they often have protected growing points and stored reserves.

In electrical weeding, the treatment result depends on whether useful electrical energy reaches the tissues that matter for recovery. Good results require the target plant to be part of the electrical circuit, with enough electrode contact, contact time, delivered energy, and an effective current path through the plant and return circuit. If the machine travels too fast, if the electrodes only touch the upper canopy, if plants are hidden under larger weeds, or if the current path bypasses important recovery tissues, the visible top growth may collapse while part of the plant remains viable.

Field conditions also matter. Soil moisture, plant moisture, soil conductivity, crop residue, mulch, terrain, plant density, and weed stage can all influence contact and current flow. Dense stands can split available energy across many plants and shield smaller weeds. Wet or highly conductive situations can change where current flows. Very dry, woody, or lignified tissues may be harder to contact or treat consistently. Mowing can sometimes improve access in layered vegetation, but it can also reduce the conductive stem pathway to deeper recovery structures in some grasses or deep-rooted perennials.

The practical answer is that regrowth is caused by survival plus recovery opportunity. A plant can regrow when enough growing tissue survives and field conditions allow it to rebuild shoots. The best management response is to treat regrowth risk as part of the application plan: identify the weed species and growth stage, ensure good contact, adjust speed and pass strategy, scout after treatment, and use a second pass or integrated weed-management step where the target weed or field condition requires it.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should judge treatment success over days and weeks, not only by the first visible burn-down or wilting. A fast visual response is encouraging, but the more important question is whether the weed stays suppressed through the expected regrowth window.

For small annual weeds with accessible green tissue, regrowth risk may be relatively low when contact and operating conditions are good. For perennial weeds, grasses, dense patches, tall or layered vegetation, mature plants, woody plants, or weeds with underground storage organs, customers should plan follow-up scouting and be ready to adjust the program.

In practical terms, this means Zasso should be sold as a controlled, evidence-based application system rather than a universal one-pass guarantee. Strong performance depends on matching the treatment protocol to the weed, the field, the machine configuration, and the customer's operational goals.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso eliminates all regrowth, permanently kills every root, or guarantees one-pass control in all conditions. Regrowth risk is species-specific and field-specific, and it can be increased by protected growing points, underground reserves, large or mature plants, dense or layered vegetation, poor contact, high travel speed, unsuitable moisture conditions, difficult soil conditions, or incomplete current path through recovery tissues.

Avoid universal recommendations for pass number, speed, timing, dose, or mowing strategy unless Zasso has approved guidance for the specific product, crop, weed, region, and field condition. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, control logic, unpublished trial data, customer-specific outcomes, pricing, certification details, or private roadmap information.
