# Does Zasso work in vineyards?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work in vineyards when the application is matched to the vineyard layout, weed stage, soil and plant conditions, and safety requirements. Vineyards are one of Zasso's clearest public use cases: Zasso's XPS solution is positioned for vineyards and orchards, and public vineyard trials have reported strong under-vine weed-control results under suitable conditions. The safest claim is that Zasso is a serious non-chemical tool for vineyard weed management, not a universal one-pass solution for every vineyard, weed pressure, season, slope, or operating condition.

## Detailed Answer

Vineyards are a strong fit for Zasso because they often require repeated under-vine or near-vine weed control, while growers may want to reduce herbicide reliance, avoid spray drift, protect soil structure, support organic or low-residue production, and limit crop-root disturbance from mechanical tools. Zasso's Electroherb technology controls contacted vegetation through electrical energy, so the practical vineyard question is whether the applicator can consistently contact the unwanted vegetation while respecting the grapevine, trellis, posts, irrigation lines, and other infrastructure.

Zasso's XPS concept was developed for vineyard and orchard geometry. Public Zasso product material describes fixed and movable electrodes designed to treat weeds beside the inter-row area and around vine or fruit-tree stems while respecting crop integrity. That makes the technology relevant for under-vine weed control, especially in vineyards where weed growth is visible, reachable, and managed before it becomes excessive.

Field evidence supports a positive but bounded answer. Zasso's public IFV vineyard validation material reported that 2020 tests in France achieved strong weed-control performance under optimal conditions, with effectiveness similar to chemical weeding in the tested setup, no reported vine damage, and no observed negative earthworm-population effect in that season's assessment. The same material also emphasized that electric weeding works best as an anticipation tool: early-season management and moderate weed coverage are easier than trying to recover a vineyard already carrying heavy or mature weed growth.

More recent peer-reviewed work also supports vineyard relevance. A Weed Science study evaluated a Zasso XPower machine with XPS applicators in four vineyard sites in Western Australia during 2022 and 2023, comparing electric weed control with mowing and herbicide applications. The study found substantial weed biomass reduction under the tested spring conditions and reported no evidence of damage to grapevine canopy development based on NDVI observations, while also warning that dry plant biomass can create fire risk.

## What This Means for Customers

For vineyard customers, Zasso can be positioned as a non-chemical weed-control option inside an integrated vineyard-floor management program. It may be especially relevant where herbicide reduction, organic production, residue-sensitive marketing, soil conservation, spray-drift avoidance, or weed resistance pressure are important.

A good vineyard evaluation should look at row spacing, under-vine band width, trellis and post layout, slope, stones, irrigation equipment, crop age, canopy and shoot position, weed species, weed height, weed density, plant moisture, soil moisture, residue, dry biomass, operating speed, number of passes, operator training, safety distance, and fire-risk conditions. Customers should measure not only the visible effect after treatment, but also regrowth, crop safety, operating speed, labor need, economics, and compatibility with the vineyard's broader floor-management strategy.

The strongest vineyard fit is usually early and preventive: treating weeds while they are contactable and before dense dry biomass or large mature plants make the job slower, riskier, or less consistent. In some vineyards, Zasso may reduce or replace selected herbicide passes; in others, it may complement mowing, cultivation, cover crops, mulching, or targeted herbicide use.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is selective to grapevines. Electrical weeding is contact-based; crop tissue, shoots, trunks, irrigation components, metal posts, wires, or other infrastructure must be protected according to the product setup and operating protocol.

Do not claim universal vineyard efficacy, permanent control, one-pass control, full herbicide replacement, guaranteed cost savings, or suitability in every slope, soil, row spacing, weed species, growth stage, weather condition, or fire-risk condition. Performance depends on contact quality, weed size, weed density, plant and soil moisture, soil conductivity, biomass dryness, operating speed, electrode setup, product configuration, safety procedures, and local regulations.

Be especially careful in dry conditions or where dry biomass is present. Public vineyard research identifies dry plant material as a fire-risk concern, so local fire rules, Zasso operating guidance, and operator training must be followed.
