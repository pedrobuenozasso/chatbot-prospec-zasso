# Is it compatible with no-till systems?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can be compatible with no-till systems when it is used in a way that supports the farm's soil-cover, residue, traffic, and crop-management goals. Its main fit is that it controls contacted vegetation without tilling, cultivating, inverting, or mixing the soil. Compatibility still depends on the crop, weed stage, cover-crop strategy, residue level, soil moisture, equipment configuration, traffic management, and local validation.

## Detailed Answer

No-till systems are built around minimizing soil disturbance while maintaining soil cover and managing crop rotations, residues, weeds, pests, fertility, and traffic in a coordinated way. Weed control is often one of the hardest parts of no-till management because the grower is trying to avoid soil-moving cultivation while still preventing weeds or cover crops from competing with the cash crop.

Zasso's electrical weeding can fit this logic because the weed-control action does not rely on soil inversion, cultivation, hoeing, or uprooting. The system applies electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit, so it can treat emerged weeds while leaving the soil surface largely undisturbed compared with tillage or cultivation. That makes it relevant for customers who want a non-chemical weed-control option without moving the soil.

The fit is strongest where Zasso replaces or reduces a soil-disturbing weed-control pass, supports cover-crop termination concepts, helps manage escapes in conservation-oriented systems, or gives organic and low-chemical growers another tool where herbicides or cultivation are undesirable. It can be especially relevant in permanent crops, inter-row zones, organic systems, residue-sensitive systems, and fields where erosion or soil-structure protection matters.

No-till compatibility is not automatic in every situation. Zasso is non-selective on contacted vegetation, so it must be targeted carefully where living cover, crop seedlings, habitat strips, or desired ground cover should remain. Heavy residue, dense mulch, lodged biomass, uneven soil surface, or poor electrode contact can reduce treatment consistency. As with any field operation, tractor or carrier traffic can still create compaction risk if soil is wet or traffic is poorly managed.

The safest customer-facing position is that Zasso is no-till-compatible in suitable applications because it can control emerged vegetation without mechanical soil disturbance, but it should be integrated into the no-till system rather than presented as a complete no-till program by itself.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers using no-till, Zasso can provide another weed-control option between herbicide spraying and soil-disturbing cultivation. It may help preserve residue cover, reduce erosion exposure, avoid bringing buried weed seeds to the surface, and reduce selected chemical use where the agronomic program supports it.

Customers should plan applications around the specific no-till objective. If the goal is to terminate a cover crop, the customer should validate species response, biomass level, regrowth risk, timing before planting, and whether a second pass or complementary practice is needed. If the goal is weed escape control, the customer should confirm that weeds are reachable by the electrodes without unacceptable crop contact.

Good implementation also requires normal no-till discipline: protect residue cover, avoid unnecessary passes, manage traffic lanes and soil moisture, scout for regrowth, avoid treating desired vegetation, and compare results against the customer's current herbicide, mowing, rolling, or cultivation program.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso automatically makes a farm no-till, guarantees improved soil health, or works in every no-till field. No-till performance depends on the whole system, including crop rotation, residue, cover crops, weed pressure, soil moisture, soil type, traffic, planting equipment, and local management.

Do not imply that Zasso is selective to crops or desired cover. Direct contact with crop plants, cover crops, habitat strips, or other intended vegetation can injure them, so treatment zones and timing must be planned carefully.

Do not ignore residue and contact issues. Heavy residue, lodged biomass, thick mulch, uneven terrain, or poor electrode contact may reduce consistency and may require adapted equipment settings, timing, or complementary practices.

Do not claim that Zasso eliminates compaction risk. The electrical treatment is not a compaction mechanism, but tractor or carrier traffic still needs normal no-till traffic management, especially on wet soils.
