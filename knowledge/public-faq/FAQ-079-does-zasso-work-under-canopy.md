# Does Zasso work under canopy?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work under a crop canopy when the target weeds are emerged, reachable, and contacted by the electrodes without contacting the crop. The key limitation is physical access: electrical weeding is a contact-based technology, so weeds hidden by leaves, stems, residue, low branches, uneven terrain, or dense crop growth may receive little or no treatment. Under-canopy use should therefore be positioned as a suitable-conditions application, not as a guarantee of controlling every weed beneath a closed canopy.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology works by delivering electrical energy through contacted plant tissue and the plant-soil electrical path. For under-canopy weed control, the practical question is whether the applicator can reach the unwanted vegetation and maintain enough electrode contact while avoiding direct contact with the crop.

In perennial crops, orchards, vineyards, citrus, coffee, and similar systems, this can be a useful fit when weeds are growing in accessible strips or under-tree/under-vine zones and the equipment is configured for that geometry. The same principle can apply in row crops before the canopy closes or where the target weeds are in an open inter-row band. If the crop canopy is dense, low, tangled, or physically shielding the weeds, performance can drop because the electrodes may ride over foliage, miss lower weeds, or contact the crop rather than the target.

Under-canopy use also raises crop-safety and operational questions. Electrical weeding is not chemically selective; it affects plants that are contacted and included in the electrical pathway. For that reason, machine setup, applicator width, electrode height, guidance, crop architecture, travel speed, soil condition, weed height, and operator training matter. The safest sales position is that Zasso can treat contactable weeds under canopy in validated applications, while shielded or inaccessible weeds may need another pass, a different applicator setup, timing before canopy closure, or complementary weed-control tactics.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should assess the canopy as part of the field protocol. Good candidates are situations where the weed band is visible, the electrode path can reach the weeds, crop structures can be avoided, and the operator can maintain stable machine position. More difficult situations include closed crop canopies, low branches, uneven ground, thick mulch or residue, hidden weeds, dense mixed vegetation, or crops that are easily contacted by the applicator.

For perennial and specialty-crop customers, Zasso may help reduce chemical use and soil disturbance in under-row or under-canopy zones where mechanical tools or herbicide programs are constrained. The value is strongest when the crop geometry gives the machine a clear treatment path and when local trials confirm efficacy and crop safety.

Customers should not expect Zasso to control weeds it does not touch. If weeds are beneath dense foliage, protected by crop residue, below the electrode working height, or mixed tightly with crop tissue, the treatment may be incomplete. Timing the pass before canopy closure or before weeds become physically shielded can improve the chance of consistent contact.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can work under canopy when the weeds are reachable and the electrodes can contact the target vegetation without contacting the crop. The system needs physical access to the weeds; hidden or shielded weeds may require another tactic or a different timing."

"For under-canopy applications, we validate the crop geometry, canopy height, weed stage, electrode setup, and operating speed before making performance commitments. Zasso is strongest where the treatment zone is visible and contactable."

"Zasso can be a useful non-chemical tool in under-row or under-canopy weed-management programs, especially in suitable perennial and specialty-crop applications, but it should be integrated with local agronomy and crop-safety procedures."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso controls all weeds under a closed or dense canopy. The treatment depends on electrode access and contact quality.

Do not claim crop selectivity if the electrodes contact crop plants. Zasso is non-selective at the point of contact, so crop safety depends on equipment setup, guidance, operating procedure, and local validation.

Avoid promising one-pass, season-long, or universal under-canopy control. Weeds hidden by crop foliage, residue, mulch, low branches, stems, stones, uneven terrain, or dense vegetation may escape treatment.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, roadmap commitments, internal safety analysis, or private meeting details unless explicitly approved for external use.
