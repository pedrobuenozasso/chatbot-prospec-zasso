# How long does the equipment take to discharge?

## Short Answer

The discharge time is product-specific and should always be taken from the approved Zasso manual, training, or service instruction for the exact machine. Customers should not rely on a generic waiting time or assume the equipment is safe immediately after shutdown. The safe rule is to stop the system, follow the product-specific discharge and isolation procedure, and allow access only after the safe state has been verified by trained personnel.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical discharge time depends on the actual high-voltage architecture, stored energy, cable and component capacitance, discharge path, control logic, fault state, moisture, contamination, and the safety threshold being used. In simple electrical terms, stored charge usually decays over time through a defined resistance or discharge circuit, but the time is not universal across all machines or all operating conditions.

For Zasso customers, the practical answer is therefore not a single public number. The correct discharge time is the validated time or verification sequence stated for the specific product and task. Normal shutdown, high-voltage disable, emergency stop, or silence from the machine should be treated as steps toward safety, not proof that electrodes, cables, applicators, conductive structures, or service areas are safe to touch.

The most reliable operating habit is procedure-based: stop the application, secure the area, follow the approved shutdown and discharge process, prevent restart where required, and verify the safe condition before access. If the product manual or trained Zasso service team requires a measured residual-voltage check, a waiting period, lockout, PPE, or a specific sequence, that instruction takes priority over any general FAQ wording.

In some electrical-safety references, stored-energy systems are designed so capacitors discharge to a defined low voltage within a maximum time, often with verification and grounding requirements. Those references are useful context, but they do not replace Zasso's product-specific instructions or local legal requirements.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should train operators not to say, "wait a few seconds and it is safe," unless that exact instruction is part of the approved manual for that product. The safer sales and operating message is: discharge time is defined by the machine and procedure, and access is allowed only after the procedure confirms safety.

This matters most before cleaning, clearing vegetation, inspecting electrodes, disconnecting cables, servicing applicators, transporting the implement, or responding to alarms, emergency stops, unusual sparking, contact with metal, wet conditions, damaged insulation, or suspected faults.

The customer benefit is a clear and defensible safety routine. Instead of guessing a time in the field, the team follows the validated Zasso process for the specific equipment, task, and local regulation.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Discharge time is product-specific. Customers should follow the approved Zasso manual and training for the machine, and allow access only after the discharge and verification procedure confirms it is safe."

"We do not recommend using a generic wait time. The safe state depends on the machine design, condition, and task, so operators should follow the validated shutdown, discharge, and verification process."

"Shutdown is not the same as verified absence of hazardous energy. The equipment should be treated as potentially energized until the approved procedure says otherwise."

## Caveats

Do not publish a universal Zasso discharge time unless it is approved for the specific product and customer-facing use. Do not state or imply that every Zasso product is safe after a fixed number of seconds or minutes.

Do not disclose product-specific capacitance, stored-energy values, voltage thresholds, discharge-circuit design, service steps, lockout details, sensor logic, interlock behavior, PPE ratings, or test procedures unless they are already approved for external release.

Discharge time may vary by product, applicator, operating mode, battery or generator architecture, cable length, electrode condition, moisture, contamination, nearby conductive material, damaged insulation, fault state, local regulation, and whether the activity is normal operation, cleaning, transport, maintenance, or service.
