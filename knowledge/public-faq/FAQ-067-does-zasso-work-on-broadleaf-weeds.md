# Does Zasso work on broadleaf weeds?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on broadleaf weeds when the plants are properly contacted and the application is matched to the weed species, growth stage, density, moisture, and regrowth risk. Broadleaf weeds are often favorable targets for electrical weeding because their wider leaves and exposed stems can provide good electrode contact and conductive pathways into the plant. The right claim is that broadleaf weeds are within Zasso's treatable target spectrum, not that every broadleaf species will be controlled equally or in one pass.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology uses controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation. Because the mode of action is physical rather than herbicide-based, broadleaf weeds are not treated through a biochemical herbicide target site. Instead, performance depends on whether the electrodes make sufficient contact and whether enough useful energy is delivered through the plant tissues and plant-soil circuit to damage the structures that allow the weed to survive and regrow.

Broadleaf morphology can be helpful. Many broadleaf weeds have wider leaves, more exposed stems, and above-ground structure that can be easier for electrodes to contact than narrow-leaved grasses. Internal and external sources reviewed for this FAQ consistently indicate that plant morphology matters: contact area, stem/root architecture, root volume, growth stage, soil moisture, application speed, and weed density all influence the final result. Broadleaf weeds may therefore be easier to treat in many practical situations, but they are not automatically easy in all conditions.

The main caveats are species and regrowth. Young, upright, actively growing broadleaf weeds with good contact are generally more favorable targets. Mature broadleaf weeds, deep-rooted species, rosette-forming plants, weeds with crowns or storage organs, dense mixed stands, shielded plants, or weeds under wet or very variable soil conditions may require slower operation, stronger contact discipline, repeat passes, mowing or other preparation, or integration with other weed-management tactics.

For customer discussions, Zasso should be positioned as a serious non-chemical option for broadleaf weeds, especially where chemical reduction, herbicide resistance management, residue avoidance, soil disturbance reduction, or integrated weed management are priorities. Field validation should still identify the target species, growth stage, plant height, density, soil and plant moisture, crop proximity, application speed, number of passes, and the customer's acceptable regrowth threshold.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers with broadleaf weeds should include Zasso in the evaluation, especially where the target weeds are accessible to the applicator and where a non-chemical or reduced-chemical program is valuable. Broadleaf weeds may respond well because the applicator can often contact enough leaf and stem area to move energy through the plant.

The best customer trial should assess broadleaf weeds separately from grasses and sedges. Record the dominant species, growth stage, plant height, density, whether weeds are upright or rosette-forming, soil moisture, recent rainfall, crop proximity, application speed, pass count, and regrowth at follow-up. For perennial broadleaf weeds or species with crowns, taproots, tubers, rhizomes, or other reserve organs, success should be measured after enough time has passed for regrowth pressure to appear.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can treat broadleaf weeds when the applicator achieves good contact and the operating protocol is matched to the species, growth stage, density, and field conditions."

"Broadleaf weeds are often favorable targets for electrical weeding because their leaf and stem structure can support good contact, but we still validate the protocol for the customer's species and conditions."

"For mature, perennial, dense, or regrowth-prone broadleaf weeds, Zasso should be positioned as part of an integrated program with appropriate speed, contact, follow-up, and repeat-pass planning."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso controls every broadleaf weed, controls all broadleaf weeds in one pass, or performs independently of species, growth stage, contact, soil moisture, plant moisture, density, or operating speed. Broadleaf weeds with strong taproots, crowns, tubers, rhizomes, stolons, woody tissue, dense canopies, or protected regrowth points may need more careful protocol design and follow-up.

Do not equate visible burn-down with confirmed control. For many customers, the important result is not first-day discoloration but reduced regrowth over the following days or weeks. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished customer results, raw trial data, control logic, telemetry details, pricing, certification plans, or private meeting content unless explicitly approved for external use.
