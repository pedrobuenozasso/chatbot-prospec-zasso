# What maintenance is required for safety-critical components?

## Short Answer

Safety-critical components should be maintained exactly as defined in the approved Zasso manual, training, service plan, and local requirements for the specific product. At a customer-facing level, this means regular checks of electrodes, high-voltage cables, connectors, insulation, guards, warning devices, emergency stops, sensors, grounding or return-path elements, control interfaces, alarms, and other parts that affect safe high-voltage operation. Routine cleaning and visual checks may be operator tasks, but electrical inspection, high-voltage diagnostics, safety-system repair, and replacement of critical parts should be handled only by Zasso, an authorized dealer, or qualified personnel.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso equipment uses controlled high-voltage energy to treat vegetation, so maintenance of safety-critical components is part of safe operation rather than a secondary service activity. The exact maintenance tasks, intervals, acceptance criteria, and authorization levels depend on the machine, applicator, market, software and hardware version, operating environment, and approved documentation.

In general terms, customers should expect maintenance to cover several categories. Electrical-contact parts such as electrodes, applicators, high-voltage leads, plugs, connectors, and return-path components must be kept clean, intact, correctly mounted, and free from damage or unsafe contamination. Insulation, guards, warning labels, protective housings, cable routing, strain relief, and protective mats or barriers where fitted should be checked for wear, cracks, moisture, missing parts, or signs of misuse.

Safety devices and detection systems also need attention. Depending on the product, this may include emergency-stop functions, high-voltage enable or inhibit logic, warning lights and acoustic alerts, height or motion detection, tilt detection, insulation monitoring, fault detection, people or animal detection where fitted, control-unit alarms, and logged failures. These systems should not be bypassed, ignored, or treated as optional. If a safety-relevant alarm, repeated fault, unexplained shutdown, damaged part, abnormal spark behavior, wet contamination, or connector problem appears, the machine should stay out of high-voltage operation until the cause is cleared according to the manual or by qualified support.

There is an important distinction between operator maintenance and safety-critical service. Operators may normally perform approved pre-use checks, cleaning, simple visual inspections, and replacement of specific wear parts only if the manual and training allow it. Work involving high-voltage diagnostics, insulation testing, internal electrical components, safety-device repair, software or control-system changes, formal periodic inspection, or uncertain damage should be assigned to Zasso, an authorized service partner, or qualified high-voltage personnel.

Maintenance should also include records and return-to-service discipline. For professional customers, especially contractors, farms, municipalities, distributors, and fleet operators, it is good practice to document inspections, faults, parts replaced, authorized service work, and any abnormal event before the machine is returned to operation.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should plan maintenance before the season starts, before each workday, after transport or storage, and after any abnormal event. A machine that has damaged insulation, worn electrodes, loose connectors, dirty sensors, missing guards, unresolved alarms, modified wiring, or uncertain high-voltage behavior should not be used until it has been checked and cleared.

This protects people, uptime, warranty discipline, and operating confidence. It also helps customers separate normal operator care from technical service that requires specialist tools, training, documentation, or authorization.

The practical message is simple: keep safety-critical components clean, intact, inspected, documented, and repaired only through the appropriate authorization level. The approved Zasso manual for the delivered machine remains the source of truth.

## Caveats

Do not give one universal maintenance interval, inspection checklist, part-life estimate, or replacement rule for all Zasso products. Requirements can vary by product family, applicator, country, software or hardware version, operating hours, soil and weather exposure, storage and transport, crop environment, service contract, warranty terms, and local regulation.

Do not claim that visual inspection alone proves that a high-voltage system is safe. Do not imply that alarms can be ignored, sensors can be bypassed, or damaged insulation, connectors, electrodes, guards, warning systems, or safety devices can remain in service.

Do not disclose confidential service manuals, fault-code logic, voltage or current thresholds, insulation values, test procedures, sensor thresholds, circuit designs, certification details, warranty negotiations, customer-specific stock plans, or internal service strategy unless Zasso has approved that language for external use.
