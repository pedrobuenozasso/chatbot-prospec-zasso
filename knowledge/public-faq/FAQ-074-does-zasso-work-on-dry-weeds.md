# Does Zasso work on dry weeds?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on dry weeds, but dry conditions need careful qualification. Very dry plant tissue may conduct electricity less effectively than greener, hydrated tissue, and dry biomass can increase fire risk. For customers, the right message is that dry weeds are not automatically out of scope, but the application window, safety conditions, plant moisture, soil condition, residue load, speed, and operator protocol must be assessed before treatment.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding depends on the target plant becoming part of an electrical circuit. Current must enter the contacted weed, move through plant tissues and the plant-soil system, and deliver enough useful energy to damage cells, vascular tissues, crowns, roots, or other recovery structures. Moisture inside the plant affects this process because hydrated tissue usually conducts current more readily than very dry tissue.

That means dry weeds can be a more variable target than green, actively growing weeds. If the plant is drought-stressed but still alive and contactable, treatment can still be effective, and some stressed plants may have less recovery capacity after injury. If the plant tissue is very dry, woody, senescent, or surrounded by dry residue, conductivity and contact quality may be poorer and fire-risk controls become more important.

Dry soil and dry biomass should be considered separately. Dry soil can reduce the reliability of the plant-soil return path in some situations, while very wet soil can also reduce efficiency by allowing current to dissipate away from the plant. Dry above-ground biomass, especially dead or mowed residue, creates a safety issue because electrical treatment can produce localized heating or sparks under the wrong conditions. Zasso operators should therefore treat dry-weed applications as protocol-driven work, not as a simple yes/no question.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not assume that dry weeds are impossible to treat, but they should expect a field-condition check before application. Operators should look at whether the weeds are still alive or already dead, whether there is continuous dry residue or mulch, whether the soil is extremely dry, whether wind or heat increases fire risk, and whether the electrodes can make reliable contact with the target plants.

In suitable dry conditions, Zasso can be part of a non-chemical weed-control program, especially where herbicide resistance, chemical residues, spray drift, soil disturbance, or organic compatibility matter. In high fire-risk conditions, however, the right decision may be to delay treatment, change timing, reduce fuel load, adapt the operating protocol, or use another method until conditions are safer.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can treat dry weeds in suitable conditions, but dry vegetation requires a field check because very dry plant tissue may conduct less well and dry biomass can increase fire risk."

"We look at plant moisture, soil condition, residue load, wind, temperature, contact quality, and speed before recommending treatment in dry conditions."

"Dry weeds are not automatically a no-go, but high fire-risk conditions are a stop-and-assess situation, not a situation for blanket promises."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso always works equally well on dry and green weeds. Very dry, dead, woody, dusty, dense, or residue-heavy vegetation can reduce conductivity, make contact less reliable, and increase fire risk.

Do not claim that dry soil is always better or always worse. Soil moisture effects depend on soil type, conductivity, weed species, root system, electrode geometry, speed, and whether current is being directed through the plant or dissipated through easier soil paths.

Do not operate or recommend operation in high fire-risk conditions without local safety procedures, weather checks, residue assessment, operator training, and any applicable local fire restrictions. Avoid disclosing confidential settings, internal control logic, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, certification details, telemetry, or private meeting content.
