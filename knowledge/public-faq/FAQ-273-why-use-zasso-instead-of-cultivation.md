# Why use Zasso instead of cultivation?

## Short Answer

Use Zasso instead of cultivation when the goal is to control emerged weeds without cutting, scraping, or moving the soil. In suitable applications, Zasso can provide non-chemical weed control while preserving more surface cover and avoiding the shallow soil disturbance that cultivation often creates. The safe commercial position is not that Zasso replaces cultivation everywhere, but that it can be a strong alternative when the customer wants weed control without a cultivation pass.

## Detailed Answer

Cultivation is a familiar and useful mechanical weed-control method. Depending on the tool, it can slice off small weeds, uproot seedlings, loosen the soil surface, manage inter-row weeds, or help with selected row-crop operations after planting. In the right timing window, especially on small weeds, cultivation can be effective and operationally simple. Its limitation is that it works through soil disturbance. That means it can move soil, disrupt residue cover, risk crop-root injury or crop damage if not adjusted carefully, and sometimes bring buried weed seeds or vegetative propagules into conditions that favor new weed emergence.

Zasso is chosen instead of cultivation when the customer wants a different mode of action. Electroherb is designed to transfer controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit, without depending on blades, sweeps, shanks, or other soil-working parts. That can make Zasso attractive where the customer wants non-chemical control with less direct soil disturbance, wants to avoid repeated inter-row soil movement, or wants a treatment aimed at deeper plant injury rather than simply slicing or uprooting small weeds near the surface.

This difference is especially relevant in perennial crops, under-row management, residue-conserving systems, erosion-sensitive sites, and fields where repeated cultivation may create agronomic tradeoffs. If the customer's current cultivation pass is mainly a weed-control pass, Zasso may be a credible alternative. If the customer also relies on cultivation for seedbed refinement, crust breaking, row shaping, or another true soil-working function, then the comparison is not one-to-one and cultivation may still have a role.

The safest customer-facing comparison is therefore practical: use cultivation when a shallow soil-working pass is the best fit for the crop stage and field condition; use Zasso when the main goal is non-chemical weed control without a cultivation pass, subject to weed species, size, density, moisture, crop proximity, residue level, equipment fit, safety setup, and local validation.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, Zasso can be valuable where weed control is needed but soil movement is undesirable. That may include orchards, vineyards, citrus, berries, olives, coffee, no-till or reduced-till programs, erosion-prone sites, and situations where repeated cultivation creates labor, residue-management, or root-zone tradeoffs.

The decision should still be made against the customer's actual cultivation job. If the weeds are very small, the crop row is tight, and the operator needs a selective shallow soil-working pass, cultivation may remain the better tool. If the customer wants a non-chemical option that avoids repeated soil movement and may provide a more durable effect on suitable weeds, Zasso may offer better value.

Customers should validate the fit under local conditions. Weed species, weed stage, regrowth biology, soil and plant moisture, residue cover, stones, slope, crop geometry, travel speed, pass number, and traffic management all matter. In some programs, the best result may come from reducing selected cultivation passes rather than eliminating cultivation completely.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Use Zasso instead of cultivation when the customer wants non-chemical weed control without repeatedly moving or disturbing the soil."

"Zasso is often strongest where the current cultivation pass is mainly for weed control and the customer wants a lower-disturbance alternative, especially in perennial crops or erosion-sensitive systems."

"If the customer also needs a true shallow soil-working function close to the crop row, cultivation may still have a role and Zasso should be positioned as a complementary or alternative tool depending on the field fit."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso always replaces cultivation, always kills roots, or always reduces the number of passes. Cultivation can still be the better fit for very small weeds, certain crop stages, or situations where the customer genuinely needs a shallow soil-working pass.

Do not imply that Zasso has no operational constraints. Performance depends on weed species, growth stage, density, plant and soil moisture, residue, crop geometry, speed, contact quality, safety protocols, and local validation.

Do not make blanket economic claims. Whether Zasso is more efficient than cultivation depends on labor, number of repeat passes otherwise needed, regrowth pressure, field conditions, machine setup, traffic, and the value of avoiding soil disturbance.
