# Does Zasso avoid spray drift?

## Short Answer

Yes, with an important qualifier. Zasso's electrical weed-control treatment is not a spray application, so the treatment itself does not generate herbicide droplets, vapor drift, or off-target spray deposits. The practical benefit appears only where Zasso actually replaces or reduces a sprayed herbicide pass; it does not erase other risks in the broader weed-control program.

## Detailed Answer

Spray drift is specifically the off-target movement of pesticide or herbicide droplets, dust, or vapor during or shortly after application. That risk exists because spray equipment atomizes a product into airborne particles that can move with wind, inversions, boom height, droplet size, volatility, and application technique. Zasso changes that mechanism because Electroherb is a physical electrical treatment delivered through electrode contact with the vegetation rather than a liquid herbicide spray.

Because no herbicide liquid is sprayed during the Zasso treatment, the treatment itself does not create a herbicide spray-drift pathway. There are no spray droplets from the electrical pass to move onto neighboring crops, waterways, homes, field edges, organic blocks, or public spaces. In that narrow and important sense, Zasso avoids spray drift from the treatment itself.

The safer commercial claim is still conditional. Zasso avoids spray drift only for the spray operation that it replaces or reduces. If a customer still uses herbicides elsewhere in the program, those applications can still face drift restrictions, buffer requirements, label obligations, and neighbor-risk concerns. Zasso also brings its own operating requirements, including training, correct machine setup, crop clearance, contact quality, weather judgment, and fire-risk control in dry biomass.

In practice, the answer is strongest in drift-sensitive situations: specialty crops, vineyards, orchards, vegetables, organic or residue-sensitive production, areas near water, roadsides, railways, public spaces, field borders, and places where a chemical spray pass would create commercial or social risk. That does not make Zasso universally better than spraying, but it can remove the spray-drift mechanism from the weed-control pass that is being avoided.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the value is risk reduction and operating flexibility. Where a herbicide pass would be limited by wind, sensitive neighbors, high-value adjacent crops, organic certification concerns, or water proximity, Zasso can offer a non-chemical option that does not introduce airborne herbicide droplets from that pass.

This can matter commercially beyond agronomy alone. Avoiding a spray pass can help reduce drift complaints, buffer pressure, concerns about off-target deposition, and the difficulty of spraying near sensitive areas. The decision should still be made case by case, based on weed spectrum, crop setting, operating conditions, machine fit, and local rules.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso eliminates all off-target risk or all regulatory obligations. The defensible claim is narrower: the electrical treatment itself does not create herbicide spray drift.

Do not extend this answer to herbicide applications that remain elsewhere in the customer's program. Those applications can still have drift restrictions, label requirements, and liability exposure.

Do not present this as a guarantee that Zasso is suitable in every weather condition or site. Safe use still depends on trained operation, correct setup, crop protection, local requirements, and fire-risk management in dry residues or hot conditions.
