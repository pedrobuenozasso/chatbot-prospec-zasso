# What are the main safety risks?

## Short Answer

The main safety risks are electrical contact or shock, unintended conductive paths through metal, water, wet ground, machine parts, people or animals entering the active zone, sparks or fire in dry conditions, and unsafe maintenance or operation by untrained users. Zasso equipment is designed for professional use with layered safety controls, but it must never be treated as risk-free. Customers should manage these risks through trained operators, exclusion zones, inspections, approved PPE where required, emergency shutdown procedures, and strict adherence to the product-specific manual.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's technology uses high-voltage electricity to treat plants through direct contact with applicators or electrodes. That creates a clear safety profile: the intended current path is through the target plant and the plant-soil return circuit, while the safety objective is to prevent people, animals, bystanders, metal objects, water paths, damaged insulation, or machine structures from becoming part of an unintended electrical path.

The most important risk is direct or indirect electrical exposure. Direct exposure can happen if someone touches energized electrodes or conductive materials touching them. Indirect exposure can occur if a metal object, fence, irrigation component, buried utility, puddle, wet surface, damaged cable, or machine structure becomes energized or changes the current path. Shock severity depends on current, current path through the body, exposure duration, contact conditions, and the available energy, not on voltage alone.

A second major risk is loss of worksite control. People, animals, maintenance staff, or untrained operators entering the active treatment zone can create dangerous situations. For that reason, operating rules normally include safety distances, clear operator responsibility, controlled access, stopping high voltage when someone enters the zone, and keeping animals away before activation.

A third risk is operation under unsuitable field or site conditions. Active irrigation, water spray, puddles, wet conductive materials, metal debris, buried or exposed metal, unknown utilities, damaged insulation, alarms, unusual sparking, dry biomass, high fire-risk weather, or unauthorized maintenance can all change the risk profile. These situations should be handled according to the product manual and training, and in doubtful cases the conservative answer is to stop and reassess before continuing.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat Zasso equipment as specialized high-voltage agricultural or industrial equipment. Safe use is not just a machine feature; it is the combination of machine design, correct setup, trained operation, site control, inspections, maintenance, shutdown discipline, and local compliance.

In practical terms, customers should keep people and animals outside the active zone, prevent anyone from touching electrodes or conductive objects that may be connected to the applicator, inspect cables and insulation, avoid unsafe metal or water interactions, stop for alarms or abnormal behavior, and follow lockout or shutdown procedures before maintenance. The exact safety distance, PPE, inspection routine, and operating restrictions can vary by product, country, certification, site, crop, applicator, and field condition.

This risk-based framing helps customers use a non-chemical weed-control tool responsibly. It also helps distributors, contractors, and municipalities train operators consistently and avoid overconfident claims during demonstrations or commercial discussions.

## Safe Sales Wording

"The main safety risks are electrical contact, unintended conductive paths, bystander or animal exposure, metal and water interactions, fire risk in dry conditions, and unsafe operation or maintenance. Zasso manages these risks through professional equipment design, trained operation, inspections, exclusion zones, and product-specific safety procedures."

"We do not present high-voltage weed control as risk-free. The safe message is that it is designed for controlled professional use when the approved manual, training, safety distances, PPE, maintenance routines, and local rules are followed."

"If conditions are abnormal, such as damaged cables, alarms, active irrigation, water bridging, bystanders, animals, metal objects, or high fire-risk dry biomass, the operator should stop and follow the approved safety procedure before continuing."

## Caveats

Do not say that Zasso equipment is completely safe, harmless, safe to touch, safe for untrained users, safe around all water or metal objects, safe in all weather, safe without exclusion zones, or safe without product-specific procedures.

Do not disclose confidential voltage, current, frequency, waveform, module, sensor, interlock, shutdown-threshold, diagnostic, certification, incident, customer, supplier, or test-report details unless they are already approved for public use.

The exact safety requirements depend on the product configuration, applicator, country, certification status, site, crop, weed density, soil and moisture condition, nearby people or animals, metal objects, irrigation, terrain, weather, fire risk, machine condition, maintenance state, operator training, and local regulation. The approved product manual and training always take priority over this general FAQ.
