# Does Zasso work on woody plants?

## Short Answer

Zasso can be relevant for some woody or semi-woody plants when the target tissue can be contacted properly and the application program is matched to the plant's size, structure, and regrowth potential. Woody plants are generally harder than small herbaceous weeds because bark, lignified stems, crowns, stored reserves, and larger root systems can reduce the chance of complete control from a single pass. The safest customer expectation is that woody-plant control should be validated by species and site, and may require careful timing, slower operation, repeated treatment, or integration with other vegetation-management methods.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding depends on making the target plant part of an electrical circuit. The treatment must contact conductive plant tissue and deliver enough useful energy through the plant-soil pathway to damage the tissues that support survival and regrowth. For herbaceous weeds, especially young broadleaf plants, that pathway is often easier to access. For woody plants, the target is more difficult because stems may be thicker, bark may reduce contact quality, and the plant may have more stored energy in crowns, roots, or woody perennial structures.

That does not mean woody vegetation is automatically outside the scope of Zasso. Small woody seedlings, young suckers, tender regrowth, semi-woody stems, and accessible green tissue may be more suitable than mature shrubs, hard stems, or established woody root systems. In practice, performance will depend on the species, stem diameter, bark condition, moisture status, canopy density, electrode access, soil condition, operating speed, product configuration, pass number, and the definition of success.

For sales discussions, the right distinction is between suppression, top-growth damage, and durable control. A woody plant may show visible injury after treatment but still recover if enough crown or root tissue remains alive. Where woody plants are the target, Zasso should be presented as a non-chemical tool that can be tested and integrated into a broader program, not as a universal brush-eradication solution.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers dealing with woody vegetation should start with a measured field trial rather than a blanket assumption. The trial should define the target species, plant size, stem condition, density, soil moisture, equipment setup, operating speed, number of passes, and regrowth observation period. For small or recently emerged woody plants, expectations can be more favorable than for established shrubs or woody perennial root systems.

Zasso may be attractive where customers want to reduce herbicide use, avoid spray drift or residues, manage vegetation near sensitive areas, or reduce soil disturbance compared with mechanical tools. However, woody vegetation often has higher regrowth risk than young annual weeds. Customers should plan follow-up scouting and, where needed, a second pass or complementary method.

A peer-reviewed review in Weed Science explains that electric weed control transfers electrical current through target plants after electrode contact, causing cell damage that can kill plants or suppress growth, while emphasizing the need to consider weed biology and field conditions: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/exploring-the-potential-of-electric-weed-control-a-review/231EE50C385EF8962CDC46055C264237

Public extension guidance on woody weeds generally treats established woody plants as difficult targets that vary by species and often need repeat or integrated management. That supports cautious positioning for any woody-plant claim, even when the control method is electrical rather than chemical.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can be tested on woody or semi-woody vegetation, especially where green tissue and good electrode contact are available, but woody plants are a more difficult target than small herbaceous weeds."

"For woody plants, we recommend validating the target species, growth stage, and operating protocol locally before making large-scale performance assumptions."

"Zasso may help reduce reliance on chemical or soil-disturbing methods in suitable woody-vegetation applications, but it should be planned as a program with follow-up scouting, not sold as a universal one-pass eradication tool."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso always kills woody plants, permanently eliminates shrubs, or controls every woody species in one pass. Avoid universal claims for stem diameter, plant height, root kill, regrowth prevention, or brush eradication unless Zasso has species-specific and site-specific evidence.

Woody plants with thick bark, large crowns, deep roots, rhizomes, protected buds, dense canopy, dry tissue, poor electrode contact, or strong regrowth capacity may need repeated treatment, adjusted timing, pre-treatment access work, or integration with other methods. Safety and fire-risk assessment are especially important where woody or dry biomass is present.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, control logic, unpublished trial results, customer names, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
