# Can Zasso reduce herbicide use?

## Short Answer

Yes, in suitable applications Zasso can reduce herbicide use by replacing selected chemical passes with a non-chemical electrical treatment. The safe customer-facing position is not that herbicides disappear everywhere, but that Zasso can help lower dependence on herbicides where resistance, drift, residues, public acceptance, or local operating constraints make chemical-only programs less attractive. The exact reduction depends on the crop, weeds, site conditions, machine fit, operating protocol, and local rules.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's value is strongest when customers want to reduce herbicide use without relying only on mowing, tillage, or hand labor. Electroherb adds a different mode of action: controlled electrical energy delivered through contacted vegetation, without applying a herbicide active ingredient during the treatment itself. That gives growers and land managers another way to control weeds in use cases where reducing chemical dependence has practical, regulatory, environmental, or commercial value.

In practice, herbicide reduction can happen in several ways. Zasso may replace a specific non-selective spray pass, reduce the number of herbicide applications needed over a season, or help reserve herbicides for the areas or timings where they still deliver the best return. This can matter in permanent crops, sensitive areas, public or infrastructure sites, resistant-weed situations, drift-sensitive zones, residue-conscious production, and some organic-compatible programs where the local certification framework allows it.

The commercial message should stay use-case specific. The best question is not "Can Zasso eliminate all herbicides?" but "Which herbicide pass can Zasso credibly replace or reduce for this customer, under what conditions, and with what operational tradeoffs?" That framing is stronger because it matches how real weed-management programs are built.

Zasso should still be presented as part of integrated weed management. Many customers will continue using herbicides in some form, but reducing dependence can still be strategically valuable. It can lower repeated exposure to the same herbicide modes of action, reduce spray-related constraints from the avoided passes, and create more flexibility where chemical use is restricted or under pressure.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical value is optionality. Zasso can help reduce herbicide use where a spray pass is hard to justify because of resistant weeds, neighbor sensitivity, water proximity, organic or sustainability goals, public expectations, or site-specific restrictions.

A reduction claim should always be tied to a defined task. Customers should evaluate which weeds are targeted, whether a full or partial reduction is realistic, how many passes are needed, what the application window is, what safety procedures apply, and whether the economics work in that exact setting.

The strongest customer outcome is not simply "less chemistry" as a slogan. It is a weed-control program that is more resilient because it does not depend on only one tactic. In suitable cases, reducing herbicide use can help preserve chemical tools, lower exposure to spray-related limitations from avoided applications, and improve acceptance in sensitive environments.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can reduce herbicide use in suitable applications by replacing selected spray passes with a non-chemical physical treatment."

"The right commercial question is which herbicide operation Zasso can reduce or replace for this customer, not whether all herbicides disappear from every program."

"Zasso is strongest as part of integrated weed management, especially where resistance, drift, residues, sensitive sites, or chemical-reduction goals matter."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso always reduces herbicide use in every crop, weed spectrum, geography, or season. The reduction depends on the specific use case, operating conditions, product fit, and economics.

Do not imply that any herbicide-reduction claim automatically means lower total cost, faster operation, or better efficacy. Those points must be validated locally.

Do not make crop-specific, regulatory, organic-certification, or public-policy claims without checking the relevant market, customer program, and local rules.

Do not overstate environmental or safety claims. Zasso avoids applying herbicide active ingredients during the treatment itself, but it still requires trained operation, suitable conditions, and attention to electrical and fire-risk procedures.
