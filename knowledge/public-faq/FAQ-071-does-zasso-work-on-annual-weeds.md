# Does Zasso work on annual weeds?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on annual weeds when the plants are emerged, accessible to the electrodes, and treated under suitable field conditions. Annual weeds are often more favorable targets than deep-rooted perennial weeds because they usually have smaller recovery reserves and depend on the treated shoot-root connection for survival. Results still depend on species, growth stage, plant size, density, moisture, electrode contact, operating speed, pass number, and follow-up management.

## Detailed Answer

Annual weeds complete their life cycle in one season, so they generally do not rely on long-lived crowns, rhizomes, tubers, bulbs, or deep perennial root reserves. That makes many annual weeds good candidates for electrical weeding, especially when they are young to moderately developed, actively growing, and physically contacted by the applicator.

Zasso's Electroherb technology uses controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil pathway. For an annual weed, effective treatment can damage the shoot, vascular tissues, root-shoot connection, and sometimes root tissue enough that the plant cannot recover. This is different from a residual herbicide: Zasso treats plants that are present and contacted during application; it does not prevent new seedlings from germinating later from the seedbank.

The strongest customer-facing answer is therefore conditional confidence. Annual broadleaf weeds with upright, contactable leaves and stems are often favorable targets. Annual grasses can also be treated, but may be more sensitive to growth stage, density, tillering, moisture, and whether the electrodes contact enough of the plant. Very small weeds hidden under residue, lodged plants, dense mixed stands, dry or mature plants, and weeds shielded by crop canopy or other vegetation may require better timing, slower speed, repeat passes, mowing or canopy preparation, or another integrated weed-management tactic.

For sales and agronomy discussions, annual weeds should be positioned as a strong fit for Zasso where the objective is non-chemical control of emerged weeds. The right protocol should still be validated for the customer's crop, weed spectrum, soil, moisture, product configuration, safety requirements, and operating window.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers with annual weed pressure can consider Zasso as a practical non-chemical tool, particularly where herbicide reduction, organic production, residue avoidance, drift reduction, or resistance management matters. The best results are more likely when annual weeds are treated before they become large, dense, dry, lodged, or difficult to contact.

A good customer trial should record the target species, whether weeds are broadleaf or grass, growth stage, weed height, density, soil and plant moisture, recent mowing or residue, operating speed, pass number, and regrowth or new emergence after treatment. Annual weeds can be controlled by the treatment, but new seedlings may still emerge later if conditions favor germination, so follow-up scouting remains important.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can be very relevant for annual weeds, especially when they are emerged, accessible, and treated at the right growth stage with good electrode contact."

"Annual weeds are often easier targets than perennial weeds because they usually have fewer protected regrowth reserves, but results still depend on species, stage, density, moisture, speed, and trained operation."

"Zasso treats the weeds that are present and contacted during application; later germination from the seedbank may still require follow-up scouting or another pass."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso prevents future germination, provides residual weed control, controls every annual species equally, or guarantees one-pass control in all conditions. Annual weeds can still be difficult when they are too small to contact, hidden under residue, shielded by crop or taller weeds, dry, mature, lodged, dense, or already producing seed.

Be careful with annual grasses and mixed stands. Grass morphology, tillering, narrow leaves, dense mats, and protected growing points can make some annual grasses more parameter-sensitive than upright annual broadleaf weeds. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, telemetry, certification details, or private meeting content.
