# Can it reduce chemical load in the environment?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can help reduce chemical load in the environment when it replaces or reduces a chemical herbicide treatment in a suitable application. Its electrical weed-control process does not apply a chemical active ingredient, so the treatment itself does not add herbicide residues, spray drift deposits, or herbicide runoff and leaching pathways. The claim should be made carefully: Zasso reduces chemical load only to the extent that it actually displaces chemical use in the customer's weed-control program.

## Detailed Answer

Chemical load usually refers to the quantity and environmental presence of chemical inputs such as herbicide active ingredients, formulations, metabolites, spray deposits, residues in soil or water, or chemicals moved by drift, runoff, or leaching. Zasso's technology is different from chemical herbicides because the mode of action is physical: controlled electrical energy is delivered through contacted plants and the plant-soil circuit.

Because Zasso does not spray or apply a molecular herbicide, the treatment itself does not introduce a herbicide active ingredient into the crop, soil, water, air, or surrounding environment. This can reduce chemical load where Zasso is used instead of a herbicide pass, where it reduces the number of chemical applications needed, or where it allows a customer to manage specific weeds without adding another chemical mode of action.

The strongest customer-facing statement is therefore conditional and practical: Zasso can reduce chemical load as part of an integrated weed-management program. It may be especially relevant for customers trying to reduce herbicide dependence, manage sensitive areas, avoid spray drift, support organic-compatible or residue-conscious production, respond to herbicide restrictions, or diversify programs affected by herbicide resistance.

This does not mean Zasso removes chemicals already present in the environment, eliminates all environmental impacts, or automatically reduces total farm chemical use in every program. The real reduction depends on how the customer uses the technology: which herbicide treatment is replaced, how many passes are needed, whether other chemical applications remain, the crop and weed situation, local regulation, economics, operating quality, and safety conditions.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical value is that Zasso offers a non-chemical weed-control option that can lower reliance on herbicides in suitable use cases. This can help reduce concerns about chemical residues, spray drift, runoff, leaching, operator exposure to spray chemistry, and repeated dependence on the same herbicide programs.

It is most credible when positioned as a tool that can reduce or replace selected chemical treatments, not as a universal claim that all herbicides disappear. A good implementation plan should compare the current herbicide program with the proposed Zasso operation, including control target, timing, number of passes, safety setup, energy or fuel use, weather conditions, fire risk, operator training, regulatory requirements, and cost.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can help reduce chemical load where it replaces or reduces a herbicide treatment, because the electrical treatment itself does not apply a chemical active ingredient."

"It is a non-chemical mode of action, so there is no herbicide spray deposit, drift deposit, or herbicide runoff and leaching pathway from the Zasso treatment itself."

"The actual reduction depends on the customer's weed-control program, local conditions, and which chemical applications are avoided."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso has zero environmental impact, removes existing contamination, or always eliminates the need for herbicides. The correct claim is that Zasso can reduce chemical load when it substitutes for or reduces chemical herbicide use in suitable applications.

Do not promise automatic organic approval, regulatory acceptance, food-residue compliance, water-body compliance, or customer-specific sustainability outcomes without checking the relevant local rules and certification requirements.

Do not ignore operational tradeoffs. Application speed, energy or fuel use, electrode contact, weed stage, soil moisture, soil conductivity, vegetation density, residue, weather, safety procedures, and fire-risk conditions all affect whether Zasso is the right tool for a given site.
