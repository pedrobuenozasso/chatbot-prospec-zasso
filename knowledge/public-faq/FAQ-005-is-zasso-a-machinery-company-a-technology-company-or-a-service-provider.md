# Is Zasso a machinery company, a technology company, or a service provider?

## Short Answer

Zasso is best described as a technology-led electric-weeding solutions provider. It develops core electric-weeding technology, including power electronics, control systems, applicators, safety concepts, and digital support tools, and brings that technology to customers through equipment, partners, rentals, licensing, training, and service models. So Zasso is not only a machinery company or only a service provider; machinery and services are channels for delivering its technology.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso sits at the intersection of agricultural technology, professional equipment, and field services. Its core identity is technology: Zasso develops and commercializes electric-weeding systems that use controlled high-voltage electricity to treat unwanted plants without applying chemical herbicides. Public Zasso materials describe the company as designing and commercializing electric-weeding technologies for agriculture and municipalities, and as specializing in non-chemical weed-management solutions using advanced power electronics.

That technology has to be delivered in practical form, so Zasso also works with machinery. Depending on the market and product, Zasso solutions may include tractor-based platforms, handheld or urban devices, customized applicators, power modules, control software, and integration with carriers or OEM partners. This makes the company relevant to machinery buyers, distributors, contractors, municipalities, growers, and equipment partners, but the machine is not the whole value proposition.

Zasso also has a service-provider dimension. Internal commercial materials and recent meeting summaries point to post-sales support, operator training, certification concepts, telemetry, fleet management, rental models, partner support, and recurring-revenue service models. Customer-facing language should describe this at a high level: Zasso supports deployment through training, partners, service models, and technical support where available, with the exact model depending on country, product, certification status, and partner structure.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical point is that Zasso can support more than a one-time equipment purchase. A customer may engage with Zasso through direct equipment sales, local distributors, service contractors, rental or subscription models, OEM integration, technology licensing, training, or after-sales support depending on the market.

This matters because electric weeding is operationally different from conventional spraying, mowing, or cultivation. Customers need not only hardware, but also the right configuration, safe operation, trained users, maintenance support, and realistic agronomic guidance. Zasso's positioning as a technology-led solutions provider helps explain why the company invests in electronics, applicators, software, safety, training, partnerships, and field validation rather than presenting itself as a simple machinery vendor.

## Safe Sales Wording

Zasso is a technology-led electric-weeding solutions provider: we develop the core technology and make it usable through equipment, partners, training, and support models.

For many customers, the visible product is a machine, but the value is broader: power electronics, applicator design, control software, safety systems, operational know-how, and deployment support.

Depending on the country and application, Zasso may work through direct sales, distributors, service contractors, rental models, OEM integration, or technology licensing.

## Caveats

Do not describe Zasso as only a machinery manufacturer, because that understates the company's core technology, IP, software, safety, training, and partner model. Do not describe Zasso as only a service provider either, because it also develops and commercializes physical equipment and technology components.

Avoid implying that every business model is available in every country or for every product. Availability of sales, rental, service, licensing, certification, training, and support models depends on local market structure, product configuration, regulatory status, partner coverage, and commercial agreement.
