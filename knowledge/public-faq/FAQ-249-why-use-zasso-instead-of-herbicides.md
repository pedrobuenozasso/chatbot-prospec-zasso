# Why use Zasso instead of herbicides?

## Short Answer

Customers use Zasso instead of a herbicide treatment when they want a professional, non-chemical way to control weeds in a suitable application. Zasso can help reduce reliance on herbicides, avoid chemical residues and spray drift from the avoided spray pass, and add a physical mode of action for integrated weed management. It should not be sold as the best answer for every field, crop, weed, or budget; the value is strongest where the customer has a clear reason to reduce, avoid, or diversify away from chemical weed control.

## Detailed Answer

Herbicides are widely used because they are familiar, scalable, and often cost-effective. In many systems they will remain part of a responsible weed-control program. The reason to use Zasso is not that herbicides have no role, but that some customers need another serious tool when chemical-only control is under pressure from resistance, regulation, residues, drift concerns, market expectations, organic or residue-sensitive production, sensitive sites, or public acceptance.

Zasso's Electroherb technology uses controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit. Because the treatment is physical rather than chemical, it does not apply a herbicide active ingredient. Where Zasso replaces a herbicide pass, it can remove the spray-drift, chemical-residue, rainfastness, and chemical exposure pathways associated with that avoided application.

The technology can also support integrated weed management. Repeated reliance on the same herbicide modes of action can select for resistant weeds. Zasso adds a different, non-chemical mode of action that is not based on a biochemical herbicide target site. That can be valuable for resistant escapes, sensitive areas, organic-compatible programs, permanent crops, public spaces, and customers trying to reduce chemical dependency without relying only on mowing, tillage, or manual work.

The correct comparison is application-specific. A customer should ask which weed problem Zasso is expected to solve, which herbicide pass it may replace or reduce, whether the target weeds and operating conditions are suitable, and whether the economics work once safety, training, speed, energy or fuel use, number of passes, service model, and local rules are considered.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical value is choice and risk reduction. Zasso can be attractive when a herbicide pass is limited by drift risk, residue concerns, water proximity, organic or sustainability goals, herbicide resistance, public-area restrictions, neighbor sensitivity, or the desire to reduce chemical load.

Zasso can also help customers diversify their weed-control program. Instead of depending only on chemical options, a grower, contractor, municipality, or land manager can combine electrical treatment with scouting, prevention, crop competition, mowing, mechanical tools, targeted herbicides where appropriate, and follow-up management.

Customers should still expect a professional operating decision, not a magic substitution. Performance depends on weed species, plant size, growth stage, root system, density, soil moisture, soil conductivity, contact quality, speed, product configuration, safety setup, operator training, and the number of passes required. In some cases Zasso can replace a specific herbicide operation; in others it is best used as a complementary tool.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Use Zasso where a customer needs a serious non-chemical weed-control option, especially when herbicide resistance, drift, residues, organic compatibility, sensitive sites, or chemical-reduction goals matter."

"Zasso can replace or reduce selected herbicide treatments in suitable applications, but it is best positioned as part of integrated weed management rather than as a universal replacement for all herbicides."

"The advantage is a physical mode of action with no herbicide active ingredient applied by the treatment itself, subject to the right crop, weed, operating conditions, safety procedures, and economics."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is always cheaper, always more effective, always suitable for every weed, or a complete replacement for every herbicide program. Herbicides remain legal, effective, and economically relevant in many systems when used correctly.

Do not claim zero environmental impact or zero risk. Zasso avoids herbicide active ingredients from the treatment itself, but it still requires trained operation, safety distances, correct electrode contact, suitable vegetation and soil conditions, attention to fire-risk conditions, and local validation.

Do not make crop-specific, regulatory, organic-certification, re-entry, withholding-period, or pesticide-registration claims without checking the relevant country, crop, product, label, certifier, and customer use case.
