# What level of weed control can be expected?

## Short Answer

Customers should expect weed control to be condition-dependent, not a fixed guaranteed percentage. In suitable applications with good electrode contact, appropriate speed, trained operation, and the right treatment timing, Zasso technology can deliver strong control; public field evidence includes results above 80% weed control in optimized organic blueberry trials and high biomass reduction in specific vineyard and crop contexts. Results vary by weed species, weed stage, density, soil and moisture conditions, contact quality, operating speed, pass number, and follow-up management.

## Detailed Answer

The most responsible expectation is that Zasso can provide high levels of weed suppression when the agronomic and operating conditions are matched to the target weeds. Electrical weeding is a physical treatment: the result depends on delivering enough useful electrical energy through the plant and plant-soil pathway to damage critical tissues and reduce regrowth. That means field performance is influenced by plant morphology, root and crown structure, water content, soil moisture, soil conductivity, electrode geometry, application speed, contact time, power delivery, and operator setup.

In well-matched situations, customers may see strong visual control and meaningful biomass reduction after treatment. For example, Oregon State University Extension reported that slower electrical weed-control passes in organic blueberry delivered strong control, with single slow passes reaching high control levels and repeated moderate-speed passes helping keep weed pressure low. A related Frontiers in Agronomy study reported more than 80% weed control at 28 days after treatment and biomass reduction up to 73% under optimized blueberry conditions. Other public studies and Zasso materials also support strong outcomes in suitable vineyard, orchard, soybean, cotton, sugar beet, potato, and non-crop use cases.

However, the expected level of control should always be tied to the specific job. Upright broadleaf weeds with good electrode contact may respond better than prostrate weeds, grasses, clumping species, deep-rooted perennials, or plants with protected regrowth points. Dense canopies, poor contact, high speed, dry or highly variable soil, mature plants, crop shielding constraints, and insufficient follow-up can reduce control or allow regrowth. Some situations require a slower pass, sequential passes, mowing before treatment, a different applicator setup, or integration with other weed-management tactics.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the right question is not only "what percentage control does Zasso achieve?" but "what control can be expected for this crop, weed spectrum, soil, season, machine setup, and operating plan?" A credible field trial should define the target weeds, weed stage, baseline method, success metric, travel speed, pass number, soil and moisture conditions, crop-safety constraints, and evaluation timing.

Where conditions are suitable, Zasso can be a strong non-chemical tool for reducing emerged weeds, managing escapes, reducing reliance on herbicides, and supporting integrated weed management. Customers should still plan for scouting, trained operators, local validation, and possible follow-up treatment where regrowth or new emergence occurs.

## Safe Sales Wording

"In suitable conditions, Zasso can deliver strong weed control, including results above 80% in documented optimized field trials, but the exact level depends on the weed, crop, soil, moisture, contact quality, speed, and treatment plan."

"We recommend setting performance expectations through a field-specific protocol: identify the target weeds, choose the right applicator and speed, train the operator, and evaluate control and regrowth after treatment."

"Zasso should be presented as a high-potential non-chemical tool within integrated weed management, not as a universal guaranteed percentage for every field."

## Caveats

Do not promise guaranteed control, complete root kill, permanent prevention of regrowth, one-pass success, or the same efficacy percentage across all crops, weeds, soils, climates, and product configurations. Avoid saying that Zasso is always equivalent or superior to herbicides, mowing, tillage, flame, steam, or hand weeding; the fair comparison depends on the target weed, crop, local restrictions, operating cost, safety requirements, soil disturbance, residue goals, and program design.

Do not disclose confidential customer results, raw trial data, unpublished operating thresholds, pricing, product settings, control logic, telemetry details, or private meeting content unless specifically approved for external use.
