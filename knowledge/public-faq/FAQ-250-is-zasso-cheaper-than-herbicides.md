# Is Zasso cheaper than herbicides?

## Short Answer

Not necessarily. In some validated applications, Zasso can be cheaper than a herbicide-based program on a total weed-control-cost basis, especially where herbicide costs are high, multiple passes are needed, resistance is raising treatment intensity, or customers place value on avoiding drift and residues from the replaced spray pass. In other situations, especially where herbicides are still inexpensive and operationally simple, herbicides may remain cheaper.

## Detailed Answer

The correct comparison is not only the price of herbicide per liter or the cost of a single spray pass. The better comparison is total weed-control cost for the relevant period and use case. That includes the number of passes, tractor and operator time, energy or fuel use, machine utilization, financing or ownership structure, maintenance, regrowth management, service support, and any extra operations needed because weeds were not controlled well enough the first time.

Herbicides often remain hard to beat on narrow short-term cash cost when the chemistry is still working well, the spray program is straightforward, and the target crop or site does not face strong pressure from residues, drift, regulation, public acceptance, or resistance. That is one reason herbicides still dominate many weed-control programs.

Zasso becomes more economically attractive when the comparison is made against a more demanding herbicide situation rather than against a best-case low-cost spray assumption. Examples include permanent crops and specialty crops with repeated under-row weed management, programs using more expensive herbicide strategies, situations with resistant weeds that require added passes or more complex programs, areas where drift or residue concerns create extra constraints, and customers who would otherwise need labor-intensive or mechanically disruptive alternatives.

Based on current Zasso commercial materials, selected citrus, coffee, and other permanent-crop scenarios can show lower total cost per hectare than herbicide programs when the machine is correctly matched to the crop and used enough hours per year. However, that does not justify a blanket claim that Zasso is cheaper than herbicides in every crop, region, or operating model. Broad-acre low-cost herbicide programs, low annual machine utilization, poor application fit, or the need for multiple electrical passes can change the economics materially.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should evaluate Zasso as a business case for a specific weed problem, not as a universal cost slogan. The practical question is: compared with the current herbicide program in this crop and geography, what is the total cost per hectare or per season once all operations are counted?

For some customers, the economic value comes from direct savings on herbicides and associated spray operations. For others, the value is broader: fewer resistance-related problems, less dependence on difficult chemical programs, less drift-sensitive operating pressure, better fit with organic-compatible or residue-conscious production, and an alternative to manual or mechanically disruptive control.

The strongest commercial approach is to compare one defined use case at a time. Identify which herbicide pass or program Zasso is meant to replace or reduce, estimate expected number of electrical passes, include annual utilization and financing assumptions, and then compare total operating cost rather than only input price.

## Safe Sales Wording

"In suitable applications, Zasso can be cheaper than a herbicide program on a total cost-of-control basis, especially in permanent crops or higher-cost weed-management situations."

"The right message is not that Zasso is always cheaper. The stronger message is that it can be economically competitive or better when we compare the full weeding program for the specific crop, weeds, and geography."

"We should compare one real customer use case at a time, including passes, labor, tractor time, utilization, and the herbicide program being replaced or reduced."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is always cheaper than herbicides. That is not supported across all crops, geographies, weed pressures, machine-utilization levels, financing structures, or regulatory environments.

Do not compare Zasso only to the price of herbicide product. A fair comparison should include total operational cost, including machinery, labor, energy or fuel, number of passes, service model, and regrowth management.

Do not make crop-specific savings promises without a local business case. Economics can change materially depending on target weeds, growth stage, annual hectares treated, operator quality, machine availability, and whether the herbicide alternative is conventional, resistant-weed, residue-sensitive, or organic-style weed control.
