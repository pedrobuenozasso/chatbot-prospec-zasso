# How many passes are typically required?

## Short Answer

There is no universal pass number for Zasso applications. In suitable conditions, one well-executed pass may be enough for the required level of weed suppression, especially on small, accessible annual weeds with good electrode contact. More difficult situations, such as perennial weeds, grasses, dense or layered infestations, mature plants, faster operating speeds, incomplete contact, or desiccation tasks requiring uniform dry-down, may require a planned second pass or repeated treatments. The right pass strategy should be set by the target weed, crop, field condition, machine setup, operating speed, and follow-up scouting.

## Detailed Answer

Pass number in electrical weeding is a treatment-design decision, not a fixed product promise. Zasso's technology works by delivering controlled electrical energy through contacted plants and the plant-soil pathway. The key question is whether enough useful energy reaches the tissues that allow the weed to survive or regrow, such as crowns, roots, rhizomes, tubers, runners, tillers, or woody recovery structures.

For easier targets, a single pass can be appropriate. This is most likely when weeds are small, upright, actively growing, accessible to the electrodes, and present at a density that allows good contact and effective energy delivery. In these situations, one pass may deliver enough treatment dose to meet the customer's commercial objective, especially when the goal is suppression of emerged annual weeds rather than long-term depletion of a perennial root system.

For harder targets, more than one pass may be needed. Perennial weeds and grasses can recover from protected growing points or underground reserves. Dense stands can shield smaller weeds or divide available energy across many plants. Faster travel speeds reduce contact time. Residue, mulch, uneven terrain, canopy shielding, or poor electrode positioning can reduce treatment quality. In those situations, a second pass can target survivors, regrowth, or new emergence and can help improve uniformity.

Public field work shows why the answer must remain conditional. Oregon State University Extension reports that slow single passes in organic blueberry can deliver strong control, while strategic repeated passes at faster speeds can also help maintain control over time. A Frontiers in Agronomy study in organic highbush blueberry reported strong control under optimized conditions and found that sequential applications helped sustain control where regrowth or new emergence mattered. Michigan State University Extension similarly lists number of passes, tractor speed, contact time, electrode height, species, plant moisture, and soil moisture among the factors that affect electrical weed-control outcomes.

The practical recommendation is therefore to plan the first pass carefully and scout afterward. If the field meets the target result, no extra pass should be assumed. If meaningful survivors, regrowth, new emergence, or uneven desiccation remain, an additional pass or integrated weed-management step may be justified.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not buy Zasso with a simplistic expectation that every job is always one pass or always two passes. The pass count should be chosen for the actual job: the weed species, weed stage, crop context, soil and moisture condition, weed density, contact quality, speed, product configuration, safety requirements, and desired control threshold.

For planning, the safest commercial framing is:

- One pass may be enough in easier annual-weed situations where contact and operating conditions are good.
- Two passes may be appropriate for regrowth-prone weeds, dense stands, grasses, mature plants, faster operating strategies, or where uniformity is critical.
- Repeated treatments over a season may be needed when new flushes emerge or perennial reserves remain active.

This helps customers compare Zasso fairly with herbicides, mowing, cultivation, flame, steam, and hand labor. The value of the system depends on the full treatment program, not only on the number of machine passes.

## Safe Sales Wording

"The number of passes depends on the weed, field, crop, speed, contact quality, and required result. In easy conditions one well-executed pass may be enough, while difficult or regrowth-prone situations may need a second pass or follow-up treatment."

"We do not position Zasso as a universal one-pass guarantee. We design the pass strategy around the target weeds, field conditions, operator protocol, and follow-up scouting."

"For perennial weeds, grasses, dense stands, mature weeds, or desiccation work where uniformity matters, planning for follow-up is often more realistic than assuming every field will be finished in one pass."

## Caveats

Do not promise one-pass control, permanent control, guaranteed root kill, or a fixed number of passes across all crops, weeds, soils, seasons, and product configurations. Pass number depends on weed species, plant size, growth stage, root and recovery structures, density, electrode contact, application speed, soil and plant moisture, residue, terrain, crop constraints, safety requirements, and the customer's required result.

Avoid giving universal speed, interval, energy, or retreatment recommendations unless Zasso has approved product-, crop-, weed-, region-, and field-specific guidance. Do not disclose confidential machine settings, control logic, unpublished trial data, customer-specific outcomes, pricing, certification details, telemetry, or private meeting content.
