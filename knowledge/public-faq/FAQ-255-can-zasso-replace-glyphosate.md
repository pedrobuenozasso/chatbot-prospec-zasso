# Can Zasso replace glyphosate?

## Short Answer

Sometimes, yes, but only in defined use cases. Zasso can replace selected glyphosate applications with a non-chemical electrical treatment when the target weeds can be contacted effectively and the crop, site, operating conditions, economics, and safety setup make that practical. The safe customer-facing position is not that Zasso replaces glyphosate everywhere, but that it can replace some glyphosate passes and reduce dependence on glyphosate inside an integrated weed-management program.

## Detailed Answer

Glyphosate became widely used because it is broad-spectrum, familiar, and often inexpensive per treated area. That does not mean it is always the best or only answer. Zasso's Electroherb technology works through a different mechanism: controlled electrical energy is applied through contacted vegetation, causing physical damage to plant tissues rather than relying on glyphosate's biochemical pathway.

That difference creates real replacement potential in some situations. Zasso can be a credible substitute for specific non-selective glyphosate jobs where direct plant contact is practical and where customers have a reason to avoid or reduce spraying. Examples may include inter-row or under-canopy weed control in perennial crops, fence lines, firebreaks and non-crop zones where conditions are suitable, resistant-weed situations, drift-sensitive areas, residue-conscious programs, and some applications where public acceptance or local operating constraints make glyphosate less attractive.

But the answer should stay use-case specific. Replacing one glyphosate pass is not the same as replacing glyphosate as a category. Performance still depends on weed species, plant size, architecture, growth stage, density, moisture, contact quality, travel speed, number of passes, machine fit, and operator training. Mature grasses, dense infestations, deep-rooted perennials, very large-scale burndown programs, and applications that depend on fast broad-area coverage may still be harder to replace fully with electric treatment.

The strongest commercial framing is therefore practical rather than ideological: Zasso can replace some glyphosate applications where a physical, non-chemical treatment fits the job well, but it should not be sold as a blanket universal substitute for glyphosate in every crop, season, or geography.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the most useful question is not "Can Zasso replace glyphosate completely?" It is "Which glyphosate pass can Zasso credibly replace for this weed problem, in this crop or site, under these operating conditions?"

That framing helps customers evaluate the real tradeoff. In some cases, Zasso may become the preferred option because it avoids spray drift from the replaced pass, adds a non-chemical mode of action, and helps manage glyphosate-resistant weeds or public pressure around herbicide use. In other cases, glyphosate may remain part of the broader program while Zasso replaces only the most problematic applications.

The commercial value is still significant even when replacement is partial. If Zasso can take over the passes where glyphosate is least effective, least accepted, or hardest to use safely, it can improve resilience and reduce dependence on one chemistry without requiring an all-or-nothing shift.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can replace some glyphosate applications in suitable use cases, especially where customers want a non-chemical option and direct plant contact is practical."

"The right question is which glyphosate pass Zasso can replace for this customer, not whether glyphosate disappears everywhere."

"Zasso is strongest as a physical, non-chemical tool inside integrated weed management, particularly where resistance, drift, residues, or sensitive-site constraints make glyphosate less robust."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso universally replaces glyphosate in all crops, weeds, field conditions, geographies, or seasons. That is not supported.

Do not imply that Zasso always matches glyphosate on cost, speed, work rate, simplicity, or number of passes. Those points must be validated in the actual use case.

Be especially careful with mature grasses, dense infestations, deep-rooted perennials, very large broad-acre burn-down expectations, and applications where direct plant contact is hard to maintain. In some of those cases, Zasso may complement glyphosate rather than replace it completely.

Do not overstate environmental, regulatory, or safety claims. Zasso avoids applying glyphosate during the treatment itself, but it still requires trained operation, correct setup, and attention to electrical and fire-risk procedures.
