# Is there a risk to bystanders?

## Short Answer

Yes, there can be risk to bystanders if they enter the active treatment area, touch the equipment, touch conductive objects connected to the equipment, or approach during an abnormal fault condition. Zasso equipment is designed for trained professional operation, not for uncontrolled public access during use. Bystander risk is managed through safety distances, clear worksite control, trained operators, stopping high voltage when anyone enters the safety zone, and following the product-specific manual and local rules.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso electrical weeding uses controlled high-voltage energy to treat target plants through applicators or electrodes and the intended plant-soil pathway. A bystander is not intended to be part of that pathway. The safety objective is to keep people outside the active treatment zone and prevent them from touching electrodes, applicators, cables, conductive plant material, metal objects, water paths, the implement, or machine structures while high voltage may be active.

The main bystander risk is unintended electrical exposure. A person could be exposed by directly touching energized electrodes or conductive material touching them, by touching a machine or metal object that has become energized during a fault, or by standing in an area where abnormal current paths create touch or step-potential risks. The seriousness of any exposure depends on current path, available energy, exposure time, ground and moisture conditions, footwear, contact with metal or water, and how quickly the equipment is stopped.

For customers, the practical rule is simple: bystanders should not be near the machine during active operation. Demonstrations, farms, contractors, municipalities, and roadside or public-area users should plan the worksite so unauthorized people cannot walk into the operating area. The operator should maintain the required safety distance, use signage, barriers, spotters, supervision, or local traffic and pedestrian controls where appropriate, and stop the high-voltage output if anyone enters the exclusion zone or if site control is lost.

This does not mean Zasso equipment is unsafe when used correctly. It means electrical weeding should be treated like professional high-voltage agricultural or industrial equipment: safe operation depends on the machine design, approved configuration, trained operator, pre-use inspection, controlled access, emergency stop discipline, maintenance condition, weather and site conditions, and the product-specific manual.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should plan bystander control before starting work. In a closed field this may be straightforward: keep workers, visitors, animals, and vehicles outside the safety zone. In demonstrations, orchards, vineyards, urban areas, roadsides, public land, or contractor work, it may require clearer perimeter management, communication with nearby workers, signage, cones, barriers, a spotter, or stopping work when people approach.

The operator should never assume that curiosity is harmless. People may want to watch the machine closely, touch treated plants, inspect electrodes, lean on the tractor, cross the treatment path, or approach to ask a question. Those situations should be prevented by procedure, not handled casually while the machine is active.

Exact safety distances, PPE requirements, exclusion-zone rules, emergency-stop actions, restart conditions, and maintenance procedures can vary by product, country, certification, applicator, site, crop, terrain, and local regulation. Customers should rely on the approved Zasso manual, training, and local support for the exact rules that apply to their machine and use case.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, bystanders must be kept outside the active treatment area. Zasso equipment is designed for trained professional operation with safety distances, site control, inspections, and shutdown procedures."

"The key rule is that no one should approach, touch the machine, touch electrodes, or enter the exclusion zone while high voltage may be active. If anyone enters the safety zone, the operator should stop the high-voltage output and follow the approved procedure."

"Exact safety distances and site-control requirements are product-specific, so customers should follow the Zasso manual, training, and local rules for their machine and application."

## Caveats

Do not say that bystanders are completely safe, that ordinary distance judgment is enough, that the machine is safe to approach during operation, or that people can stand near the applicators if they do not touch anything. Do not imply that demonstrations, public-area use, roadside work, or municipal work can proceed without planned bystander control.

Do not provide product-specific safety distances, discharge times, rescue instructions, PPE rules, lockout steps, warning-device details, sensor thresholds, certification claims, or emergency procedures unless they are taken from approved customer-facing product documentation.

Bystander risk can vary by product, applicator type, field or public setting, terrain, crop, soil and moisture conditions, weather, nearby metal, water, fences, irrigation, traffic, animals, machine condition, operator training, local regulation, and whether the approved safety procedure is followed.
