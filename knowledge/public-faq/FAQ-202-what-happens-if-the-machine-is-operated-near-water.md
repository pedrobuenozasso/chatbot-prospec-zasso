# What happens if the machine is operated near water?

## Short Answer

Zasso equipment should not be operated near water unless the specific product manual, trained operator procedure, and site risk assessment explicitly allow the condition. Standing water, ditches, canals, ponds, drains, irrigation water, puddles, waterlogged ground, and wet conductive infrastructure can create unintended electrical paths and make the high-voltage danger zone less predictable. The safe customer-facing rule is to stop, keep people and animals away, avoid wet conductive areas, and restart only when the site and machine meet the approved conditions for use.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding uses controlled high-voltage energy through applicators or electrodes, target plants, soil or return paths, and the machine's designed electrical system. Water can change that intended circuit. A wet surface, puddle, watercourse edge, drain, irrigation flow, or saturated soil can lower resistance and connect objects that would otherwise be separated, including vegetation, soil, metal infrastructure, tires, tools, fences, irrigation parts, animals, people, and the machine itself.

Operating near water is therefore different from operating in ordinary field moisture. Some soil moisture may be part of a suitable application window because it affects plant-soil conductivity. Near-water conditions can involve standing water, flowing water, hidden mud, soft banks, wet conductive objects, poor traction, unstable ground, public access, animals, drainage channels, or contact with irrigation and utility infrastructure. These conditions can increase shock, step-voltage, touch-voltage, machine-control, and environmental-site risks.

If water is present inside or close to the work area, the operator should treat it as a stop-and-assess condition. High-voltage operation should not continue through standing water, across waterlogged ground, or near wet conductive infrastructure unless the applicable product manual and trained site procedure explicitly allow it. If the machine has contacted water, wet metal, irrigation equipment, or a waterlogged area, operators should stop high-voltage output, maintain the required exclusion zone, follow the shutdown and discharge procedure, and inspect the equipment only as allowed by training.

The answer is not that Zasso can never be used on land that has moisture nearby. The practical distinction is control. Work may be possible in suitable, load-bearing, inspected conditions where the manual allows it and the danger zone can be controlled. It should not be improvised near ponds, streams, canals, drains, open water, active irrigation, puddles, saturated soil, or wet infrastructure simply because the weeds are reachable.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should identify water hazards before work starts. This includes ponds, lakes, rivers, streams, canals, ditches, drains, culverts, irrigation lines, wet metal, puddles, washdown areas, flooded wheel tracks, waterlogged soil, and places where people or animals may stand on wet ground near the machine.

If water cannot be clearly excluded from the working area or safety perimeter, the job should be redesigned, postponed, or handled under a product-specific approved procedure. Operators should maintain exclusion zones, avoid active irrigation or rainfall, check ground stability and traction, keep bystanders and animals away from wet conductive areas, and stop immediately if alarms, abnormal sparking, water contact, slipping, mud contamination, damaged insulation, or uncertain current paths appear.

For customers, this is mainly a safety and operational-control issue. Zasso technology is non-chemical and does not rely on herbicide residues, but near-water operation still needs conservative high-voltage safety discipline. The absence of chemical spray drift does not remove the need to manage electrical hazards around water.

## Caveats

This FAQ is not a product manual or site-specific safety approval. Exact rules for water proximity, rain, mud, irrigation, puddles, drains, slopes, cleaning, shutdown, discharge checks, PPE, safety distance, alarm handling, inspection, and restart may vary by product, configuration, country, and site.

Do not claim that Zasso equipment can safely operate through standing water, that water contact is harmless, that no alarm means wet operation is safe, or that non-chemical technology removes electrical safety constraints. Do not provide improvised rescue, repair, dewatering, grounding, restart, or troubleshooting instructions beyond the approved Zasso manual, local site procedure, and qualified support.

Do not disclose confidential enclosure ratings, insulation ratings, leakage-current limits, sensor thresholds, fault logic, grounding architecture, discharge times, service procedures, certification files, incident history, customer names, or internal test results unless Zasso has approved them for external use.
