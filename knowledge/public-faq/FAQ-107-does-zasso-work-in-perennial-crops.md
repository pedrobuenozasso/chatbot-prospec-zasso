# Does Zasso work in perennial crops?

## Short Answer

Yes. Zasso can work in perennial crops such as vineyards, orchards, coffee, citrus, blueberries, hazelnuts, and similar high-value systems when the crop geometry, applicator setup, weed stage, soil and plant conditions, and safety procedures are suitable. Perennial crops are one of the strongest application areas for electrical weeding because growers often need repeated weed control without excessive herbicide reliance, soil disturbance, spray drift, or hand labor. The right claim is that Zasso is a serious non-chemical tool for suitable perennial-crop weed-management programs, not a universal one-pass solution for every perennial crop or field condition.

## Detailed Answer

Perennial crops are often a good fit for Zasso because they are managed over many years and usually need repeated weed control in strips, under-row zones, tree rows, inter-row areas, or around crop stems. Growers may want to reduce herbicide use, avoid spray drift, protect soil structure, manage herbicide-resistant weeds, support organic or residue-sensitive production, reduce hand-labor pressure, or limit the root disturbance caused by some mechanical tools.

Zasso's Electroherb technology is contact-based. The electrical current must reach the target weeds through the applicator and the plant-soil circuit. In perennial crops, this means the practical fit depends on whether the system can contact the weeds while protecting crop trunks, stems, shoots, irrigation lines, trellis wires, posts, mulch, workers, animals, and other sensitive infrastructure.

Zasso has public perennial-crop product positioning. The XPS solution is positioned for vineyards and orchards, with fixed and movable electrodes designed to treat weeds near vine and fruit-tree stems. E.Coffee is positioned for coffee, raspberry, blueberry, and similar narrow-row crops in Latin America. E.Citrus is positioned for citrus and similar wider-row crops in Latin America. Existing Zasso FAQ work also supports crop-specific answers for coffee, vineyards, orchards, blueberries, hazelnuts, and citrus, with the same central message: positive fit where the crop system and operating conditions are suitable, and local validation before broad claims.

External research also supports the broader category. Published vineyard work using Zasso XPower/XPS reported substantial weed biomass reduction under tested spring conditions and no evidence of grapevine canopy damage in NDVI observations, while warning about dry-biomass fire risk. Oregon State University work in organic blueberry emphasizes electrode-to-weed contact, slower speed for longer contact time, soil moisture and plant condition, and careful avoidance of crop stems and irrigation lines. A USDA/NIFA project on organic perennial crops treats electric weed control as a promising tool for orchard and berry systems, while explicitly studying performance, economics, safety, and long-term soil and crop effects.

For customers, the answer should therefore be positive but practical. Zasso can be evaluated for perennial crops where weed bands are accessible, the crop and infrastructure can be protected, operators are trained, and the grower can compare Zasso against the current program. In some perennial systems, Zasso may reduce or replace selected herbicide, mowing, hand-labor, flame, or cultivation passes. In others, it may work best as part of an integrated weed-management program rather than as the only tool.

## What This Means for Customers

Perennial-crop customers should see Zasso as a potential non-chemical tool for recurring weed pressure in high-value crops. It may be especially relevant where herbicide reduction, organic compatibility, soil conservation, labor constraints, spray-drift avoidance, residue-sensitive markets, or herbicide-resistance pressure are important.

A good customer evaluation should look at crop type, row spacing, under-row or tree-row width, slope, canopy clearance, trunk or stem exposure, irrigation layout, trellis and metal infrastructure, weed species, weed height, weed density, plant moisture, soil moisture, residue, dry biomass, application speed, number of passes, fire-risk conditions, operator training, and local regulations.

The best commercial framing is a measured pilot in representative blocks. Customers should compare weed control, regrowth, crop safety, operating speed, number of passes, labor need, energy or fuel use, cost per treated hectare or treated strip, and fit with the farm's broader weed-management strategy.

"Perennial crops are one of the most relevant areas for Zasso because growers need repeated weed control and often want to reduce herbicide reliance, labor, spray drift, and soil disturbance."

"We recommend validating Zasso locally in representative blocks and measuring weed control, regrowth, crop safety, operating speed, and cost against the grower's current program."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso works in every perennial crop, every orchard, every vineyard, or every plantation. Suitability depends on row geometry, crop age, crop sensitivity, access, weed species, weed stage, plant and soil moisture, weed density, residue, dry biomass, slope, speed, equipment configuration, operator training, and local regulations.

Do not claim that Zasso is crop-selective if electrodes contact crop tissue. Electrical weeding is contact-based; crop stems, trunks, shoots, roots, irrigation lines, trellis wires, metal posts, workers, animals, and other sensitive objects must be protected through product setup and operating procedures.

Do not promise full herbicide replacement, permanent control, one-pass control, season-long control, guaranteed cost savings, or universal organic acceptance. In many perennial-crop systems, Zasso is best presented as part of integrated weed management.

Be careful in dry biomass, drought, wind, mulch, straw, plastic, and other fire-risk conditions. Public vineyard and extension sources identify fire-risk management as an important operating consideration.

Commercial availability varies by product and region. Public Zasso pages currently position E.Coffee and E.Citrus for Latin America, while XPS is positioned for vineyards and orchards; customer proposals should confirm the available configuration, service model, compliance requirements, and local support.
