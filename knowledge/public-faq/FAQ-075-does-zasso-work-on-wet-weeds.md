# Does Zasso work on wet weeds?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on wet weeds in suitable conditions, but wetness is not automatically good or bad. Moist plant tissue can conduct electricity well, which may support treatment, but very wet soil, rain, standing water, mud, or water films can change the current path and may create safety or operating limits. The practical answer is that wet weeds should be assessed together with soil moisture, weather, crop environment, contact quality, travel speed, and operator safety procedures before application.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding depends on the target weed becoming part of a controlled electrical circuit. Current must pass through contacted plant tissue and the plant-soil system so that enough useful energy reaches stems, crowns, roots, or other recovery structures. Because water and dissolved minerals influence electrical conductivity, moisture in the plant and soil can materially change the treatment result.

Wet, green, actively growing weeds may conduct electricity more readily than very dry or senescent weeds. That can be helpful when electrode contact is good and the current path remains focused through the plant. However, wet field conditions can also reduce efficiency if the soil or surface water becomes an easier pathway than the plant, allowing energy to dissipate away from the target tissue. In saturated soil, puddles, mud, or rain, the issue is not just efficacy; it can also become an equipment, operator-safety, and field-access question.

For that reason, Zasso should not be sold as simply "better in wet weeds." A better customer-facing explanation is that moisture is one of several operating variables. Slightly moist conditions may support conductivity and lower fire risk, while excessively wet, rainy, muddy, or waterlogged conditions may require delaying treatment, slowing travel speed, changing timing, or reassessing whether the application is appropriate.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should expect a field-condition check before treating wet weeds. Operators should look at whether the weeds are wet from dew, irrigation, rain, or active growth; whether the soil is slightly moist, saturated, muddy, or variable; whether there is standing water; whether the machine can maintain safe contact and stable travel; and whether local operating and safety procedures allow treatment.

In suitable conditions, wet weeds can be part of a non-chemical weed-control program, especially where customers want to reduce herbicide reliance, manage resistant weeds, avoid chemical residues, or work in organic-compatible systems. In unsuitable wet conditions, the right recommendation is to wait for a safer and more controllable application window rather than promise the same result in all moisture situations.

Oregon State University Extension guidance for electrical weed control in organic blueberry fields states that slightly moist soil can provide better conductivity, while efficiency can drop if soil is too dry or too wet. DPIRD research using Zasso XPower equipment reports that electric weed control passes current through foliage, roots, and soil, and that higher soil moisture can reduce efficiency by lowering soil resistance and increasing the chance that current dissipates out of the roots. In the reported trials, wetter soil reduced annual ryegrass control in one year, while a narrower moisture range did not show the same effect in another year: https://library.dpird.wa.gov.au/conf_papers/299/

It also emphasizes that the technology relies on electrode contact and adequate dose through the target plant: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/electric-weed-controlhow-does-it-compare-to-conventional-weed-control-methods/488C45B191B97E84E34257E80EB9CFC6

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can treat wet weeds in suitable conditions, but moisture has to be managed. Green, hydrated weeds may conduct well, while saturated soil, rain, mud, or standing water can change the current path and may create safety or operating limits."

"We do not treat wetness as a blanket advantage. We check plant moisture, soil condition, weather, contact quality, speed, and safety procedures before recommending the application window."

"Slightly moist conditions can be workable, but rainy, waterlogged, or unsafe field conditions are a stop-and-assess situation."

## Caveats

Do not claim that wet weeds are always easier to control or that wet soil always improves efficacy. Moisture can improve conductivity in some situations, but excessive moisture can allow energy to dissipate through soil or water rather than through the target plant.

Do not recommend operation in rain, standing water, saturated soil, mud, or other unsafe conditions without checking the applicable product manual, local safety procedures, training requirements, field-access limits, and regulatory or site rules.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, safety thresholds, control logic, telemetry, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
