# Does it harm crop plants?

## Short Answer

Zasso's electrical weeding is designed to treat unwanted vegetation, not the crop, but it is not chemically selective between weeds and crop plants. If a crop plant is contacted by the electrodes or becomes part of the effective current path, it may be injured. Crop safety therefore depends on correct applicator choice, crop spacing, guidance, shielding, operating speed, field conditions, operator training, and local crop-specific validation.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding controls plants by delivering electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit. The system can be highly targeted in practice because the applicator is positioned toward the treatment zone, such as inter-row weeds, under-vine vegetation, orchard rows, berry rows, non-crop strips, or other directed areas. However, the electrical mode of action does not know whether the contacted plant is a weed or a crop.

That distinction is important for safe customer wording. Zasso can be used near crops when the equipment and operating protocol are designed to keep crop tissue outside the treatment path. In perennial crops, that may involve applicator geometry, shielding, steering accuracy, maintaining separation from trunks, canes, crowns, irrigation lines, and young shoots, and operating at speeds that preserve control without creating unnecessary crop-contact risk. In row crops, the strongest current positioning is directed or inter-row use where weeds are contactable and the crop is protected.

Available public field evidence supports this conditional answer. Oregon State University Extension reported organic blueberry work with Zasso equipment in which no crop injury was observed when electrodes were kept away from the crop. GROW IWM described proof-of-concept row-crop work where continuous-contact electrocution controlled weeds between rows without injuring the crop, while also noting that weeds inside the crop row remained and may require other tactics. Zasso's own public vineyard material reported no observed vine damage in the cited IFV vineyard testing, while also emphasizing the importance of crop structure, timing, weed cover, and operating conditions.

For customers, the practical answer is: Zasso can be crop-compatible in suitable and validated applications, but crop plants should not be treated as automatically protected. Crop safety comes from design and discipline: the right machine, right applicator, right treatment zone, trained operation, and crop-specific validation.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should evaluate Zasso crop safety by crop system, not as a generic yes-or-no claim. A mature vineyard, an orchard, an organic blueberry field, a cotton field, a soybean field, a vegetable crop, and a young nursery planting each present different crop-contact risks and operating requirements.

In suitable applications, Zasso can help manage weeds near crops while avoiding chemical drift, chemical residues, and broad soil disturbance. That can be valuable in organic production, perennial crops, sensitive areas, and herbicide-reduction programs. The best starting point is a local demonstration or validation protocol that checks weed control, crop contact, visible crop injury, regrowth, operating speed, field conditions, and follow-up needs before broader adoption.

Customers should also understand that crop safety and weed efficacy can trade off if the applicator is set too far from the weeds, driven too fast, or used in dense vegetation where weeds and crop tissue are intermingled. Good agronomy is part of the technology.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can be used near crops in suitable, validated applications, but the crop must be kept outside the treatment path. The technology treats contacted vegetation, so crop safety depends on applicator design, guidance, shielding, and trained operation."

"In perennial crops and directed row-crop applications, Zasso is best positioned as a targeted, non-chemical tool for weeds that are reachable without contacting the crop."

"We recommend local validation for each crop, growth stage, and field setup before making broad crop-safety claims."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is universally safe for all crop plants. Direct electrode contact, poor guidance, incorrect applicator setup, intermingled crop and weed growth, young or sensitive crop tissue, wet bridging vegetation, irrigation infrastructure, dense biomass, unsuitable speed, or unvalidated protocols can increase crop-injury risk.

Do not claim that Zasso is crop-selective in the same way a selective herbicide may be. Selectivity comes from physical targeting, crop avoidance, applicator geometry, shielding, operator training, and validated operating conditions.

Do not promise no crop injury, no yield effect, universal row-crop compatibility, universal perennial-crop compatibility, or full in-row weed control. Avoid disclosing confidential machine settings, unpublished trial results, customer names, private operating procedures, pricing, certification timelines, or sensitive engineering details.
