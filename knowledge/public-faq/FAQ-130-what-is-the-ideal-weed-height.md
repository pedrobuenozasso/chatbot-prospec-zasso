# What is the ideal weed height?

## Short Answer

There is no single ideal weed height for every Zasso application. The best height is when the target weeds are emerged, visible, and reliably reachable by the electrodes, but not so tall, dense, woody, lodged, or shielded that contact and energy delivery become difficult. In many early post-emergence situations, young to moderately developed weeds are attractive targets; in some late escape-control situations, taller weeds can also be suitable when height separation from the crop allows safe, selective contact.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's electrical weeding is a contact-and-energy process, so weed height matters mainly because it affects electrode contact, exposure time, crop clearance, and the amount of plant tissue that must be damaged. A weed that is too small, hidden under residue, below the contact zone, or shielded by neighboring plants may not receive enough contact. A weed that is very tall, dense, woody, lodged, or reserve-rich may be easy to touch but may need slower operation, better applicator positioning, repeated passes, mowing before treatment, or integration with other tools.

For early weed management, the preferred window is often after the weed has emerged and can be contacted consistently, but before it has accumulated unnecessary biomass, large root reserves, thick stems, or seed production risk. This is why small to moderate emerged weeds are commonly a good practical target. However, very small seedlings are not automatically ideal if the applicator cannot reach them.

For late-season escape control, height can work differently. Some electrical weed-control systems use height separation: the target weed stands above the crop or accessible zone, allowing electrodes to contact the weed while avoiding the crop. In those cases, taller weeds may be useful, provided the crop, operator safety, machine setup, and local protocol allow it.

The safest rule is to define ideal height by field and product setup, not by a universal centimeter value. Customers should assess whether the target weeds are reachable, whether the crop or residue blocks contact, whether weed density causes shielding, whether the weeds are still physiologically suitable for control, and whether the operating speed and pass plan give enough contact time.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should plan Zasso applications around reliable contact rather than a fixed weed-height number. If weeds are visible and reachable while still young or moderately developed, treatment can reduce biomass, competition, regrowth potential, and later seed return. If weeds are too small to contact, too dense, flattened, hidden under mulch or crop residue, or protected by canopy, timing or field preparation may need adjustment.

For difficult fields, customers may need a more deliberate protocol: slower speed, a different applicator setup, mowing before electrical treatment where appropriate, sequential passes, or follow-up scouting. For crop situations where selectivity depends on height separation, the useful height window must also protect the crop from unintended contact.

The practical field question is: can the electrodes touch the target weeds consistently, safely, and long enough to deliver the required energy? If the answer is yes, the height is likely within a workable window. If the answer is no, the treatment plan should be adjusted.

## Safe Sales Wording

"There is no universal ideal weed height. The best target is a weed that is emerged, visible, and consistently reachable by the electrodes while still manageable for the selected Zasso product and field protocol."

"Young to moderate emerged weeds are often a good timing window, but very small weeds can be missed if the electrodes do not contact them. Taller weeds can also be suitable in some escape-control situations when crop clearance and safety allow it."

"For each field, we set the timing by contact quality, weed density, crop safety, soil and plant conditions, operating speed, and the customer's control objective."

## Caveats

Do not promise a universal ideal height, guaranteed one-pass control, or the same result across all crops, weeds, soils, products, and operators. Weed height interacts with species, morphology, growth stage, root reserves, density, lodging, crop canopy, residue, soil moisture, plant moisture, electrode setup, speed, power delivery, safety rules, and pass strategy.

Do not imply that Zasso controls weeds before emergence or provides residual control against future germination. Avoid publishing product-specific height, speed, voltage, power, or dose recommendations unless they come from approved product guidance for the relevant crop and market. Do not disclose confidential settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
