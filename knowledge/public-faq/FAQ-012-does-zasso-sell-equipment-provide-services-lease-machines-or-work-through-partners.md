# Does Zasso sell equipment, provide services, lease machines, or work through partners?

## Short Answer

Zasso can work through several commercial models, depending on the country, product, application, certification status, and local support structure. In some markets, customers may buy equipment; in others, Zasso may work through distributors, OEM partners, local representatives, service contractors, rentals, leasing, training, or technology partnerships. The right route should always be confirmed for the customer's location, crop or site, operating model, and after-sales requirements.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso is not limited to a single sales model. Its core business is the development and commercialization of electric-weeding technology, and that technology can reach customers through different channels. For some customers, the relationship may look like a conventional equipment purchase. For others, it may involve a local partner, a distributor, an OEM or technology-integration partner, a service contractor, a demonstration program, a rental or leasing arrangement, or a broader deployment package that includes training, commissioning, maintenance, spare parts, and operator support.

This flexibility is important because electric weeding is both a technology and an operational system. A vineyard, orchard, municipality, landscaping contractor, row-crop grower, coffee or citrus grower, and infrastructure operator may all need different machines, applicators, power configurations, safety procedures, training, and service coverage. The best commercial route therefore depends on the use case and on the support network available in that region.

Public Zasso materials support this broad model. Zasso describes itself as designing and commercializing electric-weeding technologies for agriculture and municipalities, and its public site addresses farmers, landscapers, service providers, and consumers. Zasso also identifies strategic partnerships, while public New Holland and AGXTEND materials show that some XPower solutions have been commercialized through partner channels. Recent Zasso public news also shows distribution partnerships for specific products, such as the eWeeding Roller in Benelux.

Internal Zasso context is consistent with this picture. Existing FAQ answers, commercial-planning materials, SharePoint search results, and recent Read.AI meeting summaries all indicate that Zasso's go-to-market can combine equipment, partners, services, demonstrations, rentals or leasing, training, certification, post-sales support, telemetry, and local representation. These internal sources support the model-level answer, but confidential details such as pricing, customer names, contract terms, sales targets, financing, certification timelines, and specific partner negotiations should not be shared externally unless specifically approved.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not assume there is only one way to access Zasso technology. A customer may be able to buy a machine, work with a local distributor or representative, use a service provider, participate in a demonstration or pilot, rent or lease equipment, or access technology through a partner-branded solution, depending on the market and product.

The practical next step is to define the customer's country, crop or site type, weed-control objective, carrier vehicle, operating season, operator capability, safety requirements, and expected support level. Zasso can then confirm whether the most appropriate route is equipment sale, rental, service provision, partner delivery, OEM integration, or another commercial structure.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can support customers through different commercial routes, including equipment sales, partners, service models, rentals or leasing, training, and after-sales support where available. The right model depends on the product, country, certification status, application, and local support network, so we confirm the best route case by case."

"For customers, the important point is that Zasso is not only selling hardware; we help make electric weeding deployable through the right machine, partner channel, training, and support model for the use case."

## Caveats

Do not promise that every model is available in every country or for every product. Equipment sale, rental, leasing, service provision, distributor support, OEM integration, training, certification, warranty, and spare-parts coverage all depend on local market structure, product status, certification, partner readiness, and commercial agreement.

Avoid quoting confidential pricing, discounts, financing terms, contract language, sales targets, customer names, private pilots, partner negotiations, certification timelines, or internal post-sales platform details. For live opportunities, confirm the current approved commercial route before making commitments to a customer.
