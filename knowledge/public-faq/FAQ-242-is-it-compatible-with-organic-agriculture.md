# Is it compatible with organic agriculture?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can be compatible with organic agriculture because it is a non-chemical, physical weed-control technology rather than a synthetic herbicide application. In certified organic production, the specific use should still be confirmed with the applicable organic certifier, local rules, crop program, and farm organic system plan. Zasso should be positioned as a tool that can support organic weed management, not as a universal organic certification by itself.

## Detailed Answer

Organic agriculture normally relies on prevention, crop rotation, soil and crop management, sanitation, mulching, mowing, grazing, mechanical cultivation, hand weeding, and other physical or cultural weed-management practices before using approved input substances. Zasso fits this general logic because it controls emerged weeds by applying electrical energy through contacted plant tissue, without spraying herbicide active ingredients onto the crop or soil.

The compatibility case is strongest where organic growers need an additional non-chemical option and where weeds are reachable by the electrodes without unacceptable crop contact. This can be relevant in permanent crops, berries, orchards, vineyards, field margins, non-crop areas, transition-to-organic fields, and selected row-crop or vegetable systems where the machine configuration and operating plan are appropriate.

Organic compatibility is still not automatic approval in every country or farm. Organic standards are interpreted through local regulation, certifiers, buyer requirements, and farm-specific organic system plans. A grower should therefore document Zasso as a physical weed-control practice where required, confirm cleaning and recordkeeping expectations, and validate the intended use with the certifier or organic advisor before making formal claims.

Zasso also should not be presented as a complete organic weed-management program by itself. It treats emerged vegetation and has no residual soil activity, so new weeds can emerge after treatment. Good organic use depends on timing, weed stage, soil and plant moisture, operating speed, electrode contact, crop protection, safety procedures, and integration with the broader organic management plan.

## What This Means for Customers

For organic growers, Zasso can add a practical non-chemical tool to the weed-control toolbox. It may help reduce dependence on hand labor, repeated cultivation, flame, steam, or limited organic-approved contact products in suitable applications.

For customers selling into organic or residue-sensitive markets, the main commercial value is that Zasso can control weeds without adding herbicide residues from the treatment itself and without the spray-drift pathway associated with chemical applications. This can support organic, transition-to-organic, and chemical-reduction programs when the use is properly validated and documented.

Before adoption, customers should define the target crop, weed species, weed size, treatment zone, crop shielding, application timing, operating speed, number of passes, soil moisture, safety controls, fire-risk conditions, cleaning procedure, and recordkeeping approach. A small local validation is the safest first step before scaling use across a certified organic operation.

## Evidence and Context

USDA organic rules at 7 CFR 205.206 require producers to use preventive, cultural, mechanical, and physical weed-management practices, and the weed-control section explicitly includes flame, heat, or electrical means as ways weed problems may be controlled: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-I/subchapter-M/part-205/subpart-C/section-205.206

USDA AMS guidance on the crop pest, weed, and disease management practice standard describes mechanical and physical practices as part of the organic management hierarchy: https://www.ams.usda.gov/grades-standards/crop-pest-weed-and-disease-management-practice-standard

European Commission organic-production guidance states that organic production aims to reduce the use of external inputs and that substances used in organic agriculture for plant protection must be pre-approved: https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/organic-farming/organic-production-and-products_en

Oregon State University Extension's 2026 guidance on electrical weed control in organic blueberry fields describes electrical weed control as a promising nonchemical tool for organic growers when speed, timing, crop protection, soil moisture, and fire-risk precautions are managed correctly: https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9716-how-speed-timing-affect-electrical-weed-control-organic-blueberry-fields

A 2025 Frontiers in Agronomy article reports electrical weed-control field studies in certified-organic highbush blueberry systems and found that slower operating speeds delivered stronger weed control and biomass reduction than faster single passes: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2025.1688961/full

Zasso public materials position Electroherb as a non-chemical electric weed-control solution and discuss recent Oregon field-trial evidence for electric weed-control performance: https://zasso.com/technology/ and https://zasso.com/electric-weeding-from-innovation-to-proven-field-performance/

Existing Zasso FAQ files reviewed for this answer include FAQ-016 on organic compatibility and FAQ-105 on organic crops. Those files support the same customer-safe framing: Zasso is a physical, non-chemical tool that can fit organic systems where the specific use is accepted by the relevant rules and certifier.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can be compatible with organic agriculture because it is a non-chemical physical weed-control technology, but the specific use should always be confirmed with the farm's organic certifier or advisor."

"For organic growers, Zasso is best described as an additional tool inside integrated weed management, alongside prevention, rotation, mulching, mowing, cultivation, hand weeding, and other approved practices."

"We do not present Zasso as a blanket organic certification; we present it as an organic-compatible weed-control option in suitable applications and under the applicable local rules."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is automatically certified organic in every country, crop, standard, buyer program, or farm. Organic approval depends on the applicable rules, certifier interpretation, machine configuration, cleaning practices, records, and the farm's organic system plan.

Do not imply that Zasso is selective to crops. Direct contact with crop plants can cause injury, so suitability depends on crop architecture, spacing, shielding, electrode configuration, operator training, and application discipline.

Do not imply residual weed prevention. Zasso treats emerged vegetation; new weeds may germinate after treatment, and follow-up scouting or additional weed-management practices may be needed.

Do not overstate performance across all organic systems. Public evidence is strong for the organic-compatibility rationale and promising for organic blueberry applications, but crop-by-crop performance still requires local validation.
