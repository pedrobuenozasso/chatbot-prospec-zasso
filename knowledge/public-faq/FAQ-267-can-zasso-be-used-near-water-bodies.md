# Can Zasso be used near water bodies?

## Short Answer

Sometimes, yes, in suitable applications and with strict site control. Because Zasso is a non-chemical electrical weed-control method, it can be relevant where customers want to reduce herbicide drift, runoff, or residue concerns near ditches, canals, ponds, or other water-adjacent areas. However, near-water use is never automatic: the site must be dry or otherwise explicitly permitted by the applicable product procedure, and local environmental and electrical-safety rules still have to be respected.

## Detailed Answer

This question has two parts that need to be separated clearly. The first is environmental fit. The second is operational safety.

From an environmental-positioning standpoint, Zasso can be attractive near water bodies because the treatment itself does not apply a chemical herbicide. That means the Zasso pass does not create herbicide spray drift, and it does not add herbicide active ingredients or chemical residues to nearby water from the treatment itself. In locations where the main concern is chemical use near streams, ditches, drainage canals, ponds, retention basins, irrigation corridors, or other water-adjacent assets, that can make Zasso commercially relevant.

But near-water use is not the same as unrestricted use. Zasso equipment uses high-voltage electricity, and water can change conductivity, create unintended current paths, reduce predictability, and increase site risk. For that reason, the right customer-facing position is conditional: Zasso may be suitable near water bodies when the actual treatment zone is dry or otherwise approved for the relevant product, the weeds are accessible, the work area can be controlled, and the operation is carried out under the correct trained procedure.

It should not be positioned as a tool for submerged aquatic weeds, floating vegetation, standing water, waterlogged ground, active irrigation flow, or improvised operation right at the water edge without a site assessment. In practice, the best fit is usually emerged terrestrial or bank-side vegetation in clearly defined zones where customers want a non-chemical option but still have safe access, exclusion control, and local permission to operate.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the main value is that Zasso can help address one of the biggest concerns around vegetation management near water: the desire to avoid or reduce chemical herbicide use in sensitive areas. That can matter for municipalities, drainage and irrigation districts, contractors, orchards, vineyards, public infrastructure, and other operations where water quality, public perception, or site restrictions make conventional spraying difficult.

The practical screen is simple. If the target is emerged vegetation in a controllable, non-submerged, safely accessible area, Zasso may be a strong option to evaluate. If the site involves standing water, unstable wet banks, submerged plants, active irrigation water, unclear safety distances, or uncertain local rules, the operation should not be treated as routine.

Customers should therefore evaluate near-water use case by case. The decision should consider the type of vegetation, distance to open water, bank conditions, soil wetness, public access, machine setup, operator training, local environmental requirements, and the specific product manual or approved operating procedure.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Often it can be evaluated near water-adjacent areas when the goal is to avoid herbicide drift or residue concerns, and when the actual treatment zone can be operated safely under the correct procedure."

"Zasso is best positioned for emerged terrestrial or bank-side vegetation, not for submerged aquatic weeds or standing-water operation."

"The final answer always depends on the exact site, local rules, and whether the work area can be controlled safely."

## Caveats

Do not say Zasso can be used in any location near water without restrictions. That would overstate both the safety and regulatory side of the answer.

Do not position Zasso as a submerged-aquatic-weed solution, a blanket replacement for aquatic herbicides, or a license to operate in standing water, waterlogged ground, or active irrigation flow.

Do not use this FAQ as legal, environmental-permitting, or electrical-safety clearance for a specific canal, pond, stream, wetland, reservoir, irrigation district, or public-water site. Those decisions require local review and the applicable product procedure.
