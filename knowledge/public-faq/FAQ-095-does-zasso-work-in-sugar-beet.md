# Does Zasso work in sugar beet?

## Short Answer

Yes. Sugar beet is one of the stronger crop-specific examples for Zasso because public Zasso material and scientific review literature both support electrical weed control in sugar beet systems, especially before emergence and between crop rows. The strongest claim is not that Zasso is selective to sugar beet plants, but that it can control contacted weeds in suitable sugar beet applications when the crop is protected from electrode contact and the protocol is locally validated.

## Detailed Answer

Sugar beet can be a good fit for Zasso because the crop is commonly grown in rows, early weed competition is important, and growers often need precise weed-control tools during the crop's low-competition juvenile stage. Zasso's Electroherb technology is a physical, non-chemical mode of action: the electrodes must contact the target vegetation, and the crop should be avoided unless a specific application protocol has been validated.

There are two credible sugar beet use cases. The first is pre-emergence or stale-seedbed weed control, where sugar beet plants are not yet exposed and crop-contact risk is lower. Zasso's public sugar beet material reports that 2019 field trials showed a strong reduction in weed density and that pre-emergence Electroherb treatments had a positive effect on early sugar beet development under the tested conditions.

The second use case is inter-row post-emergence weed control. In this scenario, the electrical treatment is placed between sugar beet rows to contact weeds while avoiding the crop. Zasso's public inter-row material and related SharePoint marketing material report high weed reduction in sugar beet trials using an XPower inter-row applicator. A Weed Science review also summarizes sugar beet work with Zasso XPower and a prototype XPR applicator, reporting strong control in double or triple inter-row applications when used with a regionally appropriate banded herbicide regime.

The key limitation is selectivity. Electrical weeding is non-selective toward contacted plants, so sugar beet leaves, crowns, or roots should not be exposed to unintended electrode contact. The commercial answer should therefore focus on validated pre-emergence and inter-row applications, not on unrestricted over-the-top treatment.

## What This Means for Customers

For sugar beet growers, Zasso can be positioned as a practical tool inside integrated weed management. It may help reduce reliance on full-field herbicide programs, reduce soil disturbance compared with cultivation, and add a physical mode of action where resistant weeds, regulatory pressure, organic production, or herbicide-reduction goals are important.

A customer evaluation should start with the actual use case: pre-emergence burndown, inter-row post-emergence control, herbicide reduction, organic production, or support for a banded-herbicide program. The most important field factors are row spacing, crop stage, weed stage, weed species, weed density, soil and plant moisture, residue level, guidance accuracy, electrode configuration, speed, and whether weeds inside the crop row need a complementary tactic.

The strongest sales position is that Zasso has credible sugar beet evidence for suitable pre-emergence and inter-row use, but performance and crop safety depend on the right equipment setup and local validation.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is chemically or biologically selective to sugar beet. Crop safety depends on timing, row geometry, guidance, electrode configuration, crop stage, and avoidance of direct crop contact.

Do not claim universal sugar beet efficacy, one-pass control, complete in-row control, full herbicide replacement, guaranteed yield improvement, or identical results across all regions. Inter-row treatment may not control weeds hidden inside the crop row, under canopy, protected by residue, too small for reliable contact, or emerging after treatment.

Do not overstate environmental claims. Zasso can reduce chemical residue and soil-disturbance issues in suitable applications, but local safety procedures, fire-risk assessment, soil and plant moisture, machine setup, and operator training remain important.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, private SharePoint filenames, private meeting content, product-roadmap details, certification timelines, or non-public commercial commitments.
