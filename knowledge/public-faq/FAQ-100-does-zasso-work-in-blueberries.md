# Does Zasso work in blueberries?

## Short Answer

Yes. Zasso can work in blueberries when the equipment is configured for the planting system, the weeds are reachable by the electrodes, and the crop, crowns, canes, mulch, drip lines, and other infrastructure are protected from direct contact. Blueberry-specific field research in organic highbush blueberry has shown that electrical weed control can provide strong non-chemical weed suppression under suitable conditions, especially when speed, timing, soil moisture, and number of passes are managed carefully. It should be positioned as a locally validated tool for blueberry weed management, not as a guaranteed one-pass or all-condition solution.

## Detailed Answer

Blueberries are a relevant crop for Zasso because weed control in blueberry rows is difficult, especially in organic systems and in fields with perennial weeds. Blueberry plantings often include shallow roots, mulch, plastic weed mat, drip irrigation, narrow access, berms, and sensitive crop tissue near the weed-control zone. That makes crop protection and applicator setup just as important as weed-kill performance.

Zasso's Electroherb technology is contact-based: the electrodes must touch the target weeds and avoid direct contact with the crop. In blueberries, the practical application is usually directed along the row shoulder, berm edge, or weed strip where weeds can be contacted while shielding the blueberry crowns and canes. When this is done properly, electrical weed control can provide a non-chemical option that complements mowing, mulching, hand weeding, organic herbicides, and other integrated weed-management tactics.

The strongest public evidence found for this FAQ comes from Oregon State University and peer-reviewed blueberry research. OSU Extension reports field studies from 2022 to 2024 in five commercial certified organic blueberry fields using commercial Zasso systems, including Raiden and XPower XPS. The work found that slower travel speeds improved control, that repeated passes at moderate speed can maintain weed pressure, and that mowing before electrical weed control can improve efficacy in dense or tall weeds. A related Frontiers in Agronomy article reported 63% to 89% total weed control at slower speeds in organic blueberries under the tested conditions, with meaningful biomass reduction compared with untreated plots.

University of California research also supports blueberry relevance. UC ANR reported organic blueberry and almond trials using a Zasso tractor-mounted electrical weed-control unit, with blueberry treatments focused on berm shoulders and evaluated for weed-control efficacy and crop safety. Zasso's public website also links to that UC research update and frames organic almonds and blueberries as active research use cases.

## What This Means for Customers

For blueberry growers, Zasso can be considered as a non-chemical weed-control tool where herbicide reduction, organic production, labor pressure, resistance management, or reduced soil disturbance are important. It may be especially useful against emerged weeds that are visible and contactable, and where repeated operations can be planned around weed regrowth.

A blueberry evaluation should look at planting age, row spacing, berm shape, mulch type, plastic weed mat condition, drip-line position, crop-crown exposure, weed species, weed height, weed density, soil moisture, plant moisture, operating speed, applicator width, shielding, number of passes, fire-risk conditions, and operator training. Customers should measure weed control, regrowth, crop safety, irrigation safety, operating speed, labor savings, and seasonal economics compared with the current program.

The best customer expectation is a measured pilot. Zasso may reduce reliance on hand labor, repeated mowing, cultivation, organic contact herbicides, or selected chemical herbicide passes, depending on the production system and local rules. However, it should normally be integrated into a broader blueberry weed-management program rather than sold as a standalone guarantee.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can work in blueberries where the weeds are reachable and the applicator is set up to protect the crop, mulch, and irrigation. Blueberry-specific university research has shown promising non-chemical weed-control results under suitable conditions."

"For blueberries, Zasso should be validated locally around row geometry, crop age, weed species, soil moisture, mulch, drip lines, operating speed, and fire-risk conditions."

"The strongest fit is as part of integrated weed management: Zasso can help control contacted emerged weeds and reduce reliance on some other tactics, while follow-up control may still be needed for regrowth or weeds the electrodes do not contact."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is automatically safe for blueberry plants. Electrical weeding is contact-based; electrodes must avoid crop crowns, canes, roots, drip lines, plastic mulch, posts, wires, and other sensitive infrastructure.

Do not promise full herbicide replacement, permanent control, one-pass control, universal organic approval, or lower cost in every blueberry operation. Results depend on weed species, weed stage, weed density, soil moisture, mulch and weed-mat conditions, operating speed, pass number, equipment configuration, operator training, and local regulations.

Dry vegetation, hot or windy weather, plastic mulch, and residue can increase operational and fire-risk concerns. Operators should follow Zasso guidance, local fire rules, and site-specific no-go conditions.

Blueberry evidence is strong for the tested organic highbush systems in Oregon and supportive UC trials, but customers in other regions, varieties, soil types, production systems, and climates should validate performance locally before making commercial commitments.
