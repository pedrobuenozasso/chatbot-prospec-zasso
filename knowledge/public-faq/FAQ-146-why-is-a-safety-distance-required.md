# Why is a safety distance required?

## Short Answer

A safety distance is required because electrical weeding uses high-voltage energy near plants, soil, metal objects, water, people, animals, and moving machinery. The distance keeps people and animals outside the area where they could touch energized electrodes, conductive materials, machine parts, or field objects, or be exposed to abnormal step-voltage, touch-voltage, arcing, or fault conditions. It is one part of a wider safety system that also includes trained operators, inspections, warnings, shutdown procedures, and the product-specific manual.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's technology is designed to deliver controlled electrical energy into target vegetation through the applicator and the intended plant-soil pathway. During safe operation, the objective is to keep that energy in the treatment circuit and away from people, animals, bystanders, tools, machine structures, crop supports, irrigation hardware, fences, wet vegetation, or other conductive objects.

The safety distance creates a controlled no-entry area around the active treatment zone. This matters because the relevant hazard is not only direct contact with an electrode. A person could also be exposed by touching conductive material that is connected to an electrode, by touching metal or water that has become part of an unintended path, by standing in a soil voltage gradient during an abnormal condition, or by approaching a moving implement whose real hazard boundary changes with crop, soil, moisture, speed, electrode position, and field objects.

This is why the distance should be treated as a practical operating rule, not a formality. It gives the operator time and space to notice bystanders, animals, obstacles, wet conditions, damaged parts, unusual sparking, alarms, or loss of site control before anyone enters a hazardous area. If the safety zone is breached, the conservative action is to stop high-voltage output and follow the approved product procedure before continuing.

A safety distance does not replace machine design. It works together with insulation, guarding, grounding or return-path management, fault detection, alarms, emergency stop functions, discharge procedures, maintenance controls, PPE where required, and operator training. Exact distances and procedures can vary by product, applicator, country, certification, site, crop, soil, moisture, weather, public access, and local regulation.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should plan the safety distance before energizing the equipment. In a closed agricultural field, this may mean keeping workers, visitors, animals, vehicles, and tools outside the active operating zone. In demonstrations, orchards, vineyards, public areas, municipalities, roadsides, or contractor work, it may also require signage, cones, barriers, a spotter, traffic or pedestrian controls, and a clear stop-work rule.

The customer benefit is disciplined, repeatable operation. A defined safety distance helps prevent unsafe curiosity, accidental approach, contact with electrodes, contact with conductive objects, and confusion during startup, operation, shutdown, or maintenance. It also helps sales teams and operators communicate the same message: Zasso equipment is professional high-voltage equipment and should be used only under trained, controlled conditions.

Customers should not improvise a distance based on appearance or comfort. The correct distance and related precautions should come from the approved Zasso manual, training, product configuration, and local rules for the specific machine and application.

## Safe Sales Wording

"A safety distance is required because electrical weeding uses high-voltage energy in a real field environment. It keeps people and animals away from electrodes, conductive objects, moving equipment, and possible abnormal electrical paths."

"The safety distance is not just about avoiding direct contact. It also helps manage indirect contact, wet or metallic paths, step and touch voltage, arcing, faults, and bystander movement."

"The exact distance is product-specific, so customers should follow the approved Zasso manual, training, site controls, and local safety rules for their machine."

## Caveats

Do not present the safety distance as a guarantee that all risk is eliminated. It is a necessary control, but safe operation also depends on product design, trained operators, inspections, alarms, shutdown procedures, maintenance discipline, PPE where required, site control, weather and soil conditions, and local regulation.

Do not publish product-specific distances, discharge times, sensor thresholds, fault-current thresholds, voltage or current values, detailed emergency procedures, certification claims, or internal test results unless they are already approved for external use. The approved manual and training always take priority over this general FAQ.

Safety distance requirements can change with product configuration, applicator geometry, crop, soil moisture, wet vegetation, irrigation, metal objects, fences, trellises, terrain, public access, animals, weather, fire risk, machine condition, operator training, and local regulation.
