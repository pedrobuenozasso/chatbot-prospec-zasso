# Can Zasso be used in organic-certified production?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can be used in organic-certified production where the specific use is accepted by the applicable organic standard, certifier, and farm organic system plan. Because Zasso is a non-chemical, physical weed-control technology, it can fit well in certified organic systems as part of integrated weed management. The safest customer-facing position is that Zasso may support organic-certified production, but the grower should confirm the intended use with the certifier or organic advisor before making a compliance claim.

## Detailed Answer

Organic-certified production is not only a field-performance question. It is also a compliance and documentation question. Zasso controls weeds by applying electrical energy through contacted plant tissue rather than by applying a conventional synthetic herbicide, which makes it a physical weed-control method rather than a chemical input. That is why it can be a strong fit for certified organic operations that need non-chemical weed management.

In the United States, USDA organic rules explicitly allow weed problems to be controlled through mechanical and physical methods and specifically include flame, heat, or electrical means. That supports the general compatibility case for electrical weed control in certified organic farming. Public guidance from USDA also frames physical and mechanical practices as part of the normal organic pest, weed, and disease management hierarchy. In the European Union, organic production also follows a reduced-input logic and tightly controls plant-protection substances, which supports the positioning of electrical weeding as a physical alternative rather than a prohibited herbicide input.

Even so, certified organic use should never be described as automatic approval in every country, certifier program, crop, or farm. Organic certification is implemented through local rules, certifier interpretation, buyer requirements, machine use conditions, cleaning practices, records, and the farm's organic system plan. The right customer-safe statement is therefore conditional: Zasso can be used in organic-certified production where the specific application is accepted by the relevant certifier and managed according to the farm's organic procedures.

Public crop-specific evidence also supports this positioning. Oregon State University published 2026 guidance from certified-organic blueberry field studies using Zasso equipment and presented electrical weed control as a promising nonchemical tool when speed, timing, crop shielding, soil moisture, and fire-risk precautions are managed properly. A related 2025 peer-reviewed article in Frontiers in Agronomy reported meaningful weed control and biomass reduction in certified-organic highbush blueberry systems, especially at slower operating speeds.

## What This Means for Customers

For certified organic growers, Zasso can add a non-chemical weed-control option that may reduce reliance on hand labor, repeated cultivation, or limited organic-approved contact products in suitable applications. It can be especially relevant where growers want herbicide-free weed control, need another tool for perennial or difficult weeds, or want to reduce soil disturbance in treatment zones.

In practical commercial terms, customers should treat this as a certifier-confirmed operating practice, not a blanket organic badge. Before adopting the technology at scale, the grower or service provider should confirm the intended use with the certifier or organic advisor, define how it will be recorded in the farm's organic system plan where required, validate crop safety and efficacy locally, and follow Zasso's training, safety, cleaning, and operating procedures.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso is a non-chemical physical weed-control technology, so it can be used in organic-certified production where the certifier accepts the specific application and the grower documents it properly."

"We present Zasso as an organic-compatible weed-control tool, not as an automatic organic certification for every crop, country, or farm."

"For certified organic operations, the best practice is to confirm the intended use with the farm's certifier or organic advisor and validate the application locally under the farm's real conditions."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is automatically certified organic everywhere. Organic-certified use depends on the applicable standard, certifier interpretation, buyer rules, crop context, machine configuration, cleaning practices, records, and the farm's organic system plan.

Do not imply that Zasso alone satisfies organic weed-management obligations. Certified organic systems still rely on broader preventive, cultural, mechanical, and physical weed-management practices.

Do not overstate field performance. Results depend on weed species, weed stage, crop geometry, operator training, contact quality, application speed, soil and plant moisture, pass number, safety conditions, and local validation.
