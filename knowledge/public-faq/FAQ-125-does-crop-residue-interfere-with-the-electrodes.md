# Does crop residue interfere with the electrodes?

## Short Answer

Yes, crop residue can interfere with the electrodes if it prevents reliable contact between the applicator and living target weeds. Light, settled residue is usually less of a problem when weeds are exposed above it, but thick straw, loose cut biomass, pruning material, mulch, or lodged residue can shield weeds, lift electrodes away from green tissue, reduce contact time, or create uneven current paths. Dry residue also needs a safety review because it can increase fire risk in unsuitable conditions.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso's Electroherb technology is contact-based. The applicator has to touch living target vegetation, or maintain the intended return path, so electrical energy can move through the plant and the plant-soil circuit. Crop residue matters when it physically separates the electrodes from the weed, hides the target plant, changes working height, or creates easier unwanted paths for current than the target vegetation.

In practical field conditions, residue can interfere in several ways. Loose straw, cut stems, dry leaves, crop trash, prunings, and heavy mulch can make the electrode ride over the weed instead of contacting it. Residue can also cover small weeds, reduce contact with lower stems, make electrode pressure uneven, or accumulate around the applicator. Where the target weeds are still visible, upright, green, and reachable through or above the residue layer, Zasso can still be a suitable option, subject to product setup and field validation.

The issue is not only whether residue is present; it is whether the target plant remains contactable. A field with moderate residue and weeds protruding above the residue layer may treat well. A field with heavy lodged biomass, recently mowed dry material, thick straw, plastic mulch, dense pruning waste, or residue covering the weed crowns may need a different timing, a residue-management step, a slower pass, a different applicator setup, or follow-up treatment.

Dry residue deserves special caution. Crop residue that is dry, loose, and in contact with the electrode path can increase operational risk, especially in hot, windy, drought, or high-fire-risk conditions. Zasso should not be positioned as a machine that can safely work through any residue layer. Field assessment, operator training, approved safety procedures, and local restrictions remain essential.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should check residue during the pre-application field assessment. The key questions are: Are the target weeds exposed? Can the electrodes touch living green tissue consistently? Is residue lifting the applicator away from the weed? Is residue hiding small weeds or regrowth points? Is dry biomass creating fire risk? Are crop plants, irrigation lines, plastic covers, metal objects, or other sensitive structures close to the electrode path?

If residue blocks contact, the treatment plan may need adjustment. Options can include waiting for weed regrowth through the residue, changing timing, adjusting travel speed, modifying pass strategy, removing or redistributing residue, mowing earlier and allowing suitable regrowth, or using Zasso as part of a broader integrated weed-management program.

For customers, the practical message is that residue is a field-condition variable, not an automatic yes-or-no answer. Zasso works best when the target vegetation is exposed, reachable, and safely contactable by the applicator.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Crop residue does not automatically prevent Zasso from working, but it can reduce performance if it blocks electrode contact with living weeds. We assess whether the target vegetation is exposed, reachable, and safe to treat before recommending an application."

"Zasso works best when electrodes can make consistent contact with green target vegetation. Heavy straw, loose cut biomass, mulch, or dry residue may require timing, setup, or residue-management adjustments."

"In dry-residue conditions, safety comes first. We evaluate fire risk and field conditions before operating."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso can treat effectively through all crop residue, mulch, straw, or cut biomass. The system needs useful contact with living target vegetation.

Do not claim that residue only affects efficacy. Dry residue, wind, heat, drought, and crop trash near the electrode path can also affect safety and may restrict operation.

Do not provide universal recommendations for residue removal, mowing height, waiting time, travel speed, pass number, or applicator settings without crop-, product-, region-, and field-specific validation. Avoid disclosing confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, pricing, certification details, internal safety analysis, control logic, or private meeting content.
