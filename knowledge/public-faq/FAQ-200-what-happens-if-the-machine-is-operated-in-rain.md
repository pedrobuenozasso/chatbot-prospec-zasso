# What happens if the machine is operated in rain?

## Short Answer

Zasso equipment should not be operated in rain unless the specific product manual, training, and site procedure explicitly allow it. Rain can create unsafe electrical paths through water films, wet soil, puddles, mud, metal objects, wet crop residue, the machine, animals, or people. If rain starts during operation, the safe customer-facing rule is to stop high-voltage operation, keep people and animals away, follow the product-specific shutdown procedure, and restart only when conditions are dry enough and the approved checks are complete.

## Detailed Answer

Rain is a safety-critical operating condition for electrical weeding because Zasso equipment uses controlled high-voltage energy. Under normal operation, the intended circuit is through the applicator, target vegetation, soil or return path, and the machine's designed electrical system. Rain can change that circuit by lowering resistance on surfaces and creating conductive bridges outside the intended treatment path.

The concern is not only that the target plants are wet. Rain can wet the operator's surroundings, soil surface, residues, tires, metal infrastructure, irrigation components, fences, drains, tools, connectors, guards, cables, insulation, and machine frame. It can also create puddles or water films that spread voltage over a wider area, reduce predictability of the current path, increase leakage or ground-fault risk, affect step-voltage exposure, and interfere with safe access or visibility.

For some field applications, moderate soil moisture before treatment may be manageable and can even support plant-soil conductivity. That is different from operating while rain is falling or when the work area has become wet, muddy, waterlogged, or covered with puddles. A field may be agronomically moist but still safe and controlled; active rain and wet surfaces are a more conservative safety question and must be governed by the product manual.

If rain begins during operation, operators should stop high-voltage output, maintain the required exclusion zone, secure the machine according to the manual, and reassess conditions before any restart. The machine should not be operated simply because the weeds are still contactable or because the rain is light. Restart should depend on the product-specific manual, trained operator judgment, site risk assessment, visibility of the danger zone, absence of standing water or unsafe conductive paths, and the condition of cables, insulation, connectors, electrodes, boots, guards, and other safety-critical parts.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, rain is a stop-and-assess condition. The safe planning approach is to schedule treatment in a suitable application window, check weather forecasts, inspect the area before work, and avoid starting if rain, puddles, active irrigation, wet metal, saturated soil, mud, or poor visibility could compromise safety.

If a site gets wet unexpectedly, the priority is people and animal safety, not finishing the pass. The operator should keep bystanders away, shut down high voltage according to training, and wait until the site and equipment meet the approved conditions for use. If there is any sign of damaged insulation, wet connectors, abnormal sparking, alarm events, mud contamination, water ingress, or contact with wet conductive infrastructure, the machine should remain out of service until the approved inspection or service process is complete.

This does not mean Zasso needs perfectly dry agronomic conditions in every case. It means operation in rain should not be improvised. Customers should distinguish between suitable soil moisture for efficacy and unsafe wet operating conditions for high-voltage equipment.

## Caveats

This FAQ is not a substitute for a product manual. Exact rain limits, shutdown steps, discharge checks, PPE, safety distance, alarm handling, inspection criteria, restart rules, storage rules, and cleaning rules may vary by product, configuration, country, and site.

Do not claim that all Zasso products have identical rain restrictions unless the current approved manual says so. Do not claim that light rain is safe, that an operator can continue if no alarm appears, or that wet operation is acceptable because the technology is non-chemical.

Do not disclose confidential enclosure ratings, insulation ratings, sensor thresholds, fault logic, leakage-current limits, grounding architecture, test results, service procedures, customer incidents, or certification files unless Zasso has approved them for external use.
