# Does rain after application affect results?

## Short Answer

Rain after a Zasso application does not wash off a chemical spray deposit, because Zasso is a non-chemical electrical treatment. Once the target plant has received the electrical dose, the immediate injury process has already started. However, rain after treatment can still affect field follow-up, regrowth risk, safety, access, and interpretation of results, especially if it is heavy enough to create saturated soil, runoff, mud, lodging, or rapid new growth.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso treatment works by delivering electrical energy through contacted plant tissues and the plant-soil system. This is different from a foliar herbicide, where rain soon after spraying can reduce performance by washing product from the leaf surface before uptake is complete. For Zasso, the relevant question is not whether rain washes off the treatment, but whether post-treatment conditions change how the injured plant recovers, how the field can be assessed, and whether follow-up operations remain safe and practical.

In normal conditions, visible injury can begin quickly after treatment and continue to develop over the following days. Rain after treatment should not be described as automatically cancelling the application. If the electrical contact and dose were adequate at the time of treatment, the plant has already been exposed.

Heavy rain soon after application can still matter. It may make scouting harder, flatten vegetation, move residue, create mud or standing water, restrict field access, or encourage surviving plants and new flushes of weeds to recover or regrow. Rain can also change soil moisture for any second pass or follow-up treatment. In very wet conditions, operators should reassess safety, field access, contact quality, speed, and product-specific operating guidance before returning to the field.

The best customer answer is therefore balanced: rain after application is usually less critical than rain before or during application for the actual electrical dose, but it can influence post-treatment assessment and follow-up decisions.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not assume that a rainfall event after treatment automatically means the application failed. Instead, they should scout the treated area at the normal follow-up timings and compare the observed injury, regrowth, and new emergence with the treatment objective.

If rain was light and the field remains accessible, the main recommendation is usually to continue normal monitoring. If rain was heavy, created saturated soil or runoff, or was followed by rapid regrowth conditions, customers should inspect the field carefully before deciding whether a second pass is needed.

For operations teams, the key point is to separate three issues: the dose delivered during the original treatment, the biological response over the next days and weeks, and the suitability of the field for any follow-up pass. Rain after application affects mostly the second and third issues.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Rain after a Zasso application does not wash off the treatment like it can with some sprays, because the treatment is electrical rather than chemical. We still monitor the field after rain because heavy rainfall can affect regrowth, access, safety, and whether a follow-up pass is needed."

"The key question is whether the plant received the right electrical dose during application. After rain, we recommend normal scouting and a practical field check before making any follow-up decision."

## Caveats

Do not claim that rain after application never affects results. Heavy rain, saturated soil, runoff, lodging, mud, warm growing conditions, or new weed emergence can affect visible outcomes and follow-up decisions.

Do not promise a fixed rainfast interval, one-pass control, or guaranteed control after rainfall. Zasso is not a chemical herbicide, so herbicide-style rainfastness language should be avoided unless a specific approved product document uses it.

Do not recommend follow-up operation in active rain, standing water, saturated soil, mud, or unsafe field-access conditions without checking the applicable Zasso product manual, local training, site procedures, and safety rules.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, internal safety thresholds, private customer results, pricing, certification details, or private meeting content.
