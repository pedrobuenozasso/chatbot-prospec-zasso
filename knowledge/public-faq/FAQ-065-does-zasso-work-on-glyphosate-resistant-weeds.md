# Does Zasso work on glyphosate-resistant weeds?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work on glyphosate-resistant weeds when the target plants can be contacted properly and the application is matched to the weed species, growth stage, density, moisture, and operating conditions. Glyphosate resistance affects a plant's response to a Group 9 herbicide; Zasso's Electroherb technology uses a physical electrical mode of action rather than glyphosate's biochemical pathway. The strongest customer-facing claim is that Zasso can be a useful non-chemical tool for glyphosate-resistant weed programs, not that it guarantees complete control in every situation.

## Detailed Answer

Glyphosate-resistant weeds are weed populations that survive glyphosate applications that would normally control susceptible plants. Resistance is usually selected by repeated reliance on glyphosate and may involve target-site or non-target-site mechanisms that prevent the herbicide from working as intended. Once resistance is present, growers often need a more diverse program that combines remaining effective herbicides, crop competition, scouting, sanitation, mechanical or physical tools, and seedbank management.

Zasso works differently from glyphosate. Instead of inhibiting the EPSPS enzyme, Electroherb applies controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation. The current damages plant tissues physically, including conductive tissues involved in water and nutrient movement. Because this treatment does not depend on glyphosate's chemical site of action, glyphosate resistance does not automatically protect the plant from electrical treatment.

That said, the result is still agronomic and operational, not automatic. Species and plant architecture matter. Young or well-contacted plants are generally easier to treat than mature, dense, shielded, lodged, woody, or deep-rooted plants. Soil and plant moisture, electrode contact, travel speed, treatment timing, follow-up passes, and operator training also affect performance. For some glyphosate-resistant weeds, especially grasses or large mature plants, Zasso should be positioned as part of an integrated program rather than a one-pass standalone cure.

A particularly relevant public example is glyphosate-resistant annual ryegrass. Research from Western Australia using Zasso XPower equipment reported that electric weed control on a glyphosate-resistant annual ryegrass population achieved control comparable to a paraquat/diquat double-knock treatment under the tested conditions, and related public summaries reported substantial biomass reduction. This supports a careful, specific claim: Zasso can remain useful where glyphosate performance is compromised, provided the application window and operating conditions are suitable.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers dealing with glyphosate resistance, Zasso can add a different control layer that does not rely on the same chemistry that has failed. It may be especially useful for resistant escapes, field edges, fence lines, perennial-crop strips, non-crop areas, organic or residue-sensitive programs, and situations where reducing seed return is valuable.

The best customer trial should define the resistant species, growth stage, density, site constraints, crop safety requirements, soil and plant moisture, application speed, number of passes, and the customer's real comparison: another herbicide pass, mowing, cultivation, hand removal, harvest weed seed control, or doing nothing. Success should be measured not only by visible burn-down, but also by regrowth, biomass reduction, seed production, operating cost, safety compliance, and fit within the wider weed-management program.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso can help manage glyphosate-resistant weeds because it uses a physical electrical mode of action rather than relying on glyphosate's chemical pathway."

"For glyphosate-resistant weeds, Zasso is best positioned as a non-chemical layer in an integrated weed-management program, especially for emerged weeds, escapes, field edges, and situations where reducing seed return matters."

"Results still depend on species, growth stage, contact quality, speed, moisture, density, and follow-up management, so claims should be validated in the customer's field conditions."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is resistance-proof, that it eliminates glyphosate resistance, or that it controls every glyphosate-resistant weed in one pass. Glyphosate resistance does not protect a plant from electrical treatment, but plant biology, size, architecture, moisture, shielding, regrowth potential, and application quality still determine results.

Be especially careful with mature grasses, dense stands, deep-rooted perennials, lodged weeds, woody plants, and weeds shielded by crop canopy or residue. In many situations the most credible recommendation will include repeat passes, mowing before treatment, seedbank management, scouting, or integration with chemical, mechanical, cultural, or sanitation tactics where allowed.

Do not disclose confidential customer results, raw trial data, machine settings, control logic, telemetry details, pricing, contract terms, certification plans, or private meeting content unless explicitly approved for external use.
