# Why do some products require 2-3 meters of distance?

## Short Answer

Some products require a 2-3 meter safety distance because their approved operating procedure uses that distance as a practical no-entry zone around high-voltage application equipment. The distance helps keep people, animals, tools, vehicles, and conductive objects away from electrodes, treated vegetation, moving equipment, and possible abnormal electrical paths. The exact distance is product-specific, so customers should always follow the approved Zasso manual, training, labels, and local rules for their machine.

## Detailed Answer

A 2-3 meter distance should be understood as an operating control, not as a universal physics number for all Zasso systems. Electrical weeding uses high-voltage energy delivered through applicators or electrodes into target vegetation and the intended plant-soil pathway. During normal operation, the goal is to keep people and animals outside the area where they could make direct contact with electrodes, touch conductive material connected to the treatment path, approach wet or metallic objects, or be affected by an abnormal fault condition.

Some product configurations, applicator geometries, field situations, or local safety approvals may require a larger practical boundary than a narrow technical minimum. That wider boundary can account for human movement, bystanders, uneven terrain, soil and plant moisture, wet vegetation, fences, trellises, irrigation, tools, vehicles, animals, public access, machine motion, stop time, operator visibility, and the need for a simple rule that can be enforced in the field.

In other words, the distance is partly about electrical exposure and partly about site control. A clear 2-3 meter no-entry zone is easier for operators, spotters, visitors, farm staff, and contractors to understand than a complex engineering boundary that changes with every site condition. If anyone enters the required safety zone, the conservative action is to stop high-voltage output and follow the approved restart procedure before continuing.

Customers should not reduce, reinterpret, or transfer a distance from one product to another. A distance used for one Zasso product, applicator, country, or demonstration setup may not apply to another product or use case. The approved product manual and operator training always take priority.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should treat the 2-3 meter distance, where required, as a strict exclusion zone while high voltage may be active. No one should enter that zone to inspect weeds, talk to the operator, adjust equipment, touch plants, move tools, approach animals, or cross behind the applicator during operation.

Before work starts, the operator or site lead should make sure everyone understands the required boundary. In closed fields this may be handled through a briefing and supervision. In demonstrations, public-facing work, orchards, vineyards, roadsides, contractor sites, or areas with livestock or pedestrians, it may require signage, cones, barriers, spotters, or traffic and pedestrian control.

The customer benefit is safer, more repeatable operation. A simple, product-specific distance gives teams a clear rule: keep the zone clear, stop output if it is breached, and restart only when the site is controlled again.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Some Zasso products or applications require a 2-3 meter exclusion zone because the equipment uses high-voltage energy and the operating area must stay controlled. The exact distance comes from the approved manual and training for that product."

"The distance is not only about touching the electrode. It also helps prevent indirect contact, wet or metallic contact paths, bystander movement, animal entry, and abnormal fault situations."

"If the required zone is entered, the operator should stop high-voltage output and follow the approved restart procedure before continuing."

## Caveats

Do not present 2-3 meters as a universal Zasso requirement. Do not reduce a product-specific distance based on general FAQ wording, and do not transfer a distance from one product, applicator, country, certification, or field setup to another.

Do not state product-specific discharge times, voltage or current values, fault thresholds, PPE rules, sensor details, emergency procedures, certification claims, or internal test results unless they are approved for external use in the relevant product documentation.

A 2-3 meter distance does not eliminate all risk by itself. Safe operation also depends on machine design, approved configuration, trained operators, pre-use inspection, warnings, shutdown procedures, maintenance discipline, PPE where required, site control, soil and weather conditions, and local regulation.
