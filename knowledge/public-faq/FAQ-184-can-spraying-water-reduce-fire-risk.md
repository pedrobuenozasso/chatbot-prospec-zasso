# Can spraying water reduce fire risk?

## Short Answer

Spraying water can reduce fire risk only in specific, approved situations, such as pre-wetting dry biomass or having water available for fire response where local procedures allow it. It should not be treated as a simple or universal mitigation for electrical weeding. Water can also create electrical hazards if it contacts energized electrodes, the applicator, conductive objects, irrigation systems, puddles, or people, so Zasso equipment should not be operated under active water spray unless the product manual and site procedure explicitly allow it.

## Detailed Answer

Fire risk in electrical weeding is mainly the combination of an ignition source and fuel. Sparks, arcing, hot spots, unstable electrode contact, damaged insulation, or conductive debris can act as ignition sources. Dry weeds, straw, mulch, dust, dead leaves, mowed biomass, or crop residue can act as fuel. In that sense, moisture can matter: wet or less-dry biomass is generally less likely to ignite than completely dry fine fuel.

However, spraying water is not automatically the right answer. Electrical weeding uses high voltage, and water can change current paths, reduce impedance, create conductive bridges, extend energized areas, and increase bystander or operator exposure risk. Zasso internal safety scenario material treats active irrigation, water spray, puddles, and wet conductive systems as conditions that require conservative controls, including avoiding operation under active irrigation and shutting down high voltage if water contacts the applicator.

For customers, the safest practical distinction is between approved pre-work fire-risk management and unapproved spraying during operation. If an approved Zasso procedure, local fire plan, or customer site rule allows pre-wetting vegetation before treatment, it should be done only with the high-voltage system off and after confirming that there are no unsafe water paths, puddles, conductive objects, damaged insulation, active irrigation, or people inside the exclusion zone. If water contacts the machine or treatment zone unexpectedly during operation, the correct response is to stop and follow the approved shutdown, inspection, drying or reassessment, and restart procedure.

Water may also be part of general fire-response readiness, depending on the site and local rules. But water should not be used casually on energized electrical equipment. Fire-response tools must be suitable for the actual hazard: dry vegetation, energized electrical components, fuel, wind, access, and local firefighting requirements may call for different equipment or procedures.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should not assume that spraying water makes electrical weeding safe in dry or fire-restricted conditions. It may reduce dry-fuel risk in some controlled cases, but it can also introduce electrical and operational hazards if done at the wrong time or in the wrong place.

Before using water as part of a fire-control plan, the operator should confirm the product manual, Zasso-approved procedure, local fire rules, site risk assessment, and emergency plan. The team should also check wind, fuel load, biomass dryness, residue continuity, soil and surface conditions, nearby metal or irrigation infrastructure, machine condition, electrode condition, and exclusion-zone control.

A good customer message is that Zasso treats water as a controlled safety variable, not as a shortcut. If water is used, it must be used deliberately, with high voltage off when required, and only under approved conditions.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Water can be part of a fire-risk plan only when it is approved for the specific site, machine, and procedure; it is not a universal fix for dry biomass or sparking."

"Zasso equipment should not be operated under active water spray, irrigation, or unsafe wet conductive conditions unless the product manual and site procedure explicitly allow it."

"If water contacts the applicator or treatment zone unexpectedly, the safe response is to stop high voltage and follow the approved inspection and restart procedure."

## Caveats

Do not claim that spraying water eliminates fire risk. Do not tell customers to spray water onto active electrodes, energized equipment, conductive objects, puddles, irrigation systems, or people. Do not claim that pre-wetting, dew, sprinklers, irrigation, water tanks, hoses, or fire extinguishers make dry biomass, wind, drought, straw, mulch, or damaged equipment automatically safe.

Do not provide customer-specific thresholds for how much water to apply, how long to wait before treatment, acceptable wetness, puddle limits, humidity limits, fire-danger ratings, wind speed, restart timing, or firefighting equipment unless those details come from approved product manuals, Zasso safety guidance, local regulation, customer site rules, or the responsible fire authority.

If there is active irrigation, unexpected water spray, puddling, water contacting the applicator, wet conductive infrastructure, a bystander handling a wet hose or irrigation system, repeated sparking, sustained arcing, smoke, flame, damaged insulation, damaged cables, damaged electrodes, or uncertainty about safe operation, high voltage should be stopped and the approved shutdown, inspection, fire-response, and return-to-service procedure should be followed.
