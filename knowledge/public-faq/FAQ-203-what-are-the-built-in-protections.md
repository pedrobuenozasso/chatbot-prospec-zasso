# What are the built-in protections?

## Short Answer

Zasso equipment is designed with layered built-in protections for professional high-voltage operation, but the exact protections depend on the product, configuration, market, and approved manual. Based on current Zasso materials, relevant protections may include emergency stop and high-voltage inhibit functions, warnings, insulation, grounding or protective earthing concepts, sensor-based readiness checks, fault detection, alarms, controlled shutdown behavior, operator training, and product-specific maintenance and inspection procedures. These protections reduce risk, but they do not make the machine risk-free or replace trained operation, exclusion zones, PPE where required, safe shutdown, or local safety rules.

## Detailed Answer

The safest way to describe Zasso's built-in protections is as a layered safety architecture. Electrical weeding intentionally uses high-voltage energy to treat target vegetation, so the machine has to manage both normal operation and abnormal situations such as unintended contact, damaged insulation, poor grounding condition, incorrect applicator position, loss of safe site control, or detected faults.

At a customer-facing level, Zasso's built-in protections can include several categories. First are operator and site protections, such as visual and acoustic warnings, safety markings, barriers or safety packages, defined hazardous areas, required training, and operating instructions. Second are electrical protections, such as insulation, protective grounding or earthing concepts, high-voltage enable and inhibit logic, emergency stop functions, and procedures to verify that the equipment is voltage-free before access or maintenance. Third are machine and sensor protections, which may include height, speed, motion, ground-contact, linkage-position, tilt, presence, insulation, open-circuit, over-current, temperature, communication, or other fault-related detections where fitted for the specific product. Fourth are human-interface protections, such as alarms, alarm history, restart rules, key or main switch behavior, job-start logic, and troubleshooting guidance.

These protections should not be presented as identical across all machines. A vineyard applicator, row-crop configuration, urban unit, prototype, demonstrator, or future product may have different sensors, operating modes, safety distances, alarms, inspection rules, and conformity documentation. The approved manual and training for the delivered machine are therefore the authoritative source for the exact list of protections and what the operator must do when they activate.

The important customer message is that built-in protections are there to support safe professional operation, not to remove the operator's responsibility. If a protection stops output, inhibits high voltage, raises an alarm, or prevents restart, the operator should treat it as safety-relevant, keep people and animals away, understand the cause, and restart only when the approved procedure allows it.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should expect Zasso equipment to behave like professional high-voltage agricultural or vegetation-management machinery. Built-in protections help guide the operator, prevent operation when defined readiness conditions are not met, reduce exposure to abnormal electrical states, and make safety-relevant conditions visible through warnings, alarms, shutdowns, or maintenance requirements.

For owners, contractors, municipalities, distributors, and farms, this means safety is a complete operating system. The customer should combine machine protections with trained operators, controlled work areas, inspections, clean and undamaged sensors or electrodes, correct PPE where required, maintenance records, product-specific shutdown and discharge procedures, and a clear rule that alarms or abnormal behavior stop the job until the cause is understood.

For sales discussions, the practical framing is: Zasso builds safety into the machine, documentation, training, and maintenance process, but customers should always ask for the protections and procedures for the specific product they will operate.

## Caveats

Do not say that built-in protections make the equipment safe to touch, safe for untrained users, safe in all weather, safe around all water or metal objects, or risk-free.
