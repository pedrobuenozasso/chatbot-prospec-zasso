# What happens if the equipment hits a metal object?

## Short Answer

If Zasso equipment hits a metal object, the operator should treat it as an abnormal condition and stop according to the product-specific procedure. The issue is both mechanical and electrical: impact can damage electrodes, applicators, guards, cables, insulation, or nearby infrastructure, and metal can also create an unintended conductive path if high voltage is active. No one should approach, touch, move, or clear the object until the machine has been shut down, made safe, and checked by trained personnel according to the approved manual and training.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso equipment is designed to deliver controlled high-voltage energy through applicators or electrodes into target plants and the intended plant-soil pathway. A collision with a metal object changes the situation. Metal objects such as posts, stakes, tools, scrap, wire, pipes, machinery parts, rails, trellis components, irrigation hardware, or buried metal can physically damage the applicator area and may also create an unintended electrical path.

The result depends on the product, speed, object type, contact geometry, moisture, soil conductivity, machine state, insulation condition, available energy, and how the protection systems respond. A light impact may only require stopping and inspecting for damage. A more serious impact could bend or break electrodes, expose or damage insulation, stress cables or connectors, trigger alarms, create arcing or heating, damage the metal object or customer infrastructure, or energize nearby conductive material.

The safe customer-facing rule is conservative: do not continue operating after a suspected impact with metal. Stop high-voltage output using the approved controls, keep people and animals outside the safety zone, and do not touch the metal object, electrodes, applicator, cables, tractor, wet vegetation, fence, irrigation component, or other conductive parts until the system is confirmed safe. After shutdown, the machine and site should be inspected according to the relevant Zasso manual, training, and service process before restart.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should plan the work area before treatment and remove or mark visible metal obstacles where practical. This is especially important in orchards, vineyards, roadsides, rail corridors, industrial sites, municipal areas, and older fields where metal posts, wire, scrap, tools, trellis hardware, irrigation parts, rails, pipes, and buried objects may be present.

Operators should be trained to distinguish normal treatment from abnormal contact. If the machine strikes a metal object, the right response is not to push through, lift the implement casually, or clear the obstruction by hand. The right response is to stop, secure the area, follow the approved shutdown and discharge procedure, inspect for mechanical and electrical damage, and restart only when the product-specific procedure allows it.

For customers, this is part of ordinary professional risk management. Zasso technology can be used safely only when machine design, operator training, exclusion zones, pre-work inspection, maintenance, alarms, shutdown discipline, and product-specific manuals are treated as one system.

## Caveats

Do not say that hitting metal is harmless, that the equipment can always keep operating, or that protective systems make every impact automatically safe. The risk depends on the product, applicator, impact severity, metal object, moisture, soil conductivity, vegetation contact, insulation condition, machine configuration, and protective response.

Do not provide improvised rescue, electrical repair, restart, or troubleshooting instructions beyond the approved Zasso manual and local emergency procedures. If there is arcing, smoke, fire, shock, equipment damage, repeated alarms, a broken electrode, damaged cable, exposed insulation, or suspected energized metal, stop operation, keep others away, and follow the product-specific emergency and service process.

Do not disclose confidential voltage, current, power, pulse, frequency, grounding, bonding, insulation-monitoring, alarm, shutdown-threshold, sensor, firmware, certification, incident, customer, supplier, warranty, or test-result details unless they are already approved for public use.
