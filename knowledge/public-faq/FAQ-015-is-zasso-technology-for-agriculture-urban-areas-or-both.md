# Is Zasso technology for agriculture, urban areas, or both?

## Short Answer

Zasso technology is for both agriculture and urban or non-agricultural vegetation management. The same core electric-weeding approach can be adapted through different machines, applicators, carrier vehicles, power levels, safety systems, and service models. In practice, the right fit depends on the specific crop or site, local product availability, certification, operating conditions, safety requirements, and support coverage.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso should not be described as only an agricultural technology or only an urban weed-control technology. Zasso's public and internal materials consistently frame the company around two large application areas: agriculture and urban/landscaping or infrastructure vegetation management.

In agriculture, Zasso technology is used or positioned for applications such as vineyards, orchards, citrus, coffee, berries, nuts, olives, small crops, and other crop systems where non-chemical weed control can help reduce reliance on herbicides or soil-disturbing methods. Examples in public Zasso materials include XPower XPS for vineyards and orchards in Europe, E.COFFEE and E.CITRUS for Latin American crop applications, and smaller platforms such as RAIDEN for paths, sidewalks, curbs, and small agricultural plantations in Latin America.

In urban and non-crop settings, Zasso technology is positioned for municipalities, landscaping companies, industrial areas, sidewalks, pavements, roadsides, cemeteries, courtyards, parks, infrastructure, and service providers. Public Zasso materials identify XPower XPU as an urban and industrial solution, with related tools such as XPA hydraulic-arm applications, XPH handheld applications, CityZAS, and the eWeeding Roller or ZAP Weeder depending on market.

The important customer-facing point is that Zasso's core technology is adaptable, but each application needs the right configuration. A vineyard, a citrus grove, a cemetery, a sidewalk, an industrial yard, and a roadside are not the same operating environment. Weed species, weed density, soil moisture, surface type, nearby people, carrier vehicle, working speed, fire risk, safety distance, operator training, certification, and local service route all affect whether a specific Zasso solution is appropriate.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should start with the job to be done, not with a generic yes-or-no question about agriculture or cities. A grower may need under-vine, under-tree, inter-row, or crop-specific vegetation management. A municipality or contractor may need hard-surface, roadside, cemetery, park, courtyard, or industrial-site weed control.

Once the application is clear, Zasso can confirm whether a suitable product, service model, local channel, training program, and certified configuration are available. In suitable applications, Zasso can provide a non-chemical tool for customers who want to reduce herbicide use, avoid chemical residues, reduce soil movement, or manage weeds where spraying is restricted or undesirable. Availability and performance should always be confirmed for the specific country, crop or site, equipment configuration, and operating conditions.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso technology is designed for both agricultural and urban vegetation-management applications. The same electric-weeding core is adapted into different products and service models for crops, municipalities, landscaping, infrastructure, and service-provider use."

"The right solution depends on the crop or site, region, certification status, carrier vehicle, operating conditions, safety requirements, and available local support, so Zasso confirms the recommended configuration case by case."

## Caveats

Do not claim that every Zasso product is available in every country or suitable for every crop, city, or surface. Public Zasso pages show regional limits for some products, and some applications are better treated as validation or development opportunities unless current approval is confirmed.

Avoid saying that Zasso is automatically safer, cheaper, or more effective than all alternatives. Electrical weeding uses high voltage and requires trained operators, safety procedures, proper product configuration, suitable field or site conditions, and local compliance review.
