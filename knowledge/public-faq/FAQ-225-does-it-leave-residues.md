# Does it leave residues?

## Short Answer

Zasso's electrical weeding does not leave chemical herbicide residues because it does not apply a molecular active ingredient to the crop, soil, or water. The treatment uses controlled electrical energy as a physical mode of action, so there is no persistent "electrical residue" after the application. Customers should still understand that treated plants may remain as dead or drying biomass, and normal machine operation can still involve ordinary field considerations such as soil contact, dust, plant debris, and equipment maintenance.

## Detailed Answer

When customers ask about residues, they usually mean chemical residues: herbicide active ingredients, metabolites, spray deposits, drift deposits, or persistent contamination in soil, water, harvested products, or the surrounding environment. Zasso's technology is different from chemical herbicides because the weed-control action is physical. Electrical energy is delivered through contacted plant tissue and the plant-soil circuit during operation; once the machine has passed, the energy is no longer present as a chemical substance.

For that reason, Zasso can be described as a non-chemical and residue-free weed-control method in the specific sense that it does not add herbicide active ingredients or toxic chemical residues to the treated area. This is one of the main environmental and customer-facing advantages of electrical weeding, especially where customers are concerned about chemical load, spray drift, re-entry intervals, residues in food or the environment, or restrictions on herbicide use.

The claim should still be worded precisely. "Residue-free" does not mean nothing visible remains in the field. The treated weeds themselves can remain as wilted, dried, or decomposing plant material, just as with many physical weed-control methods. It also does not automatically answer every soil, water, biodiversity, crop-contact, or long-term monitoring question. Those topics depend on application conditions, local validation, and the specific endpoint being discussed.

The safest position is that Zasso leaves no chemical herbicide residue from the treatment itself, while still requiring trained operation, correct equipment setup, appropriate field conditions, and normal environmental stewardship.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical benefit is reduced dependence on chemical weed-control inputs in suitable applications. That can be valuable for organic or residue-sensitive production, areas near sensitive sites, public or urban environments, integrated weed-management programs, and markets where customers want to reduce chemical load or avoid spray drift.

It may also simplify some operational questions that are specific to herbicide residues, such as chemical withholding periods, rainfastness, spray drift, and residue-management concerns. However, customers should not treat this as a universal compliance statement. Crop certification rules, local regulations, customer specifications, food-safety programs, and site-specific environmental requirements must still be checked for the intended application.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Zasso does not apply a chemical herbicide, so the treatment itself does not leave herbicide active-ingredient residues in the soil, crop, or surrounding environment. It is a physical, electrical mode of action."

"The right distinction is that Zasso is residue-free from a chemical herbicide standpoint, while treated plant material may still remain in the field and local agronomic or certification rules should always be checked."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso creates zero environmental impact in every situation or that residue-free automatically means no need for local validation. Residue-free refers to chemical herbicide residues from the treatment, not to every possible operational or environmental endpoint.

Do not confuse this FAQ with water contamination, groundwater contamination, food-residue certification, crop injury, soil biology, biodiversity, or long-term monitoring. Those questions should be answered separately with their own evidence and caveats.

Avoid promising regulatory acceptance, organic certification, food-chain approval, or customer-specific residue compliance unless the relevant local rules and certifications have been confirmed.
