# Does Zasso work in row crops?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can work in row-crop systems when the application is designed around crop geometry, weed contact, and crop protection. The strongest current fit is for inter-row or other directed applications where the electrodes contact emerged weeds while avoiding the crop. Zasso should not be presented as a universal over-the-top row-crop solution; performance and crop safety depend on the crop, row spacing, weed stage, guidance accuracy, equipment configuration, speed, moisture, residue, and local validation.

## Detailed Answer

Row crops are a natural application area for electrical weeding because many systems have defined crop rows and visible spaces where weeds can be targeted. Zasso's Electroherb and XPower technology controls contacted vegetation through electrical energy. That physical mode of action can be useful in row crops because it does not rely on herbicide chemistry and can reduce soil disturbance compared with repeated cultivation in suitable situations.

The practical answer is conditional. Electrical weeding is not chemically selective: a contacted crop plant can be injured just as a contacted weed can be controlled. For row crops, the application must therefore be designed so the treatment zone reaches the weeds and avoids the crop. This is most credible in inter-row applications, pre-emergence or pre-plant situations, stale seedbeds, selected escaped-weed scenarios, or crop-specific directed uses where the field protocol has been tested.

Zasso and AGXTEND publicly presented the XPower XPR concept as an electric weeding solution for row crops and specifically described it for non-selective weed control between crop rows. Internal Zasso press material also describes XPR as the company's first entry into broad agriculture and links it to inter-row sugar beet results. Existing Zasso FAQ context supports the same conclusion across sugar beet, cotton, soybeans, potatoes, and between-row applications: Zasso can be relevant in row crops, but only when the target weeds are reachable and the crop is protected from electrode contact.

For customers, the most useful framing is not simply "row crops: yes or no." The right question is: which crop, which weeds, which timing, and which treatment zone? A row-crop opportunity should be evaluated against the specific crop stage, row spacing, weed spectrum, weed height, in-row versus inter-row pressure, operating speed, soil and plant moisture, residue, terrain, safety rules, and whether follow-up tactics are needed for weeds not contacted by the electrodes.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers growing row crops can consider Zasso as a non-chemical physical tool in integrated weed management, especially where herbicide resistance, organic production, residue concerns, labor pressure, or soil-conservation goals make additional tactics valuable. The strongest customer use case is usually control of emerged, contactable weeds between rows or in other directed zones.

Zasso may help reduce reliance on full-field herbicide programs or repeated soil-moving operations in suitable systems, but it should be planned as part of a program. Weeds hidden inside the crop row, shielded by canopy or residue, too small for reliable electrode contact, or emerging after treatment may need another pass or another control method.

A commercial recommendation should normally include a local demonstration or validation protocol before scale-up. Row-crop performance depends heavily on equipment setup and field conditions, so customers should measure weed control, crop safety, speed, energy use, regrowth, residue/fire risk, operator workflow, and economics under their own conditions.

## Safe Sales Wording

"Yes, Zasso can be relevant in row crops when the application is designed to contact weeds while protecting the crop, especially in inter-row or other directed treatment zones."

"For row crops, we position Zasso as a non-chemical physical mode of action that can complement herbicides, cultivation, organic practices, or other local weed-management tools where validated."

"The key is crop-specific validation: row spacing, crop stage, weed height, guidance, speed, moisture, and residue conditions all influence performance and crop safety."

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso is selective to row crops. Direct contact with crop plants can cause injury unless a specific product, crop stage, applicator, and protocol have been validated for that use.

Do not claim universal row-crop efficacy, complete in-row control, one-pass control, season-long weed control, guaranteed yield protection, full herbicide replacement, or current commercial availability of every row-crop concept in every market. Treat XPR concept and sugar beet inter-row results as strong evidence of technical direction and application fit, not as a blanket promise for all crops.

Results may vary with crop species, row spacing, crop stage, weed species, weed size, weed density, soil moisture, plant moisture, soil conductivity, residue, terrain, operating speed, applicator geometry, guidance accuracy, operator training, safety rules, and local regulatory requirements.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, unpublished trial data, customer names, private meeting details, pricing, private SharePoint filenames, internal roadmap commitments, certification timelines, or non-public commercial plans.
