# Can it reduce herbicide resistance pressure?

## Short Answer

Yes, Zasso can help reduce herbicide resistance pressure when it replaces or reduces repeated herbicide applications in a suitable weed-control program. Herbicide resistance is driven by selection: weeds that survive repeated exposure to the same herbicide mode of action can reproduce and become more common. Because Zasso uses a physical electrical mode of action rather than a chemical herbicide pathway, it can add a different control layer and reduce reliance on repeated chemical selection pressure. The claim should stay conditional: Zasso helps only where it is actually used to reduce herbicide dependence and prevent weed escapes from producing seed.

## Detailed Answer

Herbicide resistance pressure comes from repeated selection. When a weed population is repeatedly exposed to the same herbicide or herbicide group, susceptible plants are controlled while naturally resistant individuals may survive, reproduce, and increase in the population. Over time, that can make a chemical program less reliable and force customers into more complex, expensive, or restricted control options.

Zasso can reduce that pressure in suitable applications because the treatment does not rely on a herbicide active ingredient or a biochemical herbicide site of action. Electroherb applies controlled electrical energy through contacted vegetation and the plant-soil circuit. This gives customers a physical, non-chemical control tactic that can be used alongside herbicides, mechanical tools, cultural practices, scouting, and seedbank management.

The most important point is substitution or diversification. If Zasso replaces a repeated herbicide pass, reduces the number of chemical applications needed, controls resistant escapes before they set seed, or helps a customer avoid overusing one chemical mode of action, it can contribute to lower resistance selection pressure. This is most credible when Zasso is used as part of integrated weed management rather than as a single universal replacement.

Zasso does not eliminate existing herbicide resistance from a field, remove resistant seed from the soil seedbank, or guarantee that new resistance problems will not develop. It also does not remove the need for good agronomy. Results depend on target species, growth stage, plant size, contact quality, soil and plant moisture, weed density, operating speed, number of passes, crop or site constraints, safety procedures, and follow-up management.

## What This Means for Customers

For customers, the practical value is resilience. Zasso can give the weed-control program another mode of action when chemical-only programs are under pressure from resistant weeds, regulatory limits, drift concerns, residue concerns, public acceptance, or repeated chemical use.

A good customer plan should identify which herbicide pass or resistant-weed problem Zasso is expected to address. The program should also define the target weeds, timing, speed, treatment quality, follow-up scouting, seed-production risk, safety requirements, and whether Zasso is being used to replace a herbicide pass, reduce chemical frequency, manage escapes, or protect remaining effective herbicides.

The strongest customer-facing message is not that Zasso makes resistance disappear. It is that Zasso can reduce selection pressure by adding a non-chemical physical control tactic and reducing repeated dependence on the same chemical modes of action where the application is validated.

## Caveats

Do not claim that Zasso eliminates existing herbicide resistance, reverses resistance in a seedbank, prevents all future resistance, or is resistance-proof. The correct claim is that Zasso can help reduce selection pressure where it reduces repeated chemical dependence and is integrated into a sound weed-management program.

Do not imply that Zasso automatically replaces all herbicides. Many customers will still use herbicides, and the best program may combine electrical, chemical, mechanical, cultural, scouting, and seedbank-management tactics.

Do not make crop-specific or species-specific resistance claims without local evidence. Resistant grasses, large mature weeds, dense stands, shielded plants, deep-rooted perennials, or weeds with strong regrowth potential may require careful timing, slower operation, repeat passes, mowing before treatment, or other complementary practices.
