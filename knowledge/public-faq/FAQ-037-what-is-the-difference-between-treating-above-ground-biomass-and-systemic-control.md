# What is the difference between treating above-ground biomass and systemic control?

## Short Answer

Treating above-ground biomass means damaging the visible leaves, stems, or canopy that the applicator touches. Systemic control means the treatment affects the plant's internal recovery pathways, especially vascular tissues, crowns, growing points, and roots where the current path and dose are sufficient. With Zasso electrical weeding, visible top-growth injury is important, but durable control depends on whether enough useful energy reaches the tissues that allow the weed to regrow. Results remain species- and condition-dependent, so Zasso should avoid promising complete root kill in every application.

## Detailed Answer

Above-ground biomass is the visible part of the weed: leaves, stems, tillers, flowers, and other exposed growth. A treatment that mainly affects this biomass may create fast visual symptoms such as wilting, darkening, scorching, collapse, or dry-down. Those symptoms are useful, but they do not always prove that the plant has lost its ability to recover.

Systemic control is a stronger concept. In electrical weeding, it means the plant has been affected beyond surface injury: current has moved through conductive plant tissues and damaged the structures that support survival and regrowth. These can include xylem and phloem, the crown, hypocotyl, growing points, root-zone tissues, rhizomes, stolons, or other below-ground recovery structures, depending on the plant species. When those tissues are sufficiently injured, the plant is less able to move water and nutrients, restart growth, or produce new shoots.

Zasso's technology starts with contact on above-ground plant tissue, but the goal is not simply to burn leaves. The plant becomes part of an electrical circuit, and the useful effect comes from delivering energy through the plant-soil pathway so internal tissues are disrupted. This is why customer discussions should distinguish early visual effect from lasting agronomic control. A field can look brown shortly after treatment while some plants still recover from protected crowns, nodes, rhizomes, or roots.

The distinction matters most for perennial weeds, grasses, dense canopies, large root systems, or weeds with protected growing points. These weeds may require better electrode contact, slower speed, more suitable timing, mowing in some canopy situations, or sequential passes. Conversely, some upright broadleaf annuals may be controlled more readily because current can reach critical tissues more effectively.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should evaluate Zasso treatments in two stages. First, they can look for visible injury to the above-ground biomass: wilting, collapse, discoloration, and dry-down. Second, they should check whether regrowth is suppressed over the following days and weeks, especially in perennial or grass-heavy infestations.

This helps set realistic expectations. A fast visual response is encouraging, but the commercial value comes from reducing viable regrowth and lowering the need for repeated interventions. Good results depend on matching the machine, applicator, speed, contact quality, weed stage, soil condition, moisture, and pass strategy to the target weed population.

For sales conversations, the safest message is that Zasso is designed for root-oriented, systemic physical control where conditions and operating protocol allow it. It should not be sold as a universal guarantee that every root or rhizome is destroyed in one pass.

"Zasso is designed to go beyond treating the top growth: it uses controlled electrical energy to affect the plant internally, which can reduce regrowth when the weed, soil, contact, and application protocol are suitable."

"We avoid promising complete root kill in every case, because systemic control depends on species, growth stage, soil and moisture conditions, electrode contact, speed, and pass strategy."

## Caveats

Do not claim that all roots, rhizomes, crowns, or meristems are always destroyed. Systemic control is the target and potential benefit, but actual performance depends on the plant and field conditions.

Do not judge success only by the first visual browning of leaves. Some weeds may regrow from lower nodes, crowns, rhizomes, or roots if the treatment mainly damages above-ground biomass.

Do not disclose confidential machine settings, internal control logic, unpublished trial data, customer-specific results, pricing, certification details, or proprietary electrode designs.
