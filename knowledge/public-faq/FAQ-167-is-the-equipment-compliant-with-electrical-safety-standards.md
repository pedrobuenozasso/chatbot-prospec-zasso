# Is the equipment compliant with electrical safety standards?

## Short Answer

Yes, for documented Zasso products and configurations, Zasso equipment is designed and documented against applicable machinery and electrical safety requirements. The safest customer-facing answer is that compliance depends on the exact product, configuration, country, machine combination, and approved documentation supplied with that unit. Customers should always rely on the product-specific declaration of conformity, operating manual, training, and local regulatory requirements rather than a generic statement that every product is compliant everywhere.

## Detailed Answer

Electrical weeding equipment is both machinery and high-voltage electrical equipment, so compliance is not a single universal label. It normally involves a product-specific risk assessment, conformity documentation, operating instructions, safety concept, warnings, guarded or controlled access to hazard zones, electrical protection, electromagnetic compatibility review, emergency and shutdown functions, maintenance procedures, and clear rules for trained operators.

Zasso documentation reviewed for this FAQ includes EC declarations of conformity for specific XPower configurations. Those declarations state conformity with relevant EU machinery, low-voltage, and electromagnetic-compatibility requirements, and reference standards used for machinery safety, agricultural machinery safety, safety-related controls, electrical equipment of machines, high-voltage machine equipment, safety distances, hydraulic systems, PTO guarding, EMC, safety signs, and instructions for use. That supports saying that Zasso has documented compliance work for specific products and configurations.

However, customers should not interpret this as a blanket compliance claim for every Zasso product, prototype, market, attachment, tractor combination, retrofit, or future configuration. A declaration of conformity applies to the identified equipment and the approved way it is combined and operated. If a customer changes the machine, uses unapproved combinations, operates outside the manual, imports into a different regulatory market, or requests a non-standard configuration, the compliance position may need to be reviewed.

In practical terms, Zasso should answer customer questions by providing the relevant declaration, manual, safety instructions, and training package for the exact product being sold or operated. Sales teams can say that Zasso designs relevant equipment with machinery and electrical safety compliance in mind, but the formal answer for a specific machine should always come from its current product documentation.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should expect Zasso equipment to be treated like professional high-voltage agricultural or industrial machinery. Before operation, the buyer or operator should confirm that the delivered product has the correct declaration or local compliance documentation, read the operating instructions, complete training, follow the approved tractor and machine combinations, maintain safety distances, use required PPE, and stop operation if alarms, faults, damaged insulation, unsafe water or metal-contact conditions, or unapproved modifications appear.

For distributors, contractors, municipalities, and farm operators, the key point is document control. Compliance should be checked per machine and per market, not only at brand level. If the equipment is sold, leased, imported, modified, or combined with other machinery, the responsible party should confirm which conformity documents, local approvals, operator training, inspections, and maintenance records are required.

This approach protects both the customer and Zasso: it allows a clear commercial answer without overpromising that all legal and technical requirements are identical in every country or every operating scenario.

## Caveats

Do not say that every Zasso product is certified, compliant, approved, CE-marked, or legally accepted in every country, crop, machine combination, retrofit, or operating condition. Do not state that compliance removes high-voltage risk or makes the equipment safe for untrained users.

Do not disclose confidential certification files, detailed test results, engineering thresholds, safety circuit architecture, validation reports, supplier information, customer-specific legal positions, or certification timelines unless the language is already approved for external use.

Compliance can depend on the specific product version, serial configuration, tractor or carrier combination, attachment, software and hardware version, country, import route, intended use, operator training, maintenance state, modifications, local standards, and whether the equipment is a commercial product, demonstrator, prototype, or customized system.
