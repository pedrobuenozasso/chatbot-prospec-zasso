# What happens in an emergency?

## Short Answer

In an emergency, the operator should immediately stop the high-voltage output using the approved emergency stop or shutdown control, keep people and animals away from the machine and treated area, and follow the product-specific emergency procedure in the Zasso manual and training. If someone may have received an electric shock, do not touch the person until the electrical circuit has been disconnected and the area is safe; call emergency services and provide first aid only when it is safe to do so. The machine should not be restarted after an emergency, alarm, shock incident, fire, damaged cable, metal contact, or abnormal behavior until the cause has been checked and the approved procedure allows return to service.

## Detailed Answer

Zasso equipment uses controlled high-voltage electrical energy, so emergency response must protect the responder first. The first practical action is to stop high-voltage treatment through the approved emergency stop, release control, or shutdown sequence for the specific product. The operator should keep the exclusion zone controlled, prevent bystanders, animals, colleagues, vehicles, and tools from entering the area, and avoid touching electrodes, cables, conductive vegetation, metal objects, water, fences, irrigation parts, or machine structures that may be energized.

If a person or animal enters the danger zone, the conservative response is to switch off high voltage immediately and keep the machine stopped until the area is controlled again. If a person may have received an electrical shock, responders should not become part of the electrical path. They should disconnect or stop the source if they can do so safely, call local emergency services, alert nearby trained personnel, and provide first aid according to local training only after the area is safe. If the person is unconscious, burned, breathing abnormally, or not breathing normally, emergency medical help is required.

If the emergency involves fire, smoke, arcing, unusual sparking, dry biomass ignition, damaged insulation, a broken electrode, contact with metal infrastructure, wet contamination, puddles, rain, damaged cables, abnormal alarms, or uncertain machine behavior, the operator should stop operation, keep the area clear, and follow the fire, shutdown, isolation, inspection, and escalation steps in the manual and site safety plan. Fire response should prioritize self-protection, use only suitable extinguishing methods, and involve emergency services when there is any risk of spread or electric exposure.

After any emergency, the machine should be treated as out of service until the event is understood. Restart should not be based only on the absence of visible sparks or the fact that the machine appears quiet. The appropriate return-to-service decision may require shutdown, discharge, verification, inspection, documentation, replacement of damaged parts, authorized service, or Zasso support depending on the product and incident.

## What This Means for Customers

Customers should make emergency behavior part of operator training before the machine is used. Everyone involved should know how to stop high-voltage output, who controls the safety zone, how to call emergency services, where fire extinguishers or first-aid resources are located, and when the machine must remain stopped for inspection.

For supervisors, the practical rule is simple: plan the emergency response before the first application. Operators should not improvise around high voltage, metal objects, water, fire, alarms, damaged insulation, or injured people. If there is uncertainty, stop the machine, keep people away, call the appropriate support, and restart only when the approved procedure allows it.

For demonstrations, urban work, contracted services, and farms with other workers nearby, emergency planning should also include bystander control. The safest emergency is the one where the operator can stop high voltage immediately and nobody else enters the hazard area.

## Caveats

This FAQ is not a substitute for the product manual, formal operator training, first-aid training, employer emergency plan, local regulation, or emergency-services instructions. Exact emergency-stop locations, shutdown sequences, discharge checks, PPE, fire response steps, alarm handling, and restart rules may vary by product, configuration, market, and site.

Do not claim that pressing emergency stop automatically makes the machine safe to touch. Do not advise anyone to enter the electrical path, touch a shocked person, touch electrodes, move cables, handle metal objects, approach puddles, or fight a fire near potentially energized equipment unless the area has been made safe and the person is trained and equipped to do so.

Do not publish product-specific response times, fault thresholds, discharge times, emergency-stop circuit details, sensor logic, service procedures, or first-aid instructions beyond approved public material. Emergency medical and fire instructions must follow local law and trained responders.
